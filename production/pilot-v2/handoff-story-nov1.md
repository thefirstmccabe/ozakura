# Handoff to Story and Planning: reader targets loosened, and other author decisions (Nov 1, evening)

From the author's talk with the Production thread about the Ch3 cold read (`reviews/ch3/cold-read-round1.md`). Decided by the author unless marked otherwise. Production changed no canon, outline or process doc; these are for Story and Planning to fold in.

## 1. Reader targets: one principle and four tripwires (replaces the caps)
**Principle (author):** take chances. A chapter, or a whole arc, can belong to one girl, and hints can be big and one-sided. Readers are judged at the end of each arc (about 4–8 chapters, as the chapter map draws them), never scene by scene or chapter by chapter. "If it's not working, we can always revise it."

**This replaces:** windows of about 3–4 chapters; the pilot's 60% cap on the dream woman; "no reader sure (70%+)" with its Oct 25 and Nov 1 loosenings; the numeric bands as limits (they stay as information and direction of travel, not ceilings).

**Writing rules (author):**
1. Never give both girls an uncanny moment in the same chapter. They get different kinds, on their own schedules: Natsuki's through the body and the past life (the tear, knowing his moves), Nanoha's through the shrine's sight.
2. Don't answer one girl's hint with the other's in the next chapter. Let it sit.
3. Motifs are rare. Keep the wrist (the dream about her; Ch19's bandaging echoes the wrist-binding). Drop the decorative ones from here on: the knots, the apron tied like a hakama, the whiteboard's "(right one!)", the shaking left hand.

**Tripwires, checked at the end of each arc; the only things that trigger a fix** (Production's wording, approved by the author):
- Most readers treat the ending as settled.
- Something confirms the dream woman before the bloom.
- Readers stop rooting for Natsuki for a long stretch (well under 40% across two arcs).
- Readers say they can see the balancing.

Everything else (who leads, by how much, the swings) is information.

**Why:** all three Ch3 readers felt the pendulum (every clue for one girl answered by one for the other in the next part). One stopped updating on single scenes by part 10; another said the guesses flipped on matching motifs rather than clues they could weigh, and asked for one lopsided clue. Checking three numbers after every part pushed the writing toward scene-by-scene balance; Ch3's tree scene gives both girls an uncanny moment and a cover story in the same breath.

**For Story:** canon §5, "Reader targets" and the dream-woman drift (keep its core: never confirmed before the bloom; the bands become direction, not caps); the chapter map's windows table (windows become arcs); outlines stop adding "balance beats."
**For Planning:** style guide §9 step 3 and `production/pipeline/review/cold-reader.md`: readers give their numbers at chapter ends only, the results are judged per arc against the tripwires, and readers are asked whether they can see the balancing. **Open, for Planning with the author:** producing and reviewing in bigger chunks after the pilot (an arc outlined, produced, cold-read as one block and played as one build; the writing gates stay per chapter). The author is open to it.

## 2. Other decisions and leanings from the same talk
- **Kōhei's dad died of pancreatic cancer, months after it was found, and the story doesn't need to hide it** (author, refined later the same evening). Canon §6 has the hospital flashback but no cause. Say it plainly and early; Production suggests one sentence in Ch4. All three readers called the unnamed deaths and illness "too coy."
- **Akane (author, later the same evening):** the doctors don't think it's life-threatening and can't find anything structurally wrong, but they don't know what it is. The story doesn't guarantee she won't die, but it points the reader toward "we aren't planning to kill her": the reader should come away thinking it isn't life-threatening, while Kōhei can't calm down about it because of what happened to his dad. (The diagnosis itself stays undiagnosed in-story; canon's Open item can close to that.) Earlier notes, kept for Story: Production's notes: if it stays undiagnosed, a character should say so plainly ("they've run tests for years and nobody can name it"), with the stakes framed (it isn't dangerous; it just doesn't go away), so it reads as the characters not knowing rather than the author hiding it. POTS fits what Ch3 shows (dizzy on the station stairs, sitting until it passes, tiredness, daily pills, clinic days; chronic, not fatal, often eases in adulthood), but not canon's childhood hospital scare, since it usually starts in the teens. Once Dad's cancer is named, a vague illness for Akane will read as "she's next"; two readers already fear a terminal-illness turn.
- **The prologue can stay unexplained until late** (author: manga do this all the time). No echoes needed; the cold open is deferred anyway.
- **Kōhei's activity level: nothing decided** (author, Nov 2). No rule that he does something active each chapter, and no commitment to a more active direction until the author discusses it further. His view: a passive lead is normal in the genre's first half (Muv-Luv's Takeru in *Extra*); what matters is a goal he pursues at some point, more personality, and volition in choosing between the girls later. He's overwhelmed where we meet him. Avoid the opposite failure (an active lead whose choices are idiotic).
- **Pending the author's go (Production, Ch3):** under writing rule 1, Ch3's tree scene would lose Natsuki's camera beat (c3_0388, and c3_0392–0394 on the way down), leaving Nanoha's "…You came back" alone; Ch4's flashback depends on that line. Two voiced lines cut, nothing re-voiced, no spend. A reader might then pass 60% on Nanoha as the dream woman at the end of Ch3, which the new rules allow.

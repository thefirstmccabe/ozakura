karaage | Japanese fried chicken: bite-size pieces marinated in soy sauce, ginger and garlic, dusted in starch and deep-fried. A lunch-box staple, because it's still good cold.
fusuma | A sliding door of heavy paper on a wooden frame, used between rooms in a Japanese house. It doesn't lock, and you can hear through it.
kinpira | Kinpira gobō: burdock root and carrot cut into thin strips, stir-fried, then simmered in soy sauce, sugar and mirin. An everyday side dish that keeps for days, so it's easy to make too much of.
taiyaki | A fish-shaped griddle cake (the name means "baked sea bream"), usually filled with sweet red-bean paste and sold hot from street stalls. Whether to start with the head or the tail is an old argument in Japan.
shodan | The first dan grade in kendo, the first rank above the beginner grades, earned at a formal grading exam. Kendo has no belts; the rank comes as a certificate. Serious high-school players usually hold first or second dan.

karaage | Japanese fried chicken: bite-size pieces marinated in soy sauce, ginger and garlic, dusted in starch and deep-fried. A lunch-box staple, because it's still good cold.
fusuma | A sliding door of thick paper over a wooden frame, used between rooms in a Japanese house.
kinpira | Kinpira gobō: thin strips of burdock root and carrot, stir-fried, then cooked down in soy sauce, sugar and mirin (sweet rice wine). A classic make-ahead side dish that keeps for several days.
taiyaki | A fish-shaped griddle cake (the name means "baked sea bream"), usually filled with sweet red-bean paste and sold hot from street stalls. Whether to start with the head or the tail is an old argument in Japan.
shodan | First dan, the first rank above the beginner grades in kendo, earned at a formal grading exam (kendo has no belts; the rank comes as a certificate). Serious high-school players usually hold first or second dan.
hakama | Wide, pleated trousers worn over the practice jacket in kendo and other traditional martial arts. They're tied with long cords that wrap around the waist and knot at the front.
suburi | Practice swings with the bamboo sword, done in the air, usually dozens or hundreds at a time to warm up and drill clean form.
keikogi | The thick cotton jacket worn for kendo practice, usually dyed indigo. The dye fades with years of washing.
kirikaeshi | A basic kendo drill: a big strike to the head, then a fast run of alternating strikes to the left and right side of the head while stepping forward and back, all at full voice.
waza | Technique. In kendo, waza drills practice specific attacks and counters with a partner.
shiai-geiko | Practice matches: a real kendo match fought in practice, with someone calling the points.
shinai | The bamboo practice sword used in kendo: four slats of bamboo bound with leather and cord, about the length of a real sword.
zanshin | Literally "remaining mind": staying alert and ready after a strike. In a match, a strike without it doesn't count.
ki-ken-tai-itchi | "Spirit, sword and body as one": in kendo, a point needs the shout, the strike and the stamp of the front foot to land at the same instant.
kamae | A kendo stance, especially the basic one with the sword tip held at the opponent's throat.
bogu | Kendo armor: the helmet (men), padded gloves (kote), chest protector (dō) and a waist guard (tare).
men | The kendo helmet, with a steel grille over the face and padded flaps over the shoulders. It's also the name of the strike to the top of the head.
tenugui | A thin cotton cloth tied over the hair under the kendo helmet, to soak up sweat and keep the helmet snug.
zōkin | A cleaning rag, usually a folded and stitched towel. Japanese schools clean their own floors, and dojos are wiped down by hand.
zunda mochi | Rice cakes covered in a sweet, bright-green paste of mashed edamame; a specialty of Sendai.

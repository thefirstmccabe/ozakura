## Hints that felt heavy-handed, too obvious or too coy
**Heavy-handed / too obvious**
- "That I kept them waiting far too long." The dream spells out its own subtext. "That someone was waiting for me" was already enough.
- "I tell myself that all the way to the door." Ending on "Perfectly ordinary." would trust the reader. This line says the denial out loud.
- Wrists, knots and hands piled up: rubbing the callus before the dream, the hakama "off center," the apron tied like a hakama, 水 on her wrist, kote, the helmet cords, shaking out his left hand "every ten steps." Each one is fine alone. Together it starts to feel planted, and the shaking left hand is where it tipped over for me.
- "I feel like I've met him somewhere before. I just can't place it." It's the genre's stock line, said flat out. Nanoha's comeback rescues it.
- Nanoha's lit window is used as an emotional beat three times. "It's only trash bags" is great; the third time felt like a pattern.

**Too coy**
- **Dad and Mom.** Three chapters and nobody says what happened. One plain line would help me care more, not less. Right now I'm reading the grief as something on the shelf, not something in the story.
- **Akane's illness** has no name: clinic day, dizziness, "She took them." Since the medical drama on TV is there for a reason, I want to know how scared to be.
- **The prologue** (fog, a crossroads mirror, a modern hand on a katana) never comes up again in three chapters. That's a big promise left with nothing behind it. I'd forgotten it until I reread my notes.
- **The art in image_13.** The "small point of light under the low branch" reads as the sun on the horizon. I almost missed the supernatural beat in the CG.
- **"except the last one. didn't see that one at all."** I can tell it's planted, but not what it's for. Too subtle to do any work yet.

1. The past life decides the romance. If fate overrides the relationships these three chapters earned ("she's the reincarnation, so she wins"), I'm out.
2. Akane's illness turns into a cheap tearjerker countdown.
3. The pendulum keeps swinging with no one acting. Kōhei is passive: things get "decided, and I wasn't the one who decided it." Fine for a setup, deadly for a middle act.
4. The pacing stays this slow. Three chapters cover six days, and the prologue still hasn't been touched.
5. One girl gets turned into a villain to settle the triangle.
6. Mom stays a closed fusuma with nothing behind it.
7. Too many side characters keep arriving. Rika, Ishida, Ōno, Takatsuki, Kirishima, Chie, Genji, Emi, Mio and Kazuo all showed up in the first week. Most land, but Emi and Ōno are close to cardboard so far.

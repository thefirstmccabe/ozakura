**Kōhei & Natsuki:** Not introduced.

**What's going on / what kind of story?** Slice-of-life school romance with a grief backbone, and a possible supernatural thread (fog dream + ancient tree + shrine grandpa). Quiet, domestic, well-observed so far.

## Final answers

**When I became fairly sure (70%+) of anything:**
- Nanoha is in love with Kōhei: part 1, "Her hands stay a second longer than they strictly need to." Locked in by part 3: "Nanoha said it as lightly as she knew how. She'd had practice."
- Dad is dead (or at least gone for good): part 1, "He wrote it last spring. I've never written over it." Part 4's garbage-day line made it ~90%.
- Natsuki has some prior connection to Kōhei she can't explain: part 3, "I feel like I've met him somewhere before. I just can't place it." Firmed up in part 8: "She didn't know why she'd cried. She didn't know how she'd known where he would move, either."
- Kōhei loves Nanoha whether he knows it or not: part 9, "This is the version of her nobody else gets."
- The story is actually supernatural, not just dreamy: part 11, "Under it, for a second, the air smells like snow." Then "…You came back."
- I never got to 70% on who he ends up with (peaked at 60% Natsuki after parts 7–8) or on the dream woman (peaked at 55% Nanoha after parts 11–12). The triangle is genuinely balanced. Good for the story, though by the end of chapter 3 I'd like one solid fact about the dream.

**Hints that felt heavy-handed:**
- Natsuki writing 水 "on the inside of her wrist, small, just under her cuff." Then the dream wound is on the inside of his wrist. It's so clearly a plant that it made me suspicious of it rather than intrigued.
- "Someone who knows which drawer sticks in your kitchen." Natsuki repeating, almost word for word, a detail from Kōhei's narration in part 1. It's a nice irony, but it's too exact for her to have said by chance.
- "I've always been comfortable holding one. Since the first time I picked one up." Reads like a past-life flag being waved.
- The left side, over and over: left palm callus, left wrist wound, left foot forward, shaking out his left hand. One or two would be a clue; four or five starts to feel like a checklist.
- The parallel "decisions": Nanoha "decided that she liked Mizuno Natsuki… firmly" and Natsuki "decided that was none of her business." Lovely symmetry, a little too symmetrical.

**Hints that felt too coy:**
- The fog/katana prologue. It's the very first thing you see and nothing touches it for three chapters. I'd forgotten it until I went back to my notes.
- Image 13's "point of light" is invisible as an uncanny thing; it reads as the sun. If that light matters, it needs to be drawn under the branch, small and wrong.
- The shrine-step ritual ("Just stopping for a rest" / "Looks like rain tomorrow"). I liked it, but it's one more mystery stacked on the pile with no foothold. If you don't know the shrine-slope superstition, this will read as random.
- Dad and Mom: I respect the restraint, but three chapters without a single concrete fact (dead? left? how?) is long. One plain sentence would let the grief land harder.

**Names covered, could I tell them apart?**
Mostly yes.
- **Takatsuki** is the easiest (no contractions, literal, "I am telling you so that it is fair").
- **Rika** (exclamation points, stretched vowels), **Mio** (refers to herself in the third person, bossy), **Kirishima** (one-sentence commands, deadpan), and **Genji** ("Good, good." "Ha!") are all clear.
- **Ishida** and **Takatsuki** are the same pedant archetype in different outfits (thermodynamics vs. "your explanation of zanshin was incomplete").
- **Nanoha** vs. **Akane**: this is the real blur. Both use the same dry, teasing, "Then [comeback]" register. "Then go hover over the eggs!" (Akane) and "Then I've arrived." (Nanoha) are the same joke shape. "I know. That's why I didn't call." (Akane) and "I know. That's why." (Nanoha) are deliberately the same, but "Next time, don't make that face" could be either of them. Akane's "Geez!" and "Onii-chan" are the only reliable markers.
- **Nanoha** vs. **Natsuki** are mostly distinct (Nanoha is precise and formal-funny; Natsuki is quick, slangy, questions and dares). **Would I keep reading?**
Yes. Fairly eagerly, actually.
- **What hooked me:** the domestic texture (whiteboard in Dad's handwriting, garbage on the wrong day, two lunch boxes, Akane's two photos). The window scene. Natsuki's interlude ("It's the second semesters I never get to"; "The other ten she kept."). And the fact that both girls like each other and keep choosing to be decent. That's rarer than the triangle itself. Nanoha's "…You came back." is a good scene-ending sting.
- **What would make me stop:**
  - If the past-life plot turns out to decide the romance ("she's your destined one, so that settles it"). The best thing here is two real choices, and fate would cheapen them.
  - If Nanoha becomes a pure sacrificial lamb who exists to step aside nobly.
  - If the mysteries keep stacking without any payoff. After three chapters I have a prologue, a dream, a tear, a snow smell, a light, a ritual and a parent behind a door, and zero answers.
  - Pacing: three chapters cover six days. I'm fine with slice of life, but the kendo explainer and the second restaurant shift would be where I'd start skimming.

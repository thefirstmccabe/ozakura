# Final answers

**1. When I became fairly sure (70%+) of anything**
- **That Nanoha is in love with Kōhei:** 70%+ in part 1 at "Her hands stay a second longer than they strictly need to," and 90%+ by part 2's "She went looking for mine before her own."
- **That this is a reincarnation / past-life story:** 70%+ in part 4 at "There's a dream I've had since I was small," together with the kimono and katana in image 07 and "You're late." / "She says it like she's been waiting a very long time."
- **That Natsuki has a supernatural connection to him (not just a crush):** 70% at the tear in part 7 ("like she doesn't know where it came from either"), and 85% at part 8's "She hadn't read him. She'd just known."
- **On the questions the checkpoints asked (endgame, favorite, dream woman):** I never got past 60% on any of them. Endgame went 60 Nanoha → 50 → 55 Natsuki → 50/50 → 55 → 60 → 55 Natsuki. Dream woman went Nanoha 55 → 50 → Natsuki 55 → 60. The swing came from part 6 (the wrist echo) and part 7 (the tear). If the author wants the endgame uncertain through Chapter 2, it is. If the author wants me reasonably sure of the dream woman by now, it isn't there yet. The Nanoha signals in the dream (river, cherry tree, hands that tend him, a teasing scold, "waiting a very long time") pull against the Natsuki signals (wrist, recognition, the tear) almost exactly evenly.

**2. Hints that felt heavy-handed, too obvious, or too coy**
- **Heavy:** Natsuki's "Someone who knows which drawer sticks in your kitchen," right after part 1 showed Nanoha knowing exactly that. Too neat. It's the author's hand, not Natsuki's.
- **Heavy:** the mirrored interlude endings. Nanoha "decided she liked Mizuno Natsuki… why it had needed deciding," then Natsuki "liked her. That was its own small problem." The second one rhymes so exactly that I saw the structure instead of the feeling.
- **A bit heavy:** "That I kept them waiting far too long." It's acceptable as a chapter sting, but it states the theme outright.
- **Right on:** the kote hitting the wrist "in training," echoing the dream's "where a blade got past me in training." I caught it without being told. Nobody points at it, and that's the right amount.
- **Too coy:** the prologue. There's no text: a fog road, a mirror, a modern-looking dark zip jacket with a real katana, fog rolling like a wave. It never comes back in two chapters. I can't tell whether the jacket is meant to clash with the kimono-era dream, whether this is Dad, the future, or the dream itself. One line of narration or a caption would help, or a callback in Chapter 2.
- **Too coy:** Kōhei's own side of the recognition. He clenches his pen hard enough to hurt when Natsuki sees him, then never thinks about it again. Natsuki gets "I feel like I've met him somewhere before"; he gets nothing equivalent. He's a narrator who explains everything (the whiteboard, the shifts, the apron knot), so his silence on that, and on the half-second stop in the match, reads as either a deliberate withhold or a gap. If it's deliberate, give one beat of him noticing that he isn't thinking about it.
- **Possibly unintended:** Natsuki's hair. The art shows bright auburn hair and green eyes. The text only says "long, in soft waves," and the class whispers "She's so pretty" / "Is she an idol," with nothing about the color. In a Japanese classroom that would be the first remark. Either the color means something and should be acknowledged, or the art should match the text.

**3. Which girl I like more as a character**
Natsuki, by a little. She gets the active scenes (the kids' plate, the lost-and-found glasses lecture, "spiritual first-year," racing on the wet floor). She has a real inner contradiction: the confident first-day act sits over someone who never gets to keep anything, and her interlude shows it without over-explaining ("It's the second semesters I never get to"; the crossed-out SAPPORO and SENDAI; ten photos she keeps). Nanoha has the best single line in the read ("I have met him before. Every day. For sixteen years.") and the most romantic scene (the trash night). But she's off screen for all of Chapter 2, and she fades. If the triangle is supposed to feel balanced going into the shrine Saturday, Nanoha needs one beat in Chapter 2. Even a short exchange through the facing windows would do.

**5. With names covered, could I tell them apart?**
Mostly yes.
- **Rika** is unmistakable (caps, "Oh oh oh," triple exclamations).
- **Takatsuki** is formal with no contractions ("It is the hardest thing," "I have faced Fujisawa for a year").
- **Kirishima** speaks in short commands.
- **Chie** is lowercase texting.
- **Nanoha vs. Natsuki** are clearly different. Nanoha is dry, precise and mock-formal ("Now then." "I've prepared a theory." "It's a sickness."), with few exclamation marks and shy politeness with outsiders ("Um—"). Natsuki is casual, imperative and exclamatory ("Hand me a rag." "Race you!" "Okay, okay."), with "Hey." openers and sports talk. They overlap in one-word deflections (Nanoha's "Secret." vs. Natsuki's "Depends."), and Natsuki in teacher mode ("So be afraid.") briefly borrows Nanoha's deadpan.
- **Kōhei and Nanoha** share the same deadpan rhythm ("You're already in." / "Then I've arrived."). That fits people who grew up together, but in their quick exchanges I sometimes had to check who was speaking.
- **Akane and Rika** both run on exclamation energy. "Onii-chan" is the only thing separating Akane.
- **Tokiwa and Takatsuki** are both pedants with T-surnames, which could blur over a longer read. Tokiwa is pompous-casual and Takatsuki is stiff-formal, so it's OK for now.

**7. The kendo match**
I followed it fine. Point 1: he feints men, she doesn't bite, he commits, hesitates half a second, and she takes debana-kote as his arms rise. Point 2: she goes for men, he ducks under and cuts dō as they pass, on the wrong foot.

The explanation scene helped. It set up exactly the terms the match uses (kote, dō, debana as "my favorite… the hardest," "___ ari"), and Takatsuki naming "debana-kote" afterward closes the loop cleanly. The stance demo is the best part because it's character work (the "before" picture, "visual aid," Takatsuki "like a door swinging shut").

It drags in two places. Rika's questions run a beat or two past the point where I'd already got it, and tsuki and ki-ken-tai are taught but never used. Zanshin is taught, flagged as "incomplete," and left hanging. I assume that's deliberate, and I'm fine with it.

The one moment I had to reread was panel 95 ("Kōhei commits") followed by "I stop. Half a second. Then I go." The order of commit, stop and go was unclear for a second.

Natsuki later texts "i knew what he was going to do… every time," but he scored the second point on her. That's either a small contradiction or, better, a sign the wrong-foot dō was the one thing she *couldn't* read. If it's the second, make it a touch more legible.

**8. Would I keep reading?**
Yes. What hooked me:
- the dream, plus Natsuki's tear and "She'd just known"
- the quiet grief at home (Dad's handwriting on the garbage line, "tonight I went with the handwriting," Mom behind the fusuma). I want to know what happened, and I like that nobody has explained it.
- the Saturday collision: Natsuki has just invited herself into Nanoha's shrine afternoon, and Kōhei hasn't mentioned Nanoha to her. I want to see that scene.
- the ensemble: Kirishima, Takatsuki and the Lifesaver manager are all good company.

- The past life gets explained by a grandpa-at-the-shrine lore dump.

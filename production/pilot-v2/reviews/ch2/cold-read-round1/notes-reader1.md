**Hints that felt heavy-handed, too obvious, or too coy:**
- *Slightly heavy:* "I feel like I've met him somewhere before. I just can't place it." It's said plainly to Nanoha on day one. It's standard for the genre, but it's the loudest hint and it's dialogue, not behavior. Her stutter in the classroom already told me that.

- *Slightly heavy:* Kōhei's "I've always been comfortable holding one. Since the first time I picked one up." It sounds like the writer planting something, not like a teenage boy talking.

She gets more range: the performer in front of the class, the kids'-plate goofball, the fighter who's happiest when she's not given a point, and then the girl alone in a dark apartment with crossed-out moving boxes, trying out her funny line and not laughing at it. That interlude made her a person. Nanoha has the best *lines* in the whole thing ("I have met him before. Every day. For sixteen years."), and her Ch1 interlude is lovely, but she vanishes for all of Chapter 2. A character I don't see for three parts can't keep up with one who gets the restaurant, the dojo, the catch, the bridge and an interlude. If the author wants this to be a real contest, Nanoha needs a Chapter-2-sized showcase soon.

**With names covered, could I tell them apart?**
Mostly yes.
- Takatsuki is the easiest: formal, no contractions, corrects people ("It has been off center for a year." "Luck is not a technique.").
- Kirishima is terse orders and deadpan.
- Rika is exclamation points and run-ons.
- Ryōsuke argues about food like a professor.
- Akane is "Geez, Onii-chan!" and "Yaaay."
- Kōhei is short, dry, straight-man lines.

The two girls are the closest. Both tease Kōhei deadpan, and they share a joke shape: a reframe that starts with "Then…". Nanoha's "You're already in." / "Then I've arrived." and Natsuki's "First-years are supposed to do the grunt work." / "Then I'm a spiritual first-year." are the same joke. Both also open lines with "So." Without the name tags I'd tell them apart mainly by address ("Kō-chan"/"Kōhei" is Nanoha, "Fujisawa" is Natsuki), and by register: Nanoha builds elaborate deadpan theories ("I've prepared a theory," the folder, the face catalogue), while Natsuki is quicker, more physical, more "Hey." and exclamation marks. Nanoha with strangers ("Um—", "I'm working up to it") is clearly different. Nanoha bantering with Kōhei and Natsuki bantering with Kōhei is where they blur.
Name note: Mizuno/Natsuki, Tokiwa/Ryōsuke and Hoshikawa/Rika each switch between surname (narration) and given name (speaker tag), and each made me double-take once, especially "Hoshikawa" showing up at the dojo next to "[Rika appears]". Hoshino Kitchen and Hoshikawa are also very close.

**Could I follow the kendo match? Did the explanation scene help or drag?**
Yes, I followed it, and the explanation scene definitely helped. Because I'd just learned men/kote/dō, "ari," and especially *debana* ("You hit them right as they start to attack… Mostly you don't [know]. That's why it's hard."), I understood the first point when it happened, and Takatsuki naming it "debana-kote" afterward felt like a payoff, not a lecture. Rika being the stand-in idiot ("So the yelling is you telling them where") kept it light. The "visual aid" bit with Kōhei as the "before picture" is the funniest stretch of Chapter 2.
It drags slightly in the middle: the four-targets list, the tsuki line (never used), and the zanshin explanation plus Takatsuki's "incomplete," which never gets resolved. I did notice that Natsuki laughs behind her men right after scoring, minutes after teaching "No celebrating." If that's the "incomplete" joke, it's too quiet for me to be sure.
One ordering snag in the manga: the panel shows Kōhei committing to the strike, and only *after* it comes "I stop. Half a second. Then I go." For a second I thought he stopped mid-strike. The captain's "You stopped for half a second" sorted it out. Putting the "I stop" beat before the commit panel would fix it. Also "left foot forward (the wrong foot)": I only knew it was wrong because of the parenthetical, which is fine.

**Would I keep reading?**
Yes. Definitely through the next chapter.
What hooked me:
- The Saturday setup. Kōhei promised Nanoha he'd help at her grandpa's shrine, and now Natsuki is coming to "photograph that thing," and he hasn't told either of them about the other. "It's only trash bags." I want to see that scene.
- The tear, and who the dream woman is.
- Both girls being genuinely nice to each other, which makes the triangle hurt instead of feeling catty.
- The quiet grief stuff with Dad's handwriting.

What would make me stop:
- If Nanoha keeps disappearing and becomes the "sad loser" by default instead of a real contender.
- If Kōhei stays dense for too many chapters. He's observant about everything except himself, which is fine for two chapters and annoying by five.
- If the prologue and Akane's illness stay unexplained for too long with no breadcrumbs.
- If every kendo scene comes with a glossary.
- If the past-life explanation eventually arrives as one big info dump.

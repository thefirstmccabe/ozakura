If it's intended (someone in the present with a real sword), good, it's my biggest hook. If not, it's a mismatch that will send readers down a wrong path.
- *Coy but fine:* Dad, Mom, Akane's illness. Mom and Akane vanish completely in Chapter 2, so the family thread loses heat.
- *Dangling:* Takatsuki's "your explanation of zanshin was incomplete" is cut off by Kirishima and never resolved. If it's a plant, fine. If not, it reads as a dropped thread.

**Which girl I like more as a character:** Natsuki, now. She gets the richest interior life (the boxes, the "second semesters" line, the photos as "proof I was there," Chie as the one person she's kept), and her comedy is *active*: she drives the scenes (kids' plate, the teaching bit, the race). Nanoha is lovable and her lines are sharper word for word ("Every day. For sixteen years."), but her best traits are noticing, serving and deflecting. She's defined by kinpira, collars and knowing which drawer sticks. Then Chapter 2 sidelines her completely. If Nanoha doesn't get a scene where she *wants* something out loud, or acts rather than notices, she risks reading as the designated runner-up, and I'd stop rooting for her out of pity rather than choice.

**Up to 8 flat lines, and why:**
1. Natsuki: "…six times now? So please don't go easy on me." The "so" doesn't follow from moving six times; it only makes sense as a kendo line, and that comes next. It reads like a non sequitur.
2. Natsuki: "Takes one to know one." A cliché, and the idiom is usually a comeback to an insult, so it sounds slightly off-register here.
3. Kōhei: "We'll find out on the floor." A stock rival line. Kōhei's voice is better than this elsewhere.
4. Kōhei: "That's what the attendance sheet says." Generic deadpan reply to "So. You're Fujisawa."
5. Takatsuki: "Luck is not a technique." A stock stoic-rival aphorism. His "I have never once been there first" right before it is much better.
6. Natsuki: "I just mean it was a good match. I haven't had one like that in a long time." She states her own subtext, and then the narration states it again ("like a cup filled too full," which is a good image). One of them is enough.
7. Akane: "Geez, Onii-chan! Every single morning!" / "Yaaay." Default imouto voice. Akane is the only main character without a distinct idiom ("Then go hover over the eggs!" is the exception).
8. Rika: "It's destiny! It's in the stars! I'm making us matching keychains!" Generic genki. Her later "There was a head" shows she can be funnier than her template.
- Not flat, but a small factual snag: "…It's April" as the rebuttal to cedar pollen. In Kanagawa, cedar season runs into April and cypress peaks then, so Natsuki's "Late bloomers. They're real" is basically correct. The Chie callback makes it a running gag that lands, but a Japanese reader may side with Natsuki.

**With the names covered, could I tell them apart?**
- *Takatsuki:* yes, instantly (no contractions: "It has been off center for a year," "I have faced Fujisawa").
- *Kirishima:* yes (clipped imperatives, one-word verdicts).
- *Rika:* yes (exclamation stacks, repetition).
- *Tokiwa:* mostly; he's a pedant with contractions. He could blur with Takatsuki, since both are pedantic and both are T-surnames.
- *Nanoha vs. Natsuki:* mostly yes, but not always. Nanoha is dry, slightly formal and absurdist ("Now then," "I'm coming in," "It's a sickness," "I've catalogued them"). Natsuki is casual and punchy, with direct questions and "Hey," "Okay, okay," "Eh, 'san'?". The overlap is teasing that scores off him. "Now you look like someone who has his life together" (Nanoha) and "Big talk for someone who sits like he's being graded" (Natsuki) could be swapped. The dream woman's lines ("You're late," "Hold still") are deliberately in the overlap, and that works.
- *Kōhei:* short and deflecting with Nanoha; looser and more talkative with Natsuki. That difference is good characterization.
- *Name confusion:* Rika is introduced only as "Rika" in Ch1, then appears in Ch2 as "Hoshikawa's head comes around it" with no bridge. I only placed her from the sprite tag. Hoshikawa vs. Hoshino Kitchen is also close. Ryōsuke (tag) vs. Tokiwa (narration) is bridged once, which is enough. Natsuki/Nanoha both starting with "Na" is a lot to ask of a cold reader, but the art separates them.

**Mismatches between text and illustrations:**
- **Cold open (image 2):** the katana-holder wears a modern dark zip-up jacket. The dream (image 7) shows a plain period sleeve with the same hilt. If the opening is meant to be the dream-era swordsman, the zip is a real mismatch. If it's meant to be a present-day person, it's fine, but then it's the biggest unexplained thing in the story.
- **Image 4:** the text says "Nanoha walks on the river side," but in the collar CG Kōhei has his back to the river and Nanoha is on the far side. She has stepped in front of him, so it's defensible, but it's flipped. Otherwise it matches well: petal on the braid, the height gap, hands at the collar.
- **Images 5/6:** match the text ("long, in soft waves"). But she has copper-auburn hair and green eyes, which the text never acknowledges. A reader may assume she's mixed-heritage or dyes her hair and expect it to matter. In the text she bows; in the art she's waving. That's trivial.
- **Image 7:** matches ("a cut on the inside of it," cloth being wound, petals, the branch). The swordsman's hand reads slightly off anatomically (the thumb placement is ambiguous), worth a second look.
- **Catch CG (undrawn):** the description matches the text exactly.

**Could I follow the kendo match, and did the explanation help or drag?** Yes, completely. Bout 1: he feints men, she doesn't bite, he hesitates half a second and commits, and she takes debana-kote as his arms rise. Bout 2: she attacks men, he slips under with nuki-dō on the "wrong" foot. 1–1, time. The explanation scene **helped**. Without "Debana. You hit them right as they start to attack" and "'Men ari.' There was a head," neither "She was there before I moved" nor Takatsuki's "That was debana-kote" would land, and the referee calls would be noise. It also does character work (Natsuki teaching, Kōhei as "visual aid," Takatsuki's pride). It drags slightly in the back half. The stance comparison, the zanshin interjection and the whiteboard button come one after another, and that's about three closing jokes in a row. Trim one. One gap: footwork isn't explained, so "(the wrong foot)" in the panel note means nothing until Takatsuki says it afterward. That's acceptable, since his line is the payoff.

**Would I keep reading?** Yes, definitely through Chapter 3.
- *Hooks:* Natsuki's interlude (the boxes, "second semesters," the eleven pictures). The involuntary tear and "She'd just known." A triangle where both girls genuinely like each other and neither is a villain, which is rarer and more painful than the usual setup. The Saturday collision (Nanoha's shrine afternoon, which Kōhei just handed to Natsuki without noticing). The grief detail of Dad's handwriting and the off-center hakama.
- *What would make me stop:* (1) Nanoha staying reactive and offscreen until she's just the sacrificial childhood friend. (2) The supernatural being explained by a lore dump from Grandpa at the shrine instead of discovered. (3) The cold-open fog/katana never paying off. (4) Kōhei staying passive ("something's been decided, and I wasn't the one who decided it" is charming once; as a pattern it would grate). (5) Mom and Akane becoming set dressing. Two chapters is a lot of setup with only a tear as the present-day uncanny event, so Chapter 3 needs to escalate at the Ōzakura.

## Checkpoint 1 (after part 1)

**Who does Kōhei end up with?** Nanoha, 55%. She's the only romantic candidate on screen so far, and childhood-friend-next-door is the default route in a lot of these. But I've read enough of them to know the childhood friend who walks in without knocking and fixes your collar in the first ten minutes is just as often the one who *loses*. So: I'm guessing Nanoha mainly because nobody else has shown up yet. Natsuki is listed as a name I'm asked about but hasn't appeared, so there's clearly a second heroine coming.

**Who do I want him to end up with?** Nanoha, moderately (6/10). She's charming, dry, and clearly been carrying this family quietly for a year. But I haven't met the alternative.

**Woman in the dream?** Not yet. There's an opening sequence (fog, a road mirror, a hand on a sheathed sword, a fog bank rolling down a road) but no woman in it and nothing says it's a dream. If it is, it's probably Kōhei's. He does kendo, and the hand in the dark jacket fits.

**Kōhei and Nanoha:** Childhood friends and neighbours, four years in the same class, extremely comfortable. She knows his kitchen better than he does (mugs cupboard, the sticking drawer, the window latch). Running joke banter, she "keeps score." She's been feeding the family for about a year under a polite fiction ("Mom made too much again"). Classic.

**Kōhei and Natsuki:** Not introduced yet. No idea.

**Strange / unexplained:**

**What does Natsuki want?** Not yet introduced.

**What kind of story?** A slice-of-life school romance with a grief/caregiver backbone (Clannad-ish family weight), plus a supernatural or mystery thread tied to fog, a sword and the big shrine cherry. - **Ending (who he ends up with):** never reached 70%. My peak was 65% Natsuki at part 7, on the catch-and-tear. The parallel interludes pulled me back down. The triangle feels genuinely open, which I think is a success.
- **Dream woman:** never reached 70%. My peak was 60% Natsuki at the end. The clues are deliberately split, and I flipped twice (Natsuki, then Nanoha at part 6, then Natsuki).
- **Nanoha has feelings for Kōhei:** about 70% at "Her hands stay a second longer than they strictly need to." (part 1), and over 90% at "She did not let herself think about why it had needed deciding at all." (part 3).

### Hints that felt heavy-handed, too obvious or too coy
**Heavy-handed / too neat:**
- The drawer callback: Natsuki saying "Someone who knows which drawer sticks in your kitchen" to Nanoha, of all people, the day after we saw Nanoha bump that drawer. It works, but I could see the author's hand placing it.
- The wrist pile-up: 水 on the inside of her wrist, the dream cut on the inside of his wrist, the kote lesson ("Kote. The wrist."), the kote point, his hand around her forearm in the catch. Each one alone is elegant; five in two chapters starts to feel like a highlighter.
- Kōhei's "I've always been comfortable holding one. Since the first time I picked one up." That's the author telling me "past-life swordsman" in his voice. The narration that follows ("everything else goes quiet") is better and would carry it alone.
- Natsuki's "i knew what he was going to do before he did it. every time" plus "It didn't feel like it covered it." Fine on its own, but on top of the tear and "met him somewhere before" it's the third time the text has told me she has an inexplicable sense of him.
- The wet-floor slip-and-catch. A very old trope. The tear saves it.

**Too coy:**
- Kōhei gripping his pen "hard enough to hurt" when Natsuki's smile stutters (part 2). It's never explained or revisited. Did *he* recognise *her*? His narration explains everything else, so the silence reads as withholding rather than mystery. If it's intended, one more beat later would help. If it's just nerves, it's misleading.
- The prologue (fog, mirror, sword in a modern jacket) gets zero follow-up in two chapters. Fine for a cold open, but by the end of chapter 2 I've half-forgotten it.
- Mom, Akane's illness and Dad all vanish in chapter 2 (Akane only appears via text photos). The opening made them the emotional base; chapter 2 drops them. Probably intentional (Natsuki as escape), but nothing in the text marks the absence.

**Well-calibrated:** the face catalogue ("number one" kept secret), "It's only trash bags.", Nanoha going quiet "a little longer than that, which is", and the hakama "off center for a year". These trust the reader.

### Flat lines (8)
1. **Natsuki, self-intro:** "I also love horror movies, the cheesier the better, so if anyone wants to watch a bad one with me, come find me." Reads like a character-profile bullet. And the horror trait only gets one follow-up (the haunted music room), so it feels like a planted setup rather than a person talking.
2. **Natsuki:** "Looking forward to this year." It's the recovery after the stutter, but the line itself is nothing. A small, slightly-off recovery line would sell the stutter better.
3. **Kōhei:** "I've always been comfortable holding one. Since the first time I picked one up." Plot signal in teen dialogue (see above). No sixteen-year-old says this to someone he met at 8:30 a.m.
4. **Ōno-sensei:** "All right. Settle down. Before we start—as some of you have apparently already heard, through channels I'd like to know more about—we have a new student joining us this year." Stock homeroom-teacher; the joke in the middle is fine, but the "born in a cardigan" narration is funnier than anything he says.
5. **Natsuki:** "Serious question. Is this building haunted? Something in the music room was knocking." / "Perfect." Exists to remind me she likes horror. It felt like a seed being watered.
6. **Natsuki:** "Hey. Does Fujisawa ever compete up north? Around Sendai?" Expository and convenient: it sets up "met him before" a line early and makes her sound like a detective rather than a girl idly curious.
7. **Natsuki:** "I just mean it was a good match. I haven't had one like that in a long time." The narration ("like a cup filled too full") does all the work; the line is placeholder-generic.
8. **Natsuki, the zanshin lesson:** "You're still in the fight until somebody tells you you're not." It reads as the theme being stated aloud. Partly redeemed by Takatsuki's "incomplete" payoff, which is good.

### With names covered, could I tell them apart?
Mostly yes, with two overlaps.
- **Rika:** instantly (exclamation storms, "Nacchan!", "Is this a floor you're allowed to sit on?").
- **Akane:** instantly ("Geez, Onii-chan!"). Very genre-standard little sister, though; she's the least individual voice.
- **Nanoha:** dry, deadpan, polite-form absurdism, very short sentences, "Mm." Distinct.
- **Natsuki:** questions, imperatives, teasing, performer energy ("So be afraid."). Mostly distinct, *but* her deadpan one-liners ("He said they don't check." / "Late bloomers. They're real. Look it up." / "Then I'm a spiritual first-year.") are in the same register as Nanoha's ("It's a sickness." / "Then I've arrived."). In a short dry exchange I could mistake one heroine for the other. Both "win the exchange with a calm reframe." Giving them more different joke shapes would help: maybe Nanoha understates and Natsuki overstates.
- **Kōhei:** a defensive straight man ("That's what the attendance sheet says." / "I'm standing in my own kitchen."). He also deadpans like Nanoha, which suits lifelong friends but adds to the blur.
- **Tokiwa vs. Takatsuki:** the other overlap. Both are pedants who correct people ("That isn't an opinion. It's thermodynamics." vs. "Luck is not a technique."), and both surnames start with T. Takatsuki's no-contractions formality ("I have never once been there first") separates them on close reading, but on first read I briefly wondered whether they were the same running gag.
- **Kirishima:** distinct by brevity and authority.
- **Chie:** distinct by medium (lowercase, caps for emphasis).

### Would I keep reading?
Yes, definitely through chapter 3.

**What hooked me:**
- The Saturday collision. Kōhei has walked Natsuki into Nanoha's afternoon and told himself "It's only trash bags." I need to see that scene.
- The tear, the "every time," and the dream. The mystery is pitched right: specific enough to theorise about, not yet explained.
- Both heroines are decent, lonely, and like each other. That's the hardest and best kind of triangle, and the two mirrored interludes are the strongest structural choice in these chapters.
- The supporting cast is alive on very few lines (Takatsuki, Kirishima, the manager, Chie).
- The prose is clean and often funny; the dream paragraph is genuinely good.

**What would make me stop:**
- If Nanoha becomes a sacrificial stepping stone: the childhood friend who exists to lose gracefully. The text is currently flirting with that (Chapter 2 gives her one morning scene and a lit window).
- If "destiny" decides the romance with no cost or choice, i.e. the dream simply overrides sixteen years of Nanoha.
- If the family material (Mom, Akane, Dad) turns out to be set dressing. Chapter 1 made it the emotional floor; chapter 2 forgot it.
- If the triangle stalls into repeated near-misses and catches.
- Translator's-note overload. Part 6 has fifteen notes (I undercounted at checkpoint 6), plus parenthetical glosses repeated every time ("hakama (pleated kendo trousers)" four times across parts 5–6; "shinai (bamboo practice sword)" and "men (helmet)" repeatedly). During the match it actively slowed the action. Once per term is enough; the notes plus a first-use inline gloss is already double. (Correction to checkpoint 6: the "Me-e-en!" note does mention the strike, but only second, after the helmet. Leading with the strike would fit the shout.)

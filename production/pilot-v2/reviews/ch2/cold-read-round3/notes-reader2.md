Her text "i knew what he was going to do before he did it. every time" plus the tear she can't explain ("It didn't feel like it covered it") push toward a past-life bond with her. The dream's "waiting at home to bandage him" staging still points to a Nanoha-type role, so I won't go higher.

**Kōhei and Nanoha:** They're in a holding pattern that's about to break. He comes home, sees her light, and tells himself "It's only trash bags," which is guilt. He knows the shrine Saturday was *hers* and he's just let Natsuki aim at it ("That's my project, then"). He hasn't been unfaithful to anything, but he knows he's bringing someone into the one afternoon Nanoha set up for the two of them.

**Kōhei and Natsuki:** They're falling for each other and neither is saying so. She offers extra practice and then retracts it ("Forget it, it was a random thought"). He counters with Saturday morning and ends up surrendering his free afternoon. She decides to make him having fun her "project." "Something's been decided, and I wasn't the one who decided it." Her texts deny it ("not like that!!!"), and her "I cried on him" makes it obvious.

**Strange or unexplained:**
- "i knew what he was going to do before he did it. every time / except the last one." She reads him uncannily, except when he fights "wrong" (the left-foot dō). So maybe his past-life self takes over when he stops thinking, and that self is the one she *can't* read?
- The tear, still unexplained to herself.
- The Ōzakura: she's wanted to photograph it "since Monday," and it's "still holding the last of the light." Saturday at the shrine is clearly the next big set piece.
- Her home is empty. Mom writes "proud of you" every first week, and "Natsuki had never asked what for." Dad is away. The boxes are unpacked and crossed out. She's a lonely kid. It's not supernatural, but it's the most affecting thing in the chapter.
- "The other ten she kept." What's in them, and why keep them? It reads as her starting to want to stay.
- Carried over, untouched in Chapter 2: the cold-open fog and katana, Mom behind the fusuma, Akane's condition, Dad.

**What Nanoha wants:** Kōhei, and Saturday. She's also choosing friendship with Natsuki, and Natsuki now calls her "a real one," which makes this sadder. Neither girl wants to be the other's rival.

**What Natsuki wants:** To finally get a second semester, a place and people she gets to keep. Specifically, she wants Kōhei to have fun and to keep fighting her. She also wants not to want him, because of Nanoha: "Natsuki decided that was none of her business, and didn't quite believe herself."

**What kind of story:** A bittersweet reincarnation triangle where both girls are good people and friends with each other, so no villain, just bad timing and fate. I expect Saturday at the Ōzakura to be where the past starts breaking through, and where Nanoha sees the two of them together.

## Final answers

### When I became fairly sure (70%+)
- **That Dad is dead or gone (~75%), part 1:** "All of it except the bottom line, which still says 'Garbage: burnables, Mon/Thu' in Dad's. He wrote it last spring. I've never written over it." (It's still never confirmed outright. I think that's fine.)
- **That Nanoha is in love with Kōhei (80%+), part 3:** "Nanoha said it as lightly as she knew how. She'd had practice." (I was around 65% at "She went looking for mine before her own.")
- **That this is a past-life/reincarnation story (~80%), part 4:** "She says it like she's been waiting a very long time." together with image_07 (kimono, katana, cherry branch). I'd been around 55% at part 3's "I feel like I've met him somewhere before."
- **That Kōhei is falling for Natsuki (70%+), part 5:** "I've been talking. For an hour, about taiyaki and herons, to someone I met yesterday." The catch in part 7 pushed it to around 85%.
- **What Natsuki wants (belonging, a place she gets to keep) (75%), part 8:** "I'm amazing at first days. It's the second semesters I never get to."
- **Who he ends up with:** never reached 70%. The peak was Natsuki at 63% after the catch.
- **Who the dream woman is:** never reached 70%. It swung between the two girls the whole way, peaking at 55%. If that's intended, it's working. If the author thinks the answer is already clear, it isn't.

### Hints that felt heavy-handed, too obvious or too coy
**Heavy-handed / too obvious:**
- Natsuki saying "Someone who knows which drawer sticks in your kitchen" in part 3. It's the exact detail from Nanoha's kitchen routine in part 1, and I could see the author's hand placing it. A near-miss (a squeaky cupboard, a stuck window) would land the irony without feeling engineered.
- "I feel like I've met him somewhere before. I just can't place it." It's the stock reincarnation line, said straight. It works, but it's the least surprising line in the chapter. Combined with the grin stutter, the tear and the "knew what he'd do" text, the "you've met before" signal gets four separate deliveries in two chapters.
- The Natsuki interlude explains the tear and the match for us: "She didn't know why she'd cried… That should have covered it. It didn't feel like it covered it." I'd already gotten there from the scene itself. Cutting it to just the texts and "it didn't feel like it covered it" would be enough.
- The empty seat behind the protagonist by the window. It's a beloved trope and I'm not mad, but it's the most "I've seen this anime" moment.
- Kōhei's "I've always been comfortable holding one. Since the first time I picked one up." It reads like a plant, not like a teenager talking.

**Too coy:**
- Kōhei's own reaction when he first sees Natsuki ("holding my pen hard enough to hurt") is never revisited. He never wonders about it, though he's otherwise a precise, observant narrator. Either have him notice it later or I'll assume it was just attraction.
- The 水 on her wrist. If it's meant to connect to "water/Mizuno/the river," a reader who doesn't know kanji gets only "Wednesday" from the gloss. I wasn't sure whether I was overreading it.
- "The other ten she kept." I couldn't tell whether there's something specific in them (him? the shrine?) or it's just mood. Probably fine as mood.
- The cold open (fog, crossroads mirror, katana in a modern zip jacket, fog wave) is never touched again in two chapters. It's okay to hold it, but I'd forgotten it by the dojo.
- The "It's April" pollen rebuttal (twice). I wasn't sure *why* April rules out pollen; I thought spring was hay-fever season. The callback via Chie is funny, but the logic under it didn't click for me.

### Dialogue that felt lively
- Part 1's kitchen run: "You're already in." / "Then I've arrived." "You thought it in a short-person direction." "Rika-chan counts as several." "His back is a very convenient back."
- Ryōsuke's taiyaki thermodynamics and Rika's "…Oh no."
- "I have met him before. Every day. For sixteen years."
- "You have several. I've catalogued them. That one's number four. 'Somebody wants to fight me.'" / "What's number one?" / "Secret."
- The restaurant: "Do you check ID for the kids' plate?" and "…Wait. Do you work here?" / "I'm new." Best comic beat in the book.
- The dojo: "Somebody hits you in the head, and the referee goes, 'There was a head.'" / "That was a joke." (Kirishima) / "You're doing great, visual aid." / "It scored." / "Yes."
- "Yeah. You too. I mean— you're welcome." A perfect flustered line.
- Chie's texts: "oh no," "ok that's actually kind of hot," "it's april," "are you ok tho. for real."

### Flat lines (up to 8) and why
1. **Akane: "Nanoha-chan brought kinpira (braised burdock)!"** (part 1). It's a non sequitur that re-announces something that happened a few lines earlier, right after "Traitor." The beat stalls. Cut it or give her a real comeback.
2. **Kōhei: "I've always been comfortable holding one. Since the first time I picked one up."** (part 3). This is the plot talking. It's too tidy for a guy who "answers before I've decided to."
3. **Natsuki: "I also love horror movies, the cheesier the better, so if anyone wants to watch a bad one with me, come find me."** (part 2). It's a character-sheet line, and nothing has used it yet beyond the haunted-pipes joke.
4. **Kōhei: "That's the Ōzakura."** (part 8). It's the title of the book, and he gives it zero feeling. It's a label, not a response. Even one beat of what the tree means to *him* would help.
5. **Natsuki: "I just mean it was a good match. I haven't had one like that in a long time."** (part 8). It's generic. The narration ("like a cup filled too full") is doing the work the line should share.
6. **Natsuki: "Men. The head." / "Dō. The side of the body."** (part 6). The lesson starts as a fun bit but slides into a tutorial, and with a translator note on nearly every term the scene turns into a glossary for a stretch.
7. **Rika: "Oh! Oh oh oh, but have you heard? Two-B is getting a transfer student!"** (part 2). It's the stock genki-informant line. Rika is a type more than a person so far, though the frog clip with a crown helps.
8. **Ōno-sensei: "Let's see… the empty seat behind Fujisawa, by the window."** (part 2). It's functional and fine, but as dialogue it exists only to move furniture.

**A related problem, not a single line:** the inline glosses repeat every time a term recurs. "Hakama (pleated kendo trousers)" appears about four times, "shinai (bamboo practice sword)" about four times, "taiyaki (fish-shaped cake)" three times, "men (helmet)" repeatedly, and "kinpira" and "fusuma" twice each. After the first gloss plus the translator's note, I don't need the parenthetical again. It's the single most reading-fatiguing thing in Chapter 2. A few calls also get double-glossed, inline and with a note ("Kote-e-e! (Wrist!)" plus a TN).

### Could I tell the characters apart with names covered?
Mostly yes, with one overlap.
- **Easy:** Rika (exclamation marks, run-ons), Ryōsuke (pedantic, "That isn't an opinion. It's thermodynamics"), Takatsuki (no contractions, "Luck is not a technique"), Kirishima (two-to-four-word commands), Akane ("Geez, Onii-chan!"), the manager ("Lifesaver!"), and Chie (lowercase texts).
- **Harder:** Nanoha, Natsuki and Kōhei all run on the same deadpan comeback engine. Nanoha's "Then I've arrived," Natsuki's "He said they don't check" / "I'm new," and Kōhei's "I'm moving it closer to the right day" are interchangeable in rhythm. You can tell Natsuki apart by her questions and exclamations ("Eh, 'san'? We're the same age!", "No way."), her nicknaming and her physical forwardness. You can tell Nanoha apart by "Mm.", her slightly formal precision ("I'm working up to it"), and her systems and lists (scores, face numbers, the folder). In pure-deadpan mode, though, I'd sometimes guess wrong between the two girls. Takatsuki and Kirishima are both terse, but Takatsuki is formal-wordy and Kirishima is clipped, so they hold.
- Who sounds like whom: Natsuki's "Late bloomers. They're real. Look it up." could be a Nanoha line. Nanoha's "His back is a very convenient back" could be Natsuki's.

### Mismatches between text and illustrations
- **image_04 (collar):** The text says she folds his *collar* down over his tie, but the art shows her hands on his *tie knot*. The text also sets "Nanoha walks on the river side," while the CG has the river behind *him*. That's explainable because she stepped in front of him, but at a glance it reads as swapped. The petal on the braid and the height gap match.

For the most visually striking character in the cast, the narration skipping the color felt like a gap. She's also waving in both frames, which the text doesn't mention (harmless).
- **image_08 (whiteboard):** The borrowed lost-and-found glasses are sitting on the board tray, but in the text she's wearing them for the "teacher" bit. Small, but I noticed it because the glasses were the joke.
- **image_07 and image_09:** They match the text well.
- **Manga insert:** It was described in text only, with no image files referenced, so I couldn't judge the art. The panel descriptions read clearly, and the two-point structure (her debana-kote, his wrong-foot dō) was easy to follow.

### Would I keep reading?
Yes, and I'd go straight to Chapter 3.
- **What hooked me:** the dream passage ("She says it like a scolding. She says it like she's laughing. / She says it like she's been waiting a very long time."). Both girls' interludes, which are the best writing here, specifically "the way you shut a window against the wind" and "It's the second semesters I never get to." The grief details (Dad's handwriting, going "with the handwriting" on garbage day). The setup that Saturday at the shrine is about to put both girls under the Ōzakura with him, which "It's only trash bags" lands perfectly.
- **What would make me stop:**
  - If Nanoha becomes a pure sacrifice who only ever quietly hurts. Chapter 2 already sidelines her, and she needs a scene where *she* acts.
  - If the reincarnation reveal arrives as an exposition dump, from the grandpa at the shrine for example.
  - If the repeated inline glosses continue at this density.
  - If Mom, Akane and the cold open stay set dressing for much longer.
  - If both girls keep "not letting themselves think about it" for chapters on end. The restraint is lovely for two chapters, and I'd need movement by around chapter 4.

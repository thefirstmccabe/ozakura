# Reader 3 cold-read notes

## Checkpoint 1 (after part1 only)

1. Who is the dream woman?
   No characters met yet, so: a woman from a past life (a previous incarnation's partner), who will almost certainly turn up reincarnated as a girl in the present day. Chapter title "The House Next Door" makes me bet the girl next door is her. Guess: "the girl next door (whoever she turns out to be)" ~45%. "Someone we haven't met / a figure from a past life with no living counterpart" ~25%. Remaining ~30% spread (a ghost, a relative, a red herring for a different heroine).

2. Relationship narrator <-> dream woman?
   Wife (in a past life) 45%. Lover / betrothed 30%. Sibling 10%. Mother/guardian 5%. Childhood friend 5%. Other 5%.
   The intimacy is domestic and habitual ("Hurt again"), not first-meeting. "You're late" + waiting at home feels like a spouse who waits for the swordsman to come back from training.

3. Premise / mystery
   Reincarnation romance. Narrator has dreamed since childhood of a past life as a swordsman (katana hilt in the CG, man's adult forearm, old-style kimono, cherry tree, river). A woman bandaged his wrist and waited for him. The hook: "I kept them waiting far too long" -> they were separated, he failed to come back, maybe he died, maybe she died waiting. Expect: modern school romcom on the surface, with a destined-lovers / past-life mystery underneath. Probably a tear-jerker (Clannad-ish "after story" melancholy) layered under a slice-of-life romcom.

4. What drove it
   - CG: katana hilt bottom-left, a man's adult veined forearm (not a schoolboy's), striped kimono and obi on her side, cherry blossoms. Strongly signals "past era / past life."
   - "There's a dream I've had since I was small." -> recurring past-life memory trope.
   - "You're late." / "She says it like she's been waiting a very long time." -> waiting-lover motif; also double meaning (he's late in being reborn / finding her).
   - "Hurt again. Give me your hand... Hold still." -> habitual caretaker, intimate.
   - "That I kept them waiting far too long." -> guilt, separation, likely tragedy.
   - The cut on the wrist + cloth tied in a knot: I expect a wrist/knot/ribbon motif to recur in the present (someone ties his wrist, or a girl wears a ribbon tied that way).
   - Chapter title "The House Next Door" -> girl-next-door heroine incoming.

## Checkpoint 2 (after part2)

1. Who is the dream woman?
   Nanoha, 65%. The transfer student ("A girl! From somewhere! Far!") 20%, because genre convention says the childhood friend gets all the cozy cues and the mysterious transfer girl turns out to be the "destined" one (or at least is positioned to compete for that slot). Someone else / no present-day counterpart 10%. Mom (i.e. the dream is somehow his parents' story, Dad "late" coming home) 5%, a stray thought because Dad is gone and Mom is shut in her room waiting.
   Not Akane (sister; nothing points there). Not Rika.

2. Relationship narrator <-> dream woman?
   Past-life wife 45%, past-life lover / betrothed 35%, childhood friend in the past life too 10%, sibling 5%, other 5%. In the present: childhood friend / neighbor (if Nanoha).

3. Premise / mystery
   Kōhei Fujisawa, 2nd-year high school, Shirasawa (rural town by a river under the Tanzawa mountains). Dad died last spring (the garbage line in Dad's handwriting he won't write over). Mom is withdrawn, likely depressed, doesn't leave her room. Little sister Akane has some chronic condition (dizzy spells, clinic day circled on the 18th). Kōhei carries the household: lunches, kendo 4x/week, part-time at Hoshino Kitchen 3x/week. He's over-functioning; Nanoha sees it ("So you'd hear yourself say it").
   Nanoha is the next-door childhood friend who walks in through the back door, knows the kitchen blind, brings food since about a year ago (i.e. since Dad died), clearly in love with him, and he's oblivious (the "Probably a stubborn crease" joke). Her grandpa tends the Ōzakura, presumably a great old cherry tree (and the game title). A transfer girl is coming to 2-B.
   Shape: grief-and-healing school romance (Clannad / Blue Box tone) with a past-life thread. My bet: the Ōzakura tree is the hinge between past and present, and the dream is his past self and Nanoha's past self under that tree. The transfer student is either the rival heroine or the person who will "remember" the past life and claim the destined slot, which will be the drama.

4. What drove it
   - "Nanoha walks on the river side. She always walks on the river side. I don't remember when we decided that." -> river in dream ("The sound of a river, close by"), and the "I don't remember when we decided" line reads as deliberately past-life coded.
   - Collar CG: she reaches up and fixes him with both hands, petals falling, river right behind her. Composition rhymes with the dream CG (her hands on his body, fixing; cherry branch overhead; river). "Her hands stay a second longer than the collar strictly needs." echoes "Slow and careful, like the knot matters more than anything else."
   - "Petals... One lands in her hair, right on the clip, and stays there." -> dream's "Petals."
   - "Kō-chan. Your collar." -> same caretaking, mildly scolding register as "Hurt again. Give me your hand."
   - "Grandpa says the Ōzakura's the best it's been in years." -> the title word attached to her family. Big cherry tree = dream's branch.
   - Kendo: in the dream, "a cut on the inside of it from training" and a katana. Present-day Kōhei does kendo. So the swordsman link is through him.
   - The back door, no knock, "since she was tall enough to reach" -> girl next door from the chapter title.
   - Against: Rika's "A girl! From somewhere! Far!" transfer student. Classic setup for a destined stranger. Also Rika's "Hm. Hmmm." about Kōhei in 2-B shows the class already reads Nanoha as in love with him, which in romcom terms sometimes marks the friend as the one who loses.

## Checkpoint 3 (after part3)

1. Who is the dream woman?
   Natsuki (Mizuno Natsuki, the transfer student) 50%. Nanoha 40%. Other 10%.
   I've swung. Part 2 stacked every cozy-atmosphere cue on Nanoha, but part 3 gave Natsuki the two things that read as plot rather than mood: the recognition falter, and the wrist line.

2. Relationship narrator <-> dream woman?
   Past-life wife 35%, lover / betrothed 35%, something adjacent to the sword (dojo master's daughter, fellow student, sparring partner who also patched him up) 15%, sibling 10%, other 5%.
   If it's Natsuki, I now half-expect the past life to be sword-related on both sides, not a husband and a waiting wife.

3. Premise / mystery
   Same base as before (grieving over-functioning boy, sick sister, absent mother, childhood friend who's been quietly holding him up for a year). Now the past-life layer has a candidate who walks in from outside: Natsuki, kendo, moved six times, Sendai most recently, and she visibly falters when she sees him. He also reacts physically (grips the pen till it hurts, can't hear a word of first period). That's the destined-reunion beat.
   So the shape I expect: a triangle of "the girl who's been here all along" (Nanoha, present-life devotion) vs "the girl the dream points to" (Natsuki, past-life destiny). The real question the story will ask is whether a past life gets a claim on the present one. Possibly Natsuki remembers the past and Kōhei doesn't. Possibly the twist is that the cues have been misdirecting and Nanoha is the one, with Natsuki as the swordsman's rival/comrade. Nanoha's immediate, brave kindness to Natsuki sets up a friendship that will hurt later. Feels like it's turning into a Blue Box-style sports-adjacent romance (kendo club, Wednesday practice, a girl who matches him on the floor) with a melancholy reincarnation spine.

4. What drove it
   - "Her grin stutters. Just for a second. Just long enough that I'm sure I'm the only one who sees it." plus the falter CG (smile drops, mouth open, wide-eyed). Recognition.
   - "I realize I've been holding my pen hard enough to hurt." and "I don't hear a single word of it." -> his side of the jolt; hand pain echoes the dream wrist.
   - "She repeats it like she's writing it on the inside of her wrist." -> the single most specific echo of "There's a cut on the inside of it [my wrist]." This is the line that moved me most.
   - "Left palm. You've got the callus right where the tsuka (hilt) sits." -> she reads his hand; dream is all about hands ("Give me your hand"). Also she knows the sword.
   - "I do kendo" -> sword link from the dream CG's katana.
   - Keeping Nanoha at 40%: she's still the one with the river, the petals, the Ōzakura, the caretaking, and the "waiting a very long time" fits her literally (waiting for him to notice her). "the way she holds things when she's about to do something brave" shows the text cares about her interiority, which makes me think she's not just the decoy.
   - Small art note: the text says Natsuki's hair is "cut neat at her shoulders and tucked behind one ear"; in the CG it falls past her shoulders, loose, not tucked. I noticed.

## Checkpoint 4 (after part4, end of chapter)

1. Who is the dream woman?
   Natsuki 60%. Nanoha 35%. Other 5%.
   Natsuki has the plot cues; Nanoha has the voice and the mood cues. I lean Natsuki because the text has her actually feel the past ("someone you've met before?"), but I'm holding a real chunk on Nanoha because the dream woman's voice (scolding like she's laughing, fussing over him, waiting) sounds like Nanoha, not Natsuki. Natsuki's stated question is so blunt for Chapter 1 that part of me smells a deliberate decoy.

2. Relationship narrator <-> dream woman?
   Past-life lover / betrothed 40%, past-life wife 35%, sword-adjacent (master's daughter / fellow student) 15%, sibling 5%, other 5%.
   "Waiting a very long time" + "kept them waiting far too long" now reads to me as a love that ended before it could be finished (he died, or left and didn't come back), which leans lover/betrothed over settled wife.

3. Premise / mystery
   Grief-and-recovery school romance in a small river town, with a reincarnation spine. Kōhei is holding his family together after his father's death; Nanoha has been quietly holding him up and is in love with him; Natsuki arrives with a feeling of having met him before and a sword in her hands. The past life appears to have happened here (river, mountains, cherry trees, the Ōzakura). Natsuki reacts to the place itself ("lived in," the mountain window, "Oh," "like she'd been handed something").
   The story is turning into a triangle of past-life destiny (Natsuki) against present-life devotion (Nanoha), with Nanoha already seeing it coming and choosing to befriend Natsuki anyway. I expect a Clannad / Blue Box tear-jerker where the big question is whether the dream gets a claim on the present, with a possible twist that the cues have been crossed (Nanoha is the dream woman and Natsuki is someone else from that past, e.g. a rival swordsman or a sibling).

4. What drove it
   - Natsuki: "Does he ever seem like… someone you've met before?" -> the bluntest cue in the chapter. Moved me toward Natsuki.
   - "She noticed that the third-floor girls' bathroom had a window that looked straight at the mountains, and she said 'Oh,' very quietly, like she'd been handed something." and "It feels… lived in." -> déjà vu about the place.
   - Nanoha: "I have met him before. Every day. For sixteen years." -> great line, and the irony points both ways: she's the one who has truly "always" known him.
   - "She'd decided it firmly, the way you shut a window against the wind. She did not let herself think about why it had needed deciding at all." -> Nanoha senses a threat she can't name. That's the narrative telling me Natsuki is the danger to her, i.e. the destined one.
   - Nanoha keeps the dream voice: "You're taking out the trash on the wrong day." is the same scolding-but-laughing register as "You're late." Her "Good night, Kō-chan," the window across the gap, and "I stand there for a while longer than the garbage requires" (echo of "a second longer than the collar strictly needs") end the chapter on her. That's why she's still at 35%.
   - Natsuki: "Someone who knows which drawer sticks in your kitchen." -> dramatic irony against Nanoha, and Natsuki is framed as the outsider without roots, which doesn't fit the "waiting at home" woman of the dream. Small point for Nanoha.

## 6. Heavy-handed / too obvious / too coy
   - Heavy-handed: "Does he ever seem like… someone you've met before?" Saying the premise aloud, on day one, to a girl she met five hours ago, is the bluntest beat in the chapter. The jet-lag cover-up is charming, but the line does the reader's work for them.
   - Noticeably authored: "She repeats it like she's writing it on the inside of her wrist." It works, but the simile exists only to point back at the prologue. One of those per chapter is fine.
   - Stock: "Just for a second. Just long enough that I'm sure I'm the only one who sees it." That's a stock phrase for the falter. The CG carries it better than the prose does.
   - Stock: "Her hands stay a second longer than the collar strictly needs. / Probably a stubborn crease." The dense-narrator gag is well worn. I smiled, but I've read it a hundred times.
   - Too coy: the Ōzakura. It gets one line ("Grandpa says the Ōzakura's the best it's been in years") with zero description. A reader who doesn't know the word won't know it's a great cherry tree, so the dream's branch can't connect to it. It's the title. It needs one sensory sentence.
   - Clues that don't discriminate: petals and the river. They're in every outdoor scene (the whole path is a cherry tunnel by a river), so they stop working as Nanoha clues. "She always walks on the river side. I don't remember when we decided that." is the only Nanoha hint with real teeth, and it's subtle enough to miss.
   - Good coy: Kōhei's own reaction to Natsuki (the pen grip, not hearing first period) is never explained in his narration, and he tells Nanoha "She seems fine." I liked that withholding.

## 7. Dialogue
   Lively:
   - Akane: "Medical science is baffled." / "The doctor face. You don't have a license, Onii-chan." / "…That's sort of my point." The last line is the best subtext in the chapter.
   - Nanoha's class-assignment conspiracy: "A file has been opened. There have been meetings." / Kōhei: "The pool is green."
   - Ryōsuke: "That's not a counterargument. That's a timestamp."
   - Natsuki's callus read: "Left palm. You've got the callus right where the tsuka (hilt) sits."
   - Nanoha's catalogue of his faces: "That one's number four. 'Somebody wants to fight me.'" / "What's number one?" / "Secret." This is the best exchange in the chapter.
   - "You're taking out the trash on the wrong day." / "I'm moving it closer to the right day."
   - "Good morning. I'm coming in." / "You're already in." / "Then I've arrived."

   Flat, samey or written (8):
   1. Kōhei: "Looks can be deceiving." A set-up idiom that only exists to feed Nanoha's "That's why I fix them."
   2. Kōhei: "That's what the attendance sheet says." and, a few lines later, "Is that in the welcome packet?" Two generic deadpan-protagonist comebacks in one scene. Any dry MC could say them.
   3. Natsuki: "I will. I'm very decisive. Usually." This is the house "I'm very X" tic. Compare Akane's "I'm very talented," Nanoha's "I do supervise beautifully," "She's very bad at measuring burdock. It's a sickness," and "It's very old." That's three characters with one joke shape.
   4. Natsuki: "Takes one to know one." A cliché, and idiomatically off: it's a playground insult retort, not a recognition line. It deflates the sharp callus observation right before it.
   5. Natsuki: "Oh, he's cocky. Good. I hate practicing with polite people." Stock tomboy-rival line. The third-person "he's" said to his face reads as a translated manga-ism.
   6. Kōhei: "We'll find out on the floor." Stock cool-guy line.
   7. Natsuki: "This school's nice. It feels… lived in. Does that make sense?" Plus "You know?", "Is that okay?", "Is that all right?", "Really.", "Seriously.", "Anyway. Thanks." Her approval-checking padding piles up. It might be deliberate (the perpetual new girl), but on the page it reads as filler rather than characterization.
   8. Kōhei: "Whose side are you on?" / Nanoha: "I like weird." Sitcom stock, interchangeable with any show.

   Covering the names:
   - Rika: instantly identifiable (caps, "Oh! Oh oh oh," "Nano-chaaaan!"), but it's a gimmick, one note.
   - Ryōsuke: identifiable (pedantic rigor: "structurally," "theoretically," "I'll need a diagram"). But his joke is the same joke as Nanoha's: "I've prepared a theory" (Nanoha) and "I chose it theoretically" (Ryōsuke), deadpan pseudo-rigor, in the same part.
   - Nanoha, Akane and Kōhei share one voice: dry statement plus deadpan undercut. Akane's "Hers. She brought kinpira." could be Nanoha's. Nanoha's "It's a sickness." could be Akane's. Kōhei's "The pool is green." could be either.
   - Natsuki: the most distinct of the girls by energy, plus the "Eh, 'san'?" and "Is that okay?" pushiness. But she slips into the house deadpan for punchlines ("Very intense jet lag." is a Nanoha line).
   - Nanoha at her best has a formal-but-teasing register ("I'm coming in," "I'll tell her you said it like you meant it") that is hers. Lean into that and take the generic undercuts away from Akane and Kōhei.
   Net: by voice alone I'd reliably get Rika and Ryōsuke, mostly get Natsuki, and confuse Nanoha, Akane and Kōhei with each other.

## 8. Keep reading?
   Yes. What hooked me:
   - The grief texture. Dad's handwriting on the garbage line, then Kōhei taking the trash out on the wrong day because "tonight I went with the handwriting." That's the best beat in the chapter.
   - Mom's room: "Then a small shift of weight on the other side of the paper... That's an answer. Sort of."
   - "She went looking for mine before her own." That's earned. No Fukuda.
   - The Nanoha interlude: "the way you shut a window against the wind." That one line gave Nanoha an inner life and set up the triangle without melodrama.
   - The closing image: two windows across a gap "so narrow you could pass a book through it."
   - Natsuki's falter CG. The swap from grin to startled is effective.
   What would make me put it down:
   - If it becomes the formula where the childhood friend loses to the destined transfer girl, or the past life simply overrides the present relationship.
   - If the cast keeps riffing in one quippy voice and the tone never drops below banter.
   - If Kōhei stays dense about Nanoha for many chapters ("Probably a stubborn crease" can't be the running gag).
   - If the past-life mystery gets doled out in coy one-line teases for too long with no payoff.
   - Minor art distraction: the text says Natsuki's hair is "cut neat at her shoulders and tucked behind one ear," but the CG shows loose hair past her shoulders. The foreground classmates in her intro CG are four identical black-haired heads, which reads as filler.

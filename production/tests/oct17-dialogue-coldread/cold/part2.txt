[Caption: Monday, April 7]
[Background: Kōhei's bedroom, morning]
My alarm goes off at 5:40.
I'm usually awake before it. I lie there for the full ten seconds anyway, as a matter of principle, listening to the house.
The water heater ticking. A crow on the power line. Nothing from the room down the hall.
Second year of high school starts today. It doesn't feel like anything starts. It feels like Monday.
I get up.
[Background: the Fujisawa kitchen]
The rice was on the timer last night, so the kitchen already smells like steam. Eggs, the leftover spinach, the last of the frozen karaage. Two lunch boxes, side by side on the counter.
I've gotten fast at this. Fast enough to do it while reading the calendar on the wall.
Kendo: Monday, Wednesday, Friday and Saturday morning. Hoshino Kitchen: Tuesday, Thursday and Sunday. A red circle around the eighteenth, Akane's clinic day. All of it in my handwriting.
All of it except the bottom line, which still says "Garbage: burnables, Mon/Thu" in Dad's. He wrote it last spring. I've never written over it.
The fusuma to Mom's room is closed. It usually is.
[Akane appears on screen]
Akane: "Morning, Onii-chan…"
My sister shuffles in already dressed for school, eyes still at half-mast.
Kōhei: "Morning. How'd you sleep?"
Akane: "You asked me that yesterday."
Kōhei: "And?"
Akane: "And I slept yesterday too. Two nights in a row. Medical science is baffled."
I look at her anyway. Color's fine. She isn't leaning on the doorframe. She isn't holding her head the way she does when the room starts tilting on her.
Akane: "Stop doing the face."
Kōhei: "What face?"
Akane: "The doctor face. You don't have a license, Onii-chan."
Kōhei: "I've got an egg with your name on it. That's basically a license."
Akane: "I could make my own lunch, you know. I'm very talented."
Kōhei: "I'm already making two."
Akane: "…That's sort of my point."
She says it to the rice cooker, not to me. I pretend I didn't hear that.
The back door slides open. No knock. There's never a knock.
[Nanoha appears on screen]
Nanoha: "Good morning. I'm coming in."
Kōhei: "You're already in."
Nanoha: "Then I've arrived. Good morning, Akane-chan."
Akane: "Morning, Nanoha-chan!"
She sets a plastic container on the table.
Nanoha: "Kinpira. Mom made too much again."
Kōhei: "Again."
Nanoha: "Again. She's very bad at measuring burdock. It's a sickness."
She's been bringing food over for about a year now. I used to argue. Somewhere around last summer, I gave up.
Kōhei: "Tell her thanks."
Nanoha: "I'll tell her you said it like you meant it."
She goes to the cupboard left of the sink, the one with the mugs and not the one with the glasses, and takes down two without looking. She bumps the drawer under the counter with her hip so it won't stick. She reaches over the sink and jiggles the window latch at the exact angle it needs, so the steam can get out.
She's been doing all of that since she was tall enough to reach.
Which, considering it's Nanoha, wasn't that long ago.
Nanoha: "I heard that."
Kōhei: "I didn't say anything."
Nanoha: "You thought it in a short-person direction."
Akane: "He did. I saw his face."
Kōhei: "Whose side are you on?"
Akane: "Hers. She brought kinpira."
I wrap the lunches. I knock twice on the fusuma.
Kōhei: "Mom, we're heading out. There's rice in the cooker and miso on the stove. Just heat it up."
Nothing.
Then a small shift of weight on the other side of the paper. A creak of floorboard.
That's an answer. Sort of.

[Background: riverside path under cherry trees, morning]
The path to school follows the river for a while before it turns toward town.
This week it's the best walk in Shirasawa. The cherry trees along the embankment are in full bloom, so heavy with flowers the branches sag over the path, and every time the wind comes down off the mountains the whole tunnel lets go at once.
[Nanoha appears on screen]
Nanoha walks on the river side. She always walks on the river side. I don't remember when we decided that.
Nanoha: "So. Class assignments."
Kōhei: "They'll be posted at the entrance."
Nanoha: "That's the boring answer. I've prepared a theory."
Kōhei: "Of course you have."
Nanoha: "There are six second-year classes. On paper, our odds of being in the same one are one in six."
Nanoha: "However. We've been in the same class four years running. A teacher somewhere has noticed. A file has been opened. There have been meetings."
Kōhei: "Meetings."
Nanoha: "In a small room with no windows. They've realized that if we're separated, something terrible happens. The school's feng shui collapses. The pool turns green."
Kōhei: "The pool is green."
Nanoha: "Because last year they put you in 1-C for five days during the exchange program. You remember."
Kōhei: "That was a scheduling error."
Nanoha: "That's what they want you to think."
I laugh. I can't help it.
Nanoha: "You laughed. That's two points for me this morning."
Kōhei: "We're keeping score now?"
Nanoha: "We've always been keeping score. You're losing."
A gust shakes the trees. Petals come down so thick it's like walking through snow that forgot to be cold. One lands in her hair, right on the clip, and stays there.
I don't mention it. It suits her.
Nanoha: "Kō-chan. Your collar."
[Full-screen illustration shown: image file cg_collar.jpg]
Before I can do anything about it, she's stopped in front of me, up on her toes, folding my shirt collar back down over my tie.
She has to stretch to reach. She's always had to stretch to reach.
Her hands stay a second longer than the collar strictly needs.
Probably a stubborn crease.
Nanoha: "There. Now you look like someone who has his life together."
[Full-screen illustration ends]
Kōhei: "Looks can be deceiving."
Nanoha: "That's why I fix them."
We walk on. A bike bell rings somewhere behind us and doesn't pass.
Nanoha: "Practice today?"
Kōhei: "Club doesn't start until Wednesday. First week."
Nanoha: "And after that?"
Kōhei: "Monday, Wednesday, Friday, Saturday morning. Shifts Tuesday, Thursday, Sunday."
Nanoha: "I know."
Kōhei: "Then why'd you ask?"
Nanoha: "So you'd hear yourself say it."
She doesn't say it like a criticism. She says it like a weather report. That's somehow worse.
Kōhei: "It's fine. I like being busy."
Nanoha: "Mm."
A few steps of nothing. The river is loud this time of year, fat with snowmelt from the Tanzawa.
Nanoha: "Oh. Grandpa says the Ōzakura's the best it's been in years. I'm helping him sweep up there Saturday."
Kōhei: "His back again?"
Nanoha: "His back is a very convenient back. It only hurts on days with a lot of petals."
Kōhei: "I'll come after practice. Somebody has to carry the trash bags while you supervise."
Nanoha: "I do supervise beautifully."
She walks a little faster after that, and for a while I actually have to work to keep up.

[Background: school entrance hall]
The entrance hall is chaos. Two hundred students trying to read six sheets of paper at once.
[Rika appears on screen]
Rika: "Nano-chaaaan!"
A blur with pigtails and a cluster of novelty hair clips hits Nanoha from the side.
[Nanoha appears on screen]
Rika: "Two-B! You're in Two-B! I'm in Two-B! It's destiny! It's in the stars! I'm making us matching keychains!"
Nanoha: "Good morning, Rika-chan."
Today's clips are a strawberry, a star and a small frog wearing a crown. The frog is new.
Rika: "And Fujisawa-kun's in Two-B too. Hm. Hmmm."
Kōhei: "What's 'hmmm'?"
Rika: "Nothing! Nothing at all. A completely neutral hmmm."
[Ryōsuke appears on screen]
Ryōsuke: "—which is why, structurally, a hot dog is a sandwich."
Tokiwa has arrived the way he usually does, in the middle of an argument nobody else knew had started.
Kōhei: "Good morning, Tokiwa."
Ryōsuke: "Bread on two sides. Filling in the middle. A hinge doesn't disqualify it. A sub roll has a hinge, and nobody says a sub isn't a sandwich."
Kōhei: "It's seven-fifty in the morning."
Ryōsuke: "That's not a counterargument. That's a timestamp."
Rika: "It's a hot DOG! It says so in the name!"
Ryōsuke: "By that logic, a pancake is a cake."
Rika: "…Isn't it?"
Ryōsuke: "We'll resume this at lunch. I'll need a diagram."
Kōhei: "Please don't."
Ryōsuke: "Also, we're all in Two-B. I've already chosen my seat."
Kōhei: "We haven't seen the room yet."
Ryōsuke: "I chose it theoretically. Third row, by the window, out of the teacher's natural sightline but close enough to the heater for winter."
Rika: "Oh! Oh oh oh, but have you heard? Two-B is getting a transfer student!"
Nanoha: "A transfer student?"
Rika: "A girl! From somewhere! Far!"
Ryōsuke: "'Somewhere far' covers most of the planet."
Rika: "Then we've already narrowed it down!"
While they argue, I squeeze toward the board. I don't make it. Nanoha has already slipped through somewhere at elbow height and is pointing up at a line I can't see yet.
[Nanoha appears on screen]
Nanoha: "Fujisawa Kōhei. Two-B. About halfway down."
Kōhei: "You found mine before yours?"
Nanoha: "Yours was easier. It's always right under Fukuda's. Fukuda is very easy to find."
When I finally get to the board, there's no Fukuda in Two-B this year. She went looking for mine before her own.
I don't know why that's the thing that sticks with me all through the opening ceremony.

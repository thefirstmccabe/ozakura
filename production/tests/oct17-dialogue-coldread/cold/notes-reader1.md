# Reader 1 — cold read notes

## Checkpoint 1 (after part1.txt)

1. Who is the dream woman?
   I don't know yet, and haven't met anyone. Best guess: a woman from a past life (Edo-ish / samurai era) whom the narrator will meet reincarnated, probably as the girl "next door" (the chapter title is "The House Next Door"). So: the neighbour girl, whoever she turns out to be. Confidence that she'll turn out to be the neighbour / a heroine I'm about to meet: 55%. Confidence on "past-life lover reborn" framing: 60%.

2. Relationship narrator <-> dream woman:
   Past-life lover or wife. She scolds him for being late "like she's laughing", has clearly bandaged him many times ("Hurt again"), and the "waiting a very long time" line feels like a wife or betrothed waiting for a samurai to come home. Wife/lover: 65%. Childhood friend / sweetheart (the young-girl-who-waited version): 20%. Sibling or mother: 10%. Other: 5%.

3. Premise / what kind of story:
   Reincarnation romance. The narrator was a swordsman in a previous life; someone waited for him and he didn't come back in time (or came back too late — "I kept them waiting far too long"). In the present he'll meet her again, probably next door. Expect a school romance with a destined-lovers mystery layered on top, maybe a tragic past-life reveal later (did he die? did she?). The wrist cut "from training" may echo in the present (does he do kendo now? a scar/birthmark?).

4. What drove it:
   - The CG: katana hilt bottom-left, woman in a striped kimono, cherry blossom, a man's adult (veined, hairy) forearm being bandaged by small, delicate hands. Clearly period, clearly not a child's arm — so not "me as a kid".
   - "There's a dream I've had since I was small." — recurring dream = past-life memory trope.
   - "There's a cut on the inside of it from training" — swordsman.
   - "She says it like she's been waiting a very long time." / "That I kept them waiting far too long." — the guilt/lateness theme; feels like the hook.
   - "Hurt again." — intimacy and routine; she's done this before. Feels domestic, wife-like.
   - Chapter title "The House Next Door" — signals the girl next door is central.
   - Note: the narration switches from "she" to "them" at the end ("I kept them waiting") — deliberate vagueness, or a hint it's not a romantic partner? Flagging it.

## Checkpoint 2 (after part2.txt)

1. Who is the dream woman?
   Nanoha, or rather a past-life version of her. 60%. The runner-up is the unseen transfer student "from somewhere far" (20%): the genre loves to set up the obvious childhood friend and then have the mysterious new girl be the one who "remembers". Someone else entirely / not a heroine: 20%.
   Caveat: Nanoha is set up so hard as THE dream woman that I half-suspect misdirection.

2. Relationship:
   Past-life lover/wife, mirrored now by childhood friend with an unspoken crush. 60% lover/wife. Possibly something more platonic-but-devoted (a sister, a retainer's daughter) 20%. The "them" wording still nags; 20% that it turns out to be someone non-romantic (mother?) — his mother is behind a closed door and he's "keeping her waiting" in a different sense.

3. Premise / kind of story:
   Grief-tinged slice-of-life romance with a reincarnation thread. Kōhei is carrying the household: Dad is gone (dead, I think — "He wrote it last spring. I've never written over it."), Mom is withdrawn/depressed behind the fusuma, little sister Akane has some chronic dizziness condition with a clinic day. Nanoha, the girl next door, has been quietly propping up the family for a year (food since around when Dad died). The Ōzakura (presumably a giant cherry tree at her grandpa's — shrine?) is going to be the mystical center; the title of the work is probably Ōzakura. The transfer student will be the disruption. I expect: Kōhei overworking himself as avoidance, Nanoha as the person who's been "waiting" for him to notice her, and the past-life frame making that literal. Possibly a tragic loop: past-life swordsman died and left her waiting under the tree.

4. What drove it:
   - The collar CG rhymes almost shot-for-shot with the dream: river right beside them, cherry petals, her small hands fixing something on him, she's much shorter and stretching up. The dream had "The sound of a river, close by" — and "Nanoha walks on the river side. She always walks on the river side. I don't remember when we decided that." That "I don't remember when we decided" reads like a planted past-life echo.
   - "Her hands stay a second longer than the collar strictly needs." echoes the dream's "Slow and careful, like the knot matters more than anything else in the world."
   - "Nanoha: 'Kō-chan. Your collar.'" — fussing/caretaking register, same as "Hurt again. Give me your hand."
   - Kendo four days a week — matches "a cut on the inside of it from training". So Kōhei = the dream swordsman, past or present.
   - "She went looking for mine before her own." — she's been waiting on him / putting him first; the chapter is making the "waiting" theme about her.
   - "She walks a little faster after that" after he volunteers to come Saturday — she's pleased/flustered.
   - "The back door slides open. No knock. There's never a knock." + chapter title "The House Next Door" = she's the next-door girl, title-level importance.
   - Against: Rika's "A girl! From somewhere! Far!" — a transfer student plant. And the dream woman wears a striped kimono, long hair not visible; Nanoha in the CG has a long braid and a hair clip, nothing tying the two visually except height and hands.
   - Side question: Grandpa "says the Ōzakura's the best it's been in years" — the dream has "a branch moving in the wind. Petals." I bet the dream tree IS the Ōzakura.

## Checkpoint 3 (after part3.txt)

1. Who is the dream woman?
   Now genuinely split. Natsuki 45%, Nanoha 40%, someone else 15%.
   Natsuki jumped because the text tied her to two dream-specific details in one scene (the falter on seeing him, and the wrist). Nanoha is still the one the imagery rhymes with (river, petals, small hands fixing him). My gut says the story wants me to think Natsuki is "the destined one" and is building a destiny-vs-devotion triangle, with Nanoha as the one who's actually been waiting. I'm not sure which way the author will land.

2. Relationship:
   Lover/wife in a past life: 55%. Sword-related bond (fellow swordsman's sister, a training partner's wife, a nurse at a dojo) 20%. Sibling 15%. Other 10%. The "Hurt again" + coming home from training still reads domestic.
   New wrinkle: since Natsuki also does kendo, maybe in the past life it was the two of them who trained together and the bandager was someone else. That would make Natsuki = past-life sword companion/rival, and the dream woman = Nanoha. This is my best "clever" theory.

3. Premise / kind of story:
   Reincarnation love triangle. Natsuki recognizes Kōhei on sight (or recognizes something), and is either carrying the same memory or has been looking for him (six moves, "somewhere far"). Nanoha is the present-day caretaker. I expect the Ōzakura tree to be the site where the past-life story happened, and the climax of the arc to be Saturday's sweeping at the tree, where some memory surfaces. Tone: warm comedy surface, grief underneath (dead father, depressed mother, sick sister), with a supernatural/romance mystery. Feels like a Key/Clannad-adjacent setup crossed with a destined-lovers premise.

4. What drove it:
   - "Her grin stutters. Just for a second. Just long enough that I'm sure I'm the only one who sees it." + the falter CG (smile drops, mouth open, eyes wide, hand still up mid-wave). She recognizes him. That's the biggest single clue so far, and it's pointed at Natsuki.
   - "She repeats it like she's writing it on the inside of her wrist." — the inside of the wrist is exactly where the dream cut is. Can't be accidental.
   - "I realize I've been holding my pen hard enough to hurt." — his body reacts to her before his mind does. Dream bleed-through.
   - "Left palm. You've got the callus right where the tsuka (hilt) sits." / "Takes one to know one." — sword link between them; the dream has a katana in frame.
   - "My dad's job moves us around a lot ... six times now?" — rootless, searching, "somewhere far".
   - "She looks almost startled, like she isn't used to being the one who gets asked." — Natsuki seems to be a caretaker/asker type herself; interesting.
   - Pro-Nanoha: nothing new beyond "the way she holds things when she's about to do something brave" — she's bravely making a move toward the rival, which reads as her guarding her territory but kindly. The scene ends with Kōhei feeling "like furniture" between the two girls: triangle framing.

## Checkpoint 4 (after part4.txt, end of chapter)

1. Who is the dream woman?
   Nanoha 50%, Natsuki 40%, someone else 10%.
   Part 4 confirmed that Natsuki has some past-life recognition of Kōhei. But having a past-life link isn't the same as being the dream woman, and every detail of the dream itself (waiting, caretaking, small hands, river, petals, scolding-but-laughing) maps onto Nanoha. My working theory: Natsuki is the one who *remembers*, Nanoha is the one who *waited*, and the dream woman is Nanoha. Natsuki might have been a sword companion/rival/someone else in that life ("Takes one to know one"). I hold this loosely. If the author means Natsuki, the dream imagery is pointing the wrong way; if they mean Nanoha, the Natsuki clues are a very loud red herring.

2. Relationship:
   Past-life wife or lover: 55%. Betrothed / sweetheart who never got to marry him (he died or left, she waited): 20%. Sibling: 10%. Something else (retainer, nurse, someone he failed to protect): 15%.

3. Premise / kind of story:
   A grief-and-romance slice of life in a small mountain-river town (Shirasawa, under the Tanzawa), built on a reincarnation thread centered on the Ōzakura tree. Kōhei is holding a broken house together (dad gone since last spring, mom shut in, sister Akane with a dizziness condition) and using busyness to avoid feeling anything. Nanoha has loved him for years and is practiced at hiding it. Natsuki, the transfer student and fellow kendoka, recognized him on sight and asks whether he seems "like someone you've met before." I expect a triangle where destiny (Natsuki remembers) runs against history (Nanoha has been there every day), with the Ōzakura sweep on Saturday as the next mystical beat. Tonally closer to Key/Clannad or Kimi ni Todoke-with-ghosts than to a harem comedy, though the comedy-relief friends pull it toward the latter.

4. What drove it:
   - Natsuki: "Does he ever seem like… someone you've met before?" — the premise said aloud. It points to Natsuki remembering something.
   - Natsuki at the mountain window: "she said 'Oh,' very quietly, like she'd been handed something." and "This school's nice. It feels… lived in." — she's returning to somewhere.
   - Nanoha: "I have met him before. Every day. For sixteen years." — the best line in the chapter, and it reframes "waiting a very long time" onto her.
   - "Real childhood friends." / "Nanoha said it as lightly as she knew how. She'd had practice." — she's been waiting for him to notice her.
   - "She'd decided it firmly, the way you shut a window against the wind. She did not let herself think about why it had needed deciding at all." — Nanoha feels threatened by Natsuki; triangle confirmed.
   - The ending: two facing windows "so narrow you could pass a book through it", and "I stand there for a while longer than the garbage requires." echoing "Her hands stay a second longer than the collar strictly needs." The chapter closes on Nanoha. Title "The House Next Door" = Nanoha.
   - Dad's handwriting still says Monday and "tonight I went with the handwriting" — the grief thread lands quietly here.

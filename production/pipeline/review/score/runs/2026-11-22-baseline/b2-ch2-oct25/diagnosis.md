# Ch2 "Kendo": dialogue diagnosis (§9 step 3c, part 1)

**Read:** style guide §3b–§3c, the Ch2 dialogue brief, `production/pilot-v2/script/ch02.ozs` (whole chapter), and the lint output (0 flags).
**Line refs:** `#c2_xxxx` IDs, with the file's line numbers (Lnn) where they help.

**Overall:** The chapter mostly works. Natsuki's best material is right on her sheet: she notices physical details ("And you tie your apron like a hakama.", "Are your ears always that red?"), she makes things sound casual ("I'm not done with my flag."), and she challenges people ("Who do I get to fight first?"). Kōhei stays short and literal, Kirishima and the manager stay one-note on purpose, and every must-survive beat in the brief is present. The problems come in three groups:
- The narration explains the two most delicate beats, the bridge and the debrief.
- The lesson closes nearly every exchange on a punchline.
- A few jokes are borrowed from other voices: Nanoha's deadpan, Ryōsuke's taiyaki bit, and a stock sitcom flub.

## 2. Line-by-line verdicts

Every line not listed here is **KEEP**.

### c2_tuesday
- **L1. #c2_0008 (L28)** "That's one. The manager says it about nine times a night." **FLAT (explains itself).** The second sentence announces the payoff in advance. "That's one." and later "That's nine." carry the count on their own.
- **L2. #c2_0042 / #c2_0043 (L67–68)** "Head first or tail first?" / "Don't start." **FLAT (sitcom callback).** This is Ryōsuke's taiyaki debate in Natsuki's mouth. If she wasn't there for his Ch1 entrance, it's a coincidence the reader is asked to enjoy. If she was, it's still his mechanism (trivia), not hers (noticing things, poking). "Don't start." is a canned button that closes an exchange that was doing fine without one.
- **L3. #c2_0054 (L78)** "See you tomorrow, Fujisawa. Wednesday." **FLAT (unclear).** Tomorrow *is* Wednesday, so "Wednesday." reads as redundant unless it means practice, and the reader can't decode that. It's also the same sign-off she uses again at #c2_0248.
- **L4. #c2_0299 (L84)** "…with a sticky note on the lid in Nanoha's handwriting. It says "Again."" **FLAT (too tidy, and placed badly).** It's a neat closing button, and it gives the last beat of Natsuki's best scene to Nanoha (see §3). It also makes three food-and-note beats in the chapter (#c2_0009, this one, #c2_0253). The other two pair up well: Kōhei leaving notes for Akane, and Natsuki's absent mother leaving notes for her. This one blurs that pairing. (Its meaning also depends on a Ch1 setup I wasn't given.)

### c2_dojo
- **L5. #c2_0064–#c2_0066 (L97–99)** "Your hakama is tied off center." / "It's the same knot as always." / "Yes. It has been off center for a year." **FLAT (rerun, and Takatsuki on purpose).** This has the same shape as the apron exchange one scene earlier: a detail is noticed, Kōhei defends it literally ("It stays tied." / "It's the same knot as always."), and a topper follows. The topper lands as an intentional burn. His sheet makes his humor unintentional, and his "Yes." concession comes back at #c2_0172, where it works better because it's left open.
- **L6. #c2_0069 (L103)** "…the only person I know who can quiet a room by not raising his voice." **FLAT (generic).** A stock phrase. The second sentence ("nothing has ever surprised him") is fine.
- **L7. #c2_0082 (L117)** "Nobody laughs. He seems satisfied anyway." **FLAT (explains itself, tidy button).** His sheet says he doesn't wait for the laugh. The second sentence narrates his reaction instead of showing him move on to the next drill, which is how his sheet has him dodge.

## 3. Scene-level problems

**Does every exchange end on a neat button?** Too many do.
- **Scene endings.** Five of eight scenes close on a button: Tuesday ("It says "Again.""), dojo ("He seems satisfied anyway."), lesson ("From memory." / "From memory."), match (the shrine-stairs simile, mild), and the bridge (three stacked closers).
- **The lesson.** Seven of its eight exchanges end on a topper. The only exceptions are the two interruptions, "That's not why you—" (#c2_0105) and "Incomplete how?" (#c2_0131), and they're the scene's best texture. The fixes are L10, L11, L12 and L13.
- **Exchanges that end well without a button:** "Captain, I could take her, if—"; "It scored." / "Yes."; "I can't find one."; "We finish the floor. We don't race." More of the chapter should sound like these.

**Are feelings stated instead of shown?** Mostly shown, and well:
- Shown: the flag in the pen cup, the crossed-out boxes, "I'm aware of exactly where my hand is on her back.", the laugh that "comes back thin," "I think about it for longer than I'd like her to see."
- Stated: L15 (#c2_0163), L17 (#c2_0216), L18 (#c2_0224), L19 (#c2_0227), L20 (#c2_0250), L21 (#c2_0260). Four of these six sit on the bridge and in the interlude, which are exactly the scenes that should carry the most subtext.
- #c2_0046 ("Then they get careful with me.") names the chapter's theme outright. It earns that once, because it's specific and it's the only place the reader learns how people usually treat him. Don't add a second.

**Does each character use their own humor mechanism?**
- **Natsuki:** mostly yes (the apron, the kids' plate, "Race you!", the ears, "Depends."). Her two misses are borrowed jokes: Nanoha's deadpan (L10) and Ryōsuke's trivia (L2). These matter more than their size, because this chapter's job is to make her as likable as Nanoha *in her own voice*.
- **Kōhei:** a straight man throughout, and he never tops anyone. The exceptions are "Don't start." (L2) and the flub (L16).
- **Takatsuki:** unintentional and literal ("…If it's educational.", the zanshin payoff), except L5.
- **Kirishima:** right. The late deadpan lands twice ("That was a joke.", "From memory.").
- **Rika:** right at the door (#c2_0084, #c2_0296), then drifts dry (L8, L11). The art-club assignment is a good specific obsession.
- **Manager and Chie:** right.

**Does narration repeat or vouch for the dialogue?** Yes, in eight places: L1, L7, L9, L14, L15, L19, L20 and L21. The common move is narration telling the reader what a line meant just after (or just before) the line says it.

**Tics from §3b's list:**
- The "I'm very X" joke: none. The only "very" is "the very last page."
- Narration's "She says it like…": none.
- "The way you…": none. The two uses of "the way" are "the way it does" and "the way it starts."
- Two girls reading Kōhei's face: no, only Natsuki (his ears, his stance).
- American-internet bits: none at the level of the hot-dog debate. "you guys" (#c2_0088) and "Look it up." (#c2_0201) are low risk.

**Other problems:**
- **S1. A cousin of the listed tic: narration reading Natsuki through "like she's…".** Two instances: "Her eyes are wide and confused, like she doesn't know where it came from either." (#c2_0194) and "She looks at me for a long second, like she's waiting for the rest of the joke." (#c2_0238). The chapter also has about ten "like" similes in Kōhei's narration, which is a lot for a narrator who talks in fragments. Most of them are good ("like a door swinging shut," "like a museum guide"). The cut at L19 removes the weakest; keep the "like she's" readings to one.
- **S2. Stacked short sentences in the narration.** "I finish the floor. I count out the register. I walk home up the hill." (#c2_0056), "I get up. I don't know why I get up." (#c2_0123), "We bow. We step in. We crouch at the line…" (#c2_0138), "We step back to the line. We bow." (#c2_0151), and "We finish the floor. We don't race." (#c2_0210). That's five uses. Each one is fine alone, but together they become a rhythm tic. Keep the bow ones and #c2_0210.
- **S3. Rika's `scheme` expression** is on three plain questions (#c2_0088, #c2_0101, #c2_0111), and there's no scheme in her dialogue. Either give her one or change the expression.
- **S4. The brief's expression rules.**
  - Natsuki asks questions without `curious` at #c2_0040 (smile), #c2_0042 (laugh), #c2_0071 (kendo_smile), #c2_0160 (smirk), #c2_0176 (grin) and #c2_0239 (laugh).
  - #c2_0160 also collides two rules: it's a competitive beat, which allows the smirk, but it's a question, which calls for `curious`. Production should pick one.
  - Her smirk on "Depends." (#c2_0231) isn't a competitive beat. It's a bargaining flirt.
- **S5. Address.** Rika's chant "Fu-ji-sa-wa!" (#c2_0122) drops her "-kun" (§3c: Rika → Kōhei is "Fujisawa-kun"). It's defensible, since chants drop honorifics, but decide it and log it.
- **S6. English and Japanese don't match in two lines.** "Captain." vs はい。 (#c2_0156), and "Like a teacher." vs ……先生っぽい。 (#c2_0293); the English drops the hesitation.
- **S7. A missing gloss (Production).** "Bogu" first appears unglossed in Kirishima's line #c2_0076. The gloss doesn't come until #c2_0292.
- **B1.** Kōhei's "Mm." (#c2_0221) is Nanoha's listed dodge. It's minor, but it's the one place his blind-test voice slips toward hers.

**A structural note behind L4.** Both of Natsuki's walk-home scenes end by cutting to Nanoha: the "Again." note, and then Nanoha's lit window with "It's only trash bags." The brief says Ch2 has to pull the reader's wanting from about 65/35 toward even, and twice the last word goes to Nanoha. The bridge ending earns its place: it carries the unspoken shrine secret, and "It's only trash bags." is good denial. The Tuesday note doesn't earn its place in the same way.

## 4. Five highest-value fixes (ranked)

1. **Stop narrating the bridge.** Cut #c2_0227–0228 and #c2_0250, and give Natsuki one retreat instead of two (#c2_0224). Her invitation, his counter and the half-date should all stay unexplained.
2. **Give Natsuki's jokes back to her own mechanism.** Replace "So be afraid." and the borrowed taiyaki head-or-tail bit (with Kōhei's "Don't start." button) with pokes, challenges or noticed details. This is the exact Oct 17 failure, in the chapter meant to make her distinct.
3. **Don't end her Tuesday scene on Nanoha.** Cut or move the "Again." note so the scene closes on his rough throat. If the author asked for that note, it's his call.
4. **In the catch aftermath, cut Kōhei's "You too" flub** and let "…No." be his only flustered line. Sincere moments should make him shorter, not sitcom.
5. **Thin the lesson's punchlines.** Cut Rika's restatement (#c2_0109) and one of the three toppers at #c2_0126–0128, and let at least two exchanges end on an interruption or a miss. Then remove the narration that pre-states lines elsewhere: "Half a second" in #c2_0141, and the restatement of her joy in #c2_0163.

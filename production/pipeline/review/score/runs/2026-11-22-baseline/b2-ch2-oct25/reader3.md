# Reader 3: cold-read notes

## Checkpoint 1 (after part1)

**Who does Kōhei end up with, and how sure?** Nanoha, about 45%. She's the only girl on the board so far, and the chapter is built around her: the title card ("The House Next Door"), the back door with "No knock. There's never a knock," the mugs she takes down without looking, the collar CG. But I've read too many romcoms to trust a childhood friend who is this far ahead in chapter 1. The genre habit is that a new girl turns up (a transfer student, or someone tied to the sword and fog prologue) and the childhood friend becomes the one who loses. The 45% is mostly me hedging against that.

**Who do I want him with?** Nanoha, moderately (6/10). She's the best-written thing in the part: the probability "theory" ("It's not luck. It's paperwork."), "Rika-chan counts as several," and keeping score. I like her already, but I've barely met anyone else.

**Who is the woman in the dream, and how sure?** I haven't seen a woman, and nothing has told me there's a dream. The untexted opening is three images: a foggy rural crossroads with a convex traffic mirror, a close-up of a hand gripping a katana (dark zip jacket, the hand reads male to me), and fog boiling up over an empty road. Then the crossroads image again. If that's "the dream," my guess is that the sword hand is Kōhei's (he does kendo). I have no guess about a woman, 0% on anyone. If the author meant a woman to register here, she doesn't: there's no figure in any of the three images.

**What does the dream have to do with the present?** I can only guess. Fog, a crossroads and a real sword point to something supernatural or a past life, probably tied to the Ōzakura ("older than anyone's records") and the shrine Nanoha's grandpa looks after. The crossroads image showing twice suggests a choice, or a loop. The mirror could be a "seeing what's behind you" motif. Kendo plus a real katana suggests the sword-in-the-fog boy is a version of Kōhei.

**What I think is going on, and what kind of story this is:** a slice-of-life family drama under a school romance. Dad is gone: "He wrote it last spring. I've never written over it." I read that as dead, about a year ago. Mom is shut behind the fusuma, unresponsive, so depressed or grieving. Akane has some chronic illness: dizziness ("when the room starts tilting on her") and a clinic day. Kōhei is the son who keeps the house going: the whiteboard all in his handwriting, two lunch boxes. Nanoha's food deliveries started "about a year now," which lines up with Dad's death, so she has been quietly propping them up. That's a Clannad-type setup: a burdened protagonist, a caretaker heroine, and a supernatural layer (the Ōzakura) still to come.

**What drove the guesses:**
- "All of it except the bottom line, which still says 'Garbage: burnables, Mon/Thu' in Dad's." This is the strongest line in the part. It does the grief quietly.
- "She's been bringing food over for about a year now." Timed to Dad.
- "Her hands stay a second longer than they strictly need to." / "She walks a little faster after that." Nanoha's feelings are spelled out to the reader but not to Kōhei: the classic dense protagonist.
- "Saturday afternoons are free." … "I'm helping him sweep up there Saturday." She's engineering time with him, and the shrine is set up for Saturday.
- "Akane: '…You always are.' She says it to the rice cooker." Akane worries about her brother. Nice.

**Small things I noticed:**
- Image_04 (the collar CG): the text says "Nanoha walks on the river side," but in the CG the river is behind Kōhei on the left and Nanoha stands on the far side from it. She has stepped in front of him, so it's defensible, but it reads as switched. Her hands are on his tie knot more than on a collar being folded down over the tie. The petal on her braid is there, which is good.
- The height jokes ("Which, considering it's Nanoha, wasn't that long ago"; "You thought it in a short-person direction") don't quite match the CG. She comes up to about his chin, which is ordinary rather than notably short.
- Nanoha says "I heard that" to a thought Kōhei never says aloud, and he answers "I didn't say anything." That's a cute bit, but on a first read I wasn't sure whether the gag was that she reads him perfectly, or whether he muttered it. It works either way.
- The schedule appears twice in quick succession: the whiteboard, then Kōhei reciting it to Nanoha. The second time has a payoff ("So you'd hear yourself say it"), so it earns the repeat, but only just.
- The translator's notes for karaage and kinpira are fine, but the kinpira note is long for a side dish.

- **Image_08 vs. text:** a good match overall (faded pale gi against his dark indigo, the bucket and zōkin, the orange light from the high windows, the wet shine, one tear). Two small issues. (1) "Her face is close. Close enough to see the sweat at her hairline…": in the CG there's a lot of air between their faces, and he's looking at her from above at what reads as an arm's length. (2) "Are your ears always that red?": the art blushes his cheeks, but his ears aren't visibly red. That's minor, but it's the punchline of the scene.
- **The tear is a familiar device.** It's well executed, but any reader of the genre has seen "a tear she can't explain" before. It's not heavy-handed in its writing, but it's the most predictable beat so far. "Allergies" saves it with humor.
- Cedar pollen in early April isn't actually implausible in Kanagawa (the season tails into April), so Kōhei's "…It's April" is slightly off as a rebuttal. Nobody but a hay-fever sufferer will notice, and "Late bloomers. They're real" covers it anyway.
- The pacing goes from a race to a slip to a catch to a tear to a deflection to "ears" in about 30 lines. It's quick, but it's earned by the long dojo part before it.

## Checkpoint 8 (after part8, end of Chapter 2)

**Who does Kōhei end up with, and how sure?** Natsuki 55%, Nanoha 43%, other 2%. The bridge scene hands Natsuki "Saturday afternoons," which was Nanoha's slot from the first walk to school. "It occurs to me that something's been decided, and I wasn't the one who decided it." Then: "the light's on in Nanoha's window across the gap. / It's only trash bags." That last line pulls me back toward Nanoha a little. Kōhei is rationalizing, which means part of him knows Saturday was hers. He feels guilty, and you only feel guilty about someone who matters.

**Who do I want him with?** Natsuki 5.5, Nanoha 4.5. Natsuki's interlude is the best piece of writing in chapter 2. "I'm amazing at first days. But "It's only trash bags" means I'm going to be very upset on Nanoha's behalf on Saturday, which is presumably the point.

**Who is the woman in the dream, and how sure?** Natsuki 55%, Nanoha 30%, other 15%. Natsuki has "been wanting to photograph that thing since Monday" (the Ōzakura), so she's drawn to the tree. She cried without knowing why, and she "knew what he was going to do before he did it." But the interlude also gives the mundane explanations a fair hearing ("The fall, probably," the Sendai tournament question in chapter 1, "you learned to read shoulders and feet"). Neither girl has been shown having the dream herself. I still can't get past the dream's text casting the woman as the one who waits at home, while Natsuki is the one who never stays.

**What does the dream have to do with the present?** Saturday at the shrine is the convergence point: Nanoha sweeping for her grandfather, Natsuki coming to photograph the Ōzakura and to work on her "project," and Kōhei carrying the trash bags between them. I expect the next dream fragment, or something uncanny, to happen under that tree. The fog and modern sword from the silent prologue still haven't come back. I assume that's the far end of the story.

**What I think is going on, and what kind of story this is:** a balanced love triangle in which both girls like each other and see that as "its own small problem," wrapped around a past-life thread that the reader can see and the characters can't yet, with a grief story about a missing father and a shut-in mother underneath. Kōhei's arc looks like learning to want something for himself ("Tell me one thing you do for fun." … "Carrying trash bags up at a shrine.").

# Final answers

**When I became fairly sure (70%+) of anything:**
- *That this is a past-life or recurring-dream story with Kōhei as the swordsman:* "There's a dream I've had since I was small." followed by "My wrist stings. There's a cut on the inside of it, where a blade got past me in training." Together with image_07 (kimono, katana, cherry branch), I was at about 80% here, at the end of chapter 1. It was set up by "I've always been comfortable holding one. Since the first time I picked one up."
- *That Nanoha loves Kōhei:* 70%+ at "Her hands stay a second longer than they strictly need to." (part 1), about 95% at "She did not let herself think about why it had needed deciding at all." (part 3).
- *That Natsuki is falling for him:* 70%+ at "It wasn't. She's been carrying it since the dojo, like a cup filled too full." (part 8).
- *Who he ends up with:* never above 58% (Natsuki, after the tear). *Who the dream woman is:* never above 55% for anyone. Both stayed under 70% for the whole two chapters. For a two-chapter opening that's arguably correct, since the triangle is genuinely balanced. But note that my dream-woman guess flipped twice (Natsuki to Nanoha to Natsuki). The text supports both readings, and I can't tell which one the author intends to be the "fair" one.

**Hints that felt heavy-handed, too obvious or too coy:**
- *Heavy:* "I've always been comfortable holding one. Since the first time I picked one up." (part 3). Coming after a katana prologue, this is a neon "past life" sign in chapter 1. The next line ("with one in my hands, everything else goes quiet") does the same job as character, and better.
- *Obvious:* the unexplained tear on the catch (part 7). It's well written but a stock device (Kimi no Na wa). The "Allergies" / "It's April" comedy rescues it, and Chie's "it's april" callback is a good payoff.
- *Too neat:* Natsuki's "Someone who knows which drawer sticks in your kitchen" (part 3) lands on the exact detail from Nanoha's part 1 kitchen. I could see the author's hand.
- *Too coy:* the silent prologue (image_01–03, then image_01 again). There's no text, no figure except a hand, nothing to label it a dream or a flash-forward, and nothing in two chapters refers back to the fog, the crossroads or the convex mirror. The hand wears a modern zip jacket, so it isn't the later kimono dream, but only a reader who compares sleeves will catch that. As it stands, it's mood without a hook. One line of text (even "???: '…'") or one echo later (fog on the river, the mirror at a corner Kōhei passes) would tether it.
- *Possibly too coy:* the dream woman's identity. If the author intends Natsuki, it works as is (the loud clues win). If the author intends Nanoha, the quiet clues (the waiting, the scolding-laughing voice, careful hands, the river, a caretaker in plain kimono, her shrine family) carried me, but I doubt a less genre-trained reader would pick them up over the tear.
- *Well calibrated:* "It has been off center for a year" (Takatsuki), "tonight I went with the handwriting," "She went looking for mine before her own." These are the best clues in the build: quiet, specific, and they reward attention.

**Dialogue that felt lively:**
- Nanoha's staff-room folder theory ("It's not luck. It's paperwork." / "'Do not separate,' underlined twice." / "Least of all the new friends.") and "Rika-chan counts as several."
- "I have met him before. Every day. For sixteen years."

- The "Lifesaver!" count, ending in "…Wait. Do you work here?" / "I'm new."
- The whole kendo lesson: "So be afraid." / "Somebody hits you in the head, and the referee goes, 'There was a head.'" / "You're doing great, visual aid." / Kirishima's "That was a joke."
- "You have several. I've catalogued them. That one's number four."
- Chie's texts.

**Up to 8 flat lines, and why:**
1. Kōhei: "Good morning, Tokiwa." (part 2). The narration has already done the entrance ("in the middle of an argument nobody else knew had started"), so this is dead air. It also sounds stiffer than Kōhei anywhere else.
2. Natsuki: "Looking forward to this year." (part 2). It's the line right after her grin stutters, so it should show her recovering (a beat too bright, a word fumbled). It's generic instead, and the recovery is lost.
3. Natsuki: "I just mean it was a good match. I haven't had one like that in a long time." (part 8). It explains the subtext that the narration explains both before and after it.
4. Natsuki: "Is that the shrine? With the big tree?" (part 8). Exposition for the reader; she already knows (see above).
5. Nanoha: "She's nice, Kōhei. I like her." (part 4). It restates the interlude. It could work as her performing, but nothing in the delivery or the narration marks it as a performance.
6. Classmate: "Nacchan, you have to get the strawberry parfait. Everybody gets the parfait." (part 5). Generic background chatter. That's fine for a mob voice, but the three "Classmate" lines are interchangeable.
7. Kōhei: "Since I was little." (part 3). This is intentional reticence, but next to Natsuki's specific "Kendo since I was seven" it reads as nothing. "Since I was five. Dad's idea." or similar would give the reader a hook into the father.
8. Takatsuki: "It is the hardest thing in kendo to do on purpose." (part 6). It's a pure explainer line, there to set up his later praise. His later lines ("Luck is not a technique," "It scored." / "Yes.") are much better.

**Would I keep reading?** Yes, definitely through the Saturday at the shrine.
- *What hooked me:* the grief written in objects (Dad's whiteboard line, the trash day, the knot that's been "off center for a year"); "I have met him before. Every day. For sixteen years."; Natsuki's boxes and "second semesters"; the Saturday collision the chapter has carefully built (Nanoha's sweeping, Natsuki's "project," the Ōzakura); and wanting to know who the woman with the bandage is.

  2. If Nanoha keeps disappearing. She's absent from chapter 2 except one sticky note, and my attachment to her visibly decayed checkpoint by checkpoint.

# Reader 1 notes: cold read

## Checkpoint 1 (after part 1)

**Who Kōhei ends up with:** Nanoha, about 50%. She's the only girl on the board so far, and the book is clearly pitching her: she walks into his kitchen without knocking, knows which cupboard has the mugs, fixes his collar. But that is exactly the textbook childhood-friend opening, and in these stories the childhood friend often loses to someone who hasn't shown up yet. So the other 50% is "a girl we haven't met" (a transfer student, someone tied to the shrine or the fog).

**Who I want him to end up with:** Nanoha, fairly strongly (7/10). She's the best thing in the part: dry, quick, and the affection is shown through what she does, not said. "You thought it in a short-person direction" made me like her.

**Who the woman in the dream is:** I didn't see a woman. The opening "dream" (if that's what it is) is three images: an empty foggy crossroads with a convex traffic mirror, a man's hand gripping a sheathed katana, and a wall of fog rolling down a road. No woman appears. If I have to guess who the story wants me to think about: no idea, under 10% on anyone. If anything, the hand reads male (rough knuckles, dark zip jacket), so my first thought was Kōhei or his dad, not a woman.

**What the dream has to do with the present:** My guesses, low confidence:
- Dad is gone ("He wrote it last spring. I've never written over it."). Mom is shut in her room. A foggy blind intersection with a traffic mirror reads like the scene of a car accident to me, so maybe Dad died there last spring in fog. ~35%.
- The katana ties to Kōhei's kendo, but a real sword in a sheath, not a bamboo shinai, feels older and stranger, maybe something supernatural or past-life tied to the shrine and the Ōzakura tree. ~30%.
- The fog that comes "down off the mountains" in the narration echoes the fog in the images, so the dream is probably set in this town (Shirasawa).

**What I think is going on / what kind of story:** A slice-of-life school romance on top of a family in trouble: father gone a year, mother withdrawn behind the fusuma, little sister with something medical (dizziness, "the room starts tilting on her," a clinic day circled on the 18th), and Kōhei holding the house together with a whiteboard schedule. The ancient Ōzakura tree at the shrine, Nanoha's grandpa at the shrine, and the fog prologue suggest a supernatural or mystery layer will come in. I expect a melancholy, Key/Kyoto-Animation-style "small town, big tree, grief" story.

**Lines/images that drove my guesses:**
- "All of it except the bottom line, which still says 'Garbage: burnables, Mon/Thu' in Dad's. He wrote it last spring. I've never written over it." Strongest line in the part; told me Dad is dead or gone without saying it.
- "The fusuma (sliding door) to Mom's room is closed. It usually is." / "That's an answer. Sort of."
- "She isn't holding her head the way she does when the room starts tilting on her." (Akane's illness)
- "She's been doing all of that since she was tall enough to reach." and the collar CG with "Her hands stay a second longer than they strictly need to." (Nanoha as the romance lead)
- "the Ōzakura is blooming too: one cherry tree older than anyone's records" (title tree, presumably the supernatural anchor)
- image_02 (hand on a sheathed katana) vs. "Kendo: Monday, Wednesday, Friday..." (link to Kōhei?)
- image_01 convex mirror at a blind corner in fog (accident?)

**Small notes so far:**
- The collar CG (image_04): the text says "Nanoha walks on the river side," but in the picture Kōhei is the one on the river side and she's on the tree side. Minor, since she has stepped in front of him, but it's the first thing I checked.

**Who the woman in the dream is:** Natsuki (or her past self) ~60%, Nanoha ~30%, other ~10%. Natsuki being drawn to the Ōzakura ("I've been wanting to photograph that thing since Monday") and admitting to herself that the match "didn't feel like it covered it" keep her in front. The dream voice still sounds like Nanoha to me, and Nanoha's family owns the shrine with the tree, so I'm holding real odds on her.

**What the dream has to do with the present:** Past-life reunion under the Ōzakura, and Saturday at the shrine is where it'll start to surface. ~70% that the tree/shrine visit triggers something for Natsuki or Kōhei. I still can't place the modern-looking prologue.

**What kind of story:** A warm, well-observed small-town romance with a reincarnation mystery underneath, where all three leads are lonely in different ways (Kōhei holding up a grieving house, Nanoha waiting in plain sight, Natsuki never staying long enough to be known). That last framing is what the two interludes are for, and it works.

**Notes on this part:**

# Final answers

## When I became fairly sure (70%+) of anything
- **That Natsuki is part of the dream/past-life story in some way** (as the woman or as his old sparring partner): at the catch. "One tear, sliding from the corner of her eye down her cheek. Her eyes are wide and confused, like she doesn't know where it came from either." Before that I was around 60–65%.
- **That the dream is a past-life memory tied to this town and its tree** (as opposed to an ordinary memory of Dad, or an accident): fairly sure (65%, close to 70) at the end of Chapter 1 with "There's a dream I've had since I was small." plus image_07 (kimono, katana, cherry branch). It went past 70% at the tear.
- **Who Kōhei ends up with:** never reached 70%. Peak was Natsuki at 65% after the catch. I think that's healthy for two chapters in.
- **Who the dream woman is:** never reached 70%. Peak was Natsuki at 60%. The dream's voice ("like a scolding… like she's laughing… waiting a very long time") kept pulling me back toward Nanoha.

## Hints that felt heavy-handed, too obvious, or too coy
**Heavy-handed / too obvious:**
- The stack of "Natsuki already knows him" signals: grin stutters (part 2) → "I feel like I've met him somewhere before" (part 3) → she reads his feint and is "there before I moved" (part 6) → unexplained tear (part 7) → "i knew what he was going to do before he did it. every time" (part 8). Each one is fine alone. Five in two chapters, with the last one restating the dojo, is a lot. I'd cut or soften one (probably the Sendai "met him before" line or the text message).
- "Someone who knows which drawer sticks in your kitchen." Natsuki's example lands exactly on the part-1 detail. It feels like the author reaching in.
- The ends of both interludes spell out what the images already said ("She did not let herself think about why it had needed deciding at all.").

**Too coy:**
- The prologue (image_01–03): fog, a traffic mirror at a blind corner, a hand on a sword in a modern zip jacket. Nothing in two chapters refers back to it, it has no woman in it, and it doesn't match the chapter-1 dream's period setting. I couldn't tell whether it's a flash-forward, a different dream, or the same dream drawn inconsistently. One line of narration connecting it would help.
- Kōhei's own reaction in part 2 ("I realize I've been holding my pen hard enough to hurt.") suggests he feels something odd about her too, but his narration never comes back to it. If he recognizes her on some level, I want one more beat. If he's just flustered, the line oversells it.
- The family: what happened to Dad, what's wrong with Mom, what Akane's illness is. Two chapters of hints with no plain fact is about at the limit. I'm fine for now, but a casual reader needs one concrete anchor soon.

**Well-judged (for contrast):** Dad's handwriting on the whiteboard and the garbage-day payoff; "It has been off center for a year."; the Fukuda list gag. All three trust the reader.

## Dialogue that felt lively
- Nanoha's class-folder theory ("'Do not separate,' underlined twice." / "Least of all the new friends.") and "You thought it in a short-person direction."
- The face catalogue: "That one's number four. 'Somebody wants to fight me.'" / "What's number one?" / "Secret."
- Rika throughout, especially "Then we've already narrowed it down!" and "Somebody hits you in the head, and the referee goes, 'There was a head.'"
- Tokiwa's taiyaki thermodynamics.
- The kids' plate ("Do you check ID for the kids' plate?") and "I'm new."
- Kirishima: "That was a joke." / "From memory."
- Takatsuki: "Luck is not a technique." and the zanshin payoff.
- The Chie texts.

## Up to 8 flat lines, and why
1. **Natsuki: "I also love horror movies, the cheesier the better, so if anyone wants to watch a bad one with me, come find me."** Reads like a character profile, not a person talking.
2. **Natsuki: "Serious question. Is this building haunted? Something in the music room was knocking." / Nanoha: "It's old. The pipes sound like a ghost."** Exists only to remind us of the horror-movie trait; goes nowhere.
3. **Natsuki: "Allergies. It's allergies. Cedar pollen. Kanagawa's full of cedar, right?"** The "Kanagawa" part sounds like it's there to tell the reader the prefecture.
4. **Natsuki: "I just mean it was a good match. I haven't had one like that in a long time."** Generic; tells us what the scene already showed. "It was a good match" alone, then her looking at the river, would be stronger.
5. **"Half a second, I stop to think. Then I go."** Awkward sentence at a key moment. "For half a second I stop to think" reads cleaner.
6. **Nanoha: "She's nice, Kōhei. I like her."** On purpose (she's performing it), but it repeats what the interlude just told us, so it lands flat instead of sad.
7. **Classmate: "Nacchan, you have to get the strawberry parfait. Everybody gets the parfait."** Generic walk-on filler.
8. **Text: "i knew what he was going to do before he did it. every time"** Flat because it's the third telling of the same point (see above).

## Could I tell the characters apart with names covered?
Mostly yes.
- **Nanoha:** dry, deadpan, mock-formal ("Now then." "I've prepared a theory."), keeps score. Most distinctive voice in the book. Shifts to hesitant ("Um—", "I'm working up to it") with Natsuki, but the text sets that up.
- **Natsuki:** brisk, teasing, challenge-first ("Who do I get to fight first?", "So be afraid."), casual "Eh," and "Okay, okay." Distinct, but she and Nanoha share one habit: both tease Kōhei by reading his body (Nanoha's face catalogue, Natsuki's callus/posture/apron reads). Lines like "Big talk for someone who sits like he's being graded." or "Then I'm a spiritual first-year." could be either girl. It's a deliberate parallel, but it blurs them a bit.
- **Rika:** unmistakable (exclamations, triples, "Oh oh oh").
- **Akane:** bubbly ("Geez, Onii-chan!", "Yaaay"); with names covered she could pass for Rika, though context separates them.
- **Tokiwa (Ryōsuke) and Takatsuki:** the pair I'd mix up. Both are pedantic correctors ("That isn't an opinion. It's thermodynamics." vs. "Luck is not a technique."), and both have T-names. Takatsuki's no-contractions formality is the only real tell. Also, Tokiwa's speaker tag is "Ryōsuke" but the narration only ever calls him "Tokiwa," and nothing explicitly links the two names. I had to infer it.
- **Kirishima:** terse and dry, a little close to Takatsuki's terseness, but his deadpan jokes separate him.
- **Kōhei:** straight man, short lines; consistent.

1. **image_04 (collar):** text says "Nanoha walks on the river side," but in the CG Kōhei is on the river side and Nanoha is on the tree side. (She's also holding his tie knot rather than folding his collar, which is close enough.)
2. **image_06 (grin stutters):** Kōhei sits "second-to-last row by the window," and the window is on the left of the frame, but Natsuki's eyes point to the right side of the room. If the point is that she's looking at him, she's looking the wrong way.
3. **image_06:** her hand is frozen mid-wave while her face drops. Slightly odd, though it does sell "same instant."
4. **image_02 vs. image_07:** the prologue hand-on-sword wears a modern zip-up jacket; the chapter-1 dream is period (kimono, cloth bandage). If they're meant to be the same dream, the art contradicts itself. If they're different on purpose, nothing in the text tells me.
5. **Prologue generally:** the checkpoint question asks about "the woman in the dream," but the opening sequence shows no woman at all, and the only dream with a woman (image_07) never shows her face. Probably intentional; flagging that a reader can't identify her from art.
- **No mismatch:** image_07 and image_08 match their text very closely (cloth strip, petals, inside-wrist wound; one arm behind her back, forearm grip, one tear, bucket and rag, orange light).

## Would I keep reading?
Yes, definitely through the Saturday shrine scene.

**What hooked me:** the kendo material (specific, readable after Natsuki's lesson, and used as the language of the romance); a triangle where both girls are likable and each gets a third-person interlude that makes her lonely in her own way; the small grief details (Dad's handwriting, "I went with the handwriting," the off-center hakama knot); and the set-up collision of Nanoha's Saturday at the shrine with Natsuki's "That's my project, then."

**What would make me stop:**
- Nanoha turning into a jealous obstacle or a sad prop instead of a third lead.
- More unexplained tears and déjà vu without any payoff. I'll give the past-life thread about one more chapter of hints before I need a real answer.
- The family thread (Dad, Mom, Akane) staying as mood and never becoming plot.
- More translator's-note pile-ups like the one before the match.

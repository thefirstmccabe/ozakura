The Ōzakura is the likely link: the dream has a cherry branch overhead, and Natsuki has "been wanting to photograph that thing since Monday." I expect the Saturday shrine visit to trigger something. The fog/modern-sword prologue is still unexplained; my guess is it's a later, darker event (someone in the present holding the old sword).

**What's going on / what kind of story:** A reincarnation love triangle with a slow, grounded slice-of-life surface. Its real subject seems to be people who hold on to things: Kōhei to Dad's handwriting, Nanoha to a feeling she won't name, Natsuki to the only friend she ever kept and to photos as "proof I was there." The two girls' interludes mirror each other: each decides she likes the other, and each puts a feeling away ("the way you shut a window against the wind" / "She put it away to look at later, with everything else that wouldn't fit in a box"). That mirroring is the smartest thing in the build.

# Final answers

## When I became fairly sure (70%+) of anything
- **On the two "who" questions (ends up with / dream woman): never.** My peak was 60% (Natsuki for both, at part 7–8). The chapter keeps both girls genuinely live, which I think is a strength.
- **That this is a love triangle between Nanoha and Natsuki:** ~70% in part 2, at "Her grin stutters. Just for a second. Just long enough that I'm sure I'm the only one who sees it," coming right after "She went looking for mine before her own."
- **That the dream is a past-life memory connected to the present:** ~70% at the part 4 dream CG (image_07: kimono, cloth bandage, an adult man's arm), with "There's a dream I've had since I was small." Firmed to ~80% at part 7: "Her eyes are wide and confused, like she doesn't know where it came from either."
- **That Natsuki is connected to the dream (not necessarily *the* woman):** ~75% at part 7's tear, reinforced by the text "i knew what he was going to do before he did it. every time."

## Hints that felt heavy-handed, too obvious or too coy
- **Too obvious:** Natsuki crying without knowing why when he catches her. It's a well-worn reincarnation-romance beat; I thought of *Your Name* instantly. Her deflection saves the scene, but the hint itself is at the edge.
- **Slightly too direct:** "I feel like I've met him somewhere before. I just can't place it." That's the theme said out loud in the first chapter. "Then it's his face" softens it, but it might land better unspoken or half-said.
- **A bit neat:** "In bed I run my thumb over the callus on my left palm." → the dream. It ties the dream to Natsuki a little too tidily.
- **On the nose:** "Nanoha said it as lightly as she knew how. She'd had practice."
- **Too coy:** "That I kept *them* waiting far too long," after three lines of "She says it…". The pronoun switch is visible and draws attention to itself.
- **Too coy (structural):** the prologue (images 01–03: fog, a modern zip-up sleeve, a sword) is never touched again in two chapters. No fog, no callback. By the end of Chapter 2 I'd nearly forgotten it. One small echo (fog on the river, the same hilt somewhere) would keep it alive.
- **Well calibrated:** the wrist motif (dream cut → kote → "Kote. The wrist. Usually the right one." → his hand on her forearm in the catch) and the knot motif (Nanoha at his tie knot → the bandage knot → the apron "like a hakama"). Both are noticeable if you're looking and invisible if you aren't. The laugh-score echo (Nanoha's "two points" vs. Natsuki making him laugh "so hard I have to put the chair down") is also good.

## Dialogue that felt lively
- Nanoha's whole morning: "You thought it in a short-person direction." / "Rika-chan counts as several." / "His back is a very convenient back." / "I do supervise beautifully."

## Up to 8 flat lines, and why
1. **Natsuki: "Is the mountain always that close? From the station it looks like you could walk there."** / **Kōhei: "It's farther than it looks."** Generic small talk. The good material in that scene (herons on the bike racks, the drink fridge) is in narrated summary, while the spoken lines are the filler. Flip that.
2. **Natsuki: "I just mean it was a good match. I haven't had one like that in a long time."** Explains the subtext of the line before it; the narration then explains it again ("like a cup filled too full").
3. **Natsuki: "Is that the shrine? With the big tree?" / Kōhei: "That's the Ōzakura."** A title-drop by Q&A.
4. **Natsuki: "Allergies. It's allergies. Cedar pollen. Kanagawa's full of cedar, right?"** The first three beats are good; "Kanagawa's full of cedar, right?" sounds like it's there to tell the reader the prefecture.
5. **Natsuki: "I also love horror movies, the cheesier the better, so if anyone wants to watch a bad one with me, come find me."** Reads like a profile-card trait. It gets one "is this building haunted?" payoff and then vanishes.
6. **Nanoha: "Good. She noticed the squeaky stairs. Both of them."** Garbled (it's two steps on one staircase), and it's a weak setup for "Nobody notices the eleventh step."
7. **Kōhei: "I work Tuesdays and Thursdays. And Sundays."** The third time we've heard the schedule (whiteboard, then the walk to school, now this). Deliberate, maybe, but the beat is "he has no free time" and we have it.
8. **Natsuki: "Hey. Does Fujisawa ever compete up north? Around Sendai?"** A mechanical on-ramp to the "met him before" line.

## With the names covered, could I tell them apart? Who sounds like whom?
Mostly yes.
- **Easy:** Rika (exclamation chains, "Oh! Oh oh oh"), Akane ("Geez, Onii-chan!", "Yaaay"), the manager, Chie (caps, lowercase texting), Takatsuki (formal, no contractions: "It has been off center for a year." "Luck is not a technique.").
- **Distinct but overlapping:** Nanoha and Natsuki share a dry, deadpan one-liner register. "Late bloomers. They're real. Look it up." or "He said they don't check." could be Nanoha. Nanoha is more mock-formal ("Now then," "I've prepared a theory"); Natsuki is more direct and question-driven. Covered, I'd get them right maybe 75% of the time.
- **Shared tic:** the "Then + comic reframe" comeback is spread across four characters: Akane ("Then go hover over the eggs!"), Nanoha ("Then I've arrived."), Rika ("Then we've already narrowed it down!"), Natsuki ("Then I'm a spiritual first-year." / "Then one kids' plate, please."). That's the author's joke rhythm bleeding through. Give it to one character (Nanoha suits it best).
- **Same niche:** Ryōsuke and Takatsuki are both pedantic boys who correct people. Contractions and topic (food vs. kendo) separate them, but they occupy the same comic slot.
- **Kōhei vs. Kirishima:** both speak in very short, clipped lines. Kōhei's voice lives in the narration; his spoken lines alone wouldn't always identify him.

## Would I keep reading?
**Yes.** I'd start Chapter 3.

**What hooked me:**
- Both girls are likable and are becoming real friends, which makes the triangle hurt instead of compete. Their mirrored interludes are the best structure in the build.
- Saturday is set up beautifully: he promised Nanoha the shrine, Natsuki has made his Saturday her "project" and wants to photograph the Ōzakura, and he's telling himself "It's only trash bags."
- The grief layer: the fusuma that stays shut, Dad's handwriting on the whiteboard, "tonight I went with the handwriting."
- Natsuki's tear and the "every time… except the last one" text.
- The prose is clean and often funny (the manager count, the kids' plate, "the expression of a man who was born in a cardigan").

**What would make me stop:**
- If the dream simply *decides* the romance, i.e., "she was your past-life wife, so she's the one," and Nanoha turns into the gracious loser. The interesting question the book has raised is fate vs. the person who stayed; if it doesn't take that seriously, I'm out.
- If the translator's-note density of part 6 continues. Eleven notes in one part, six back to back before the match, many duplicating inline glosses, kills the pace.
- If the fog prologue keeps floating with no callback, or the family thread (Mom, Dad, Akane's illness) stays decorative.
- Pace: two chapters in, it's still all setup. Chapter 3 needs to move something (the shrine Saturday looks like the place).

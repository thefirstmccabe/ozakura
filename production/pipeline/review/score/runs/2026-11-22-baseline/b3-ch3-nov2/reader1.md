## Checkpoint 11 (after part 11, the Ōzakura)

**Who does Kōhei end up with?** Natsuki 50%, Nanoha 40%, other 10%. Nothing romantic moved here, but the supernatural beat happened while Kōhei and Natsuki were both at the branch, which reads like "the fated pair arrives at the tree." Nanoha is cast as the witness who sees it.

**Who is the woman in the dream?** Nanoha 45%, Natsuki 30%, someone else (the tree's spirit, or a past woman neither girl is) 25%.

# Final answers

### When I became fairly sure (70%+)
- **The dream is a past-life / old-era memory:** 70% at the end of Chapter 2, on "She didn't know why she'd cried. She didn't know how she'd known where he would move, either." It dipped to 55% when part 10 explained Natsuki's reading ("You lift your right heel before you feint. Every time."), then rose to 75% on "Under it, for a second, the air smells like snow. In April." and "…You came back."
- **Who he ends up with:** never reached 70%. My peak was Natsuki at 60%, right after the catch ("And she's crying.").
- **Who the dream woman is:** never reached 70%. My peaks were Natsuki at 50% (parts 7–8) and Nanoha at 45% (parts 9, 11–12). I swung back and forth, and I think the story intends that. But after three chapters I can't name even a 50% favorite, which may be more fog than the author wants.
- **Not asked, but certain early:** Nanoha loves him (part 3: "She did not let herself think about why it had needed deciding at all.") and he's falling for Natsuki (part 5: "I've been talking. For an hour... to someone I met yesterday.").

### Hints that felt heavy-handed, too obvious or too coy
**Heavy / too obvious:**
- "I've always been comfortable holding one. Since the first time I picked one up." This is on the nose as a past-life seed, especially said in reply to "What's the best part?"
- Natsuki's tear gets explained twice: in the scene ("like she doesn't know where it came from either") and again flatly in her interlude ("She didn't know why she'd cried. She didn't know how she'd known where he would move, either."). The texts to Chie already imply it, so cut one.
- Kōhei only laughs out loud with Natsuki (the chair at the restaurant, "I laugh before I can decide not to," "this time I can't stop laughing," the photo). It's a clear signal, but placed so regularly it starts to feel mechanical.
- "It's April" as the pollen rebuttal, repeated by Chie. The callback is fine, but see the problems list for the fact issue.

**Too coy:**
- **The opening fog sequence** (image_01–03: road mirror, a hand on a sword hilt in a modern zip jacket, fog rolling down a road) is never referenced again in three chapters. No label, no echo, no fog. I had forgotten it by Chapter 2. It's also unclear whether it's "the dream I've had since I was small," which turns out to be something else entirely (kimono, cherry tree). One later echo (fog on the river, the road mirror somewhere in town) would keep it alive.
- **"Just stopping for a rest." / "Looks like rain tomorrow."** Intriguing, but completely opaque. Readers who don't know the superstition about stumbling on shrine steps get nothing, and Kōhei "never once asked." That might be fine for now, but it's at the edge.
- **Mom disappears after part 1.** On the night of Akane's dizzy spell nobody mentions her: Akane texts Nanoha, Nanoha calls the clinic, Kōhei asks "Why didn't you call me?", and Mom is presumably home behind the fusuma. Not a hint, but a gap that reads as an oversight rather than a choice. One line from Kōhei would turn it into a strong beat.

**Well judged (keep):** "…You came back," "Thursday. After school. Before the window.", "I'm almost sure it's real," the hakama "off center for a year," and Natsuki lowering the camera without taking the shot.

### Lively dialogue
- Nanoha's theories and score-keeping: "It's not luck. It's paperwork." / "You thought it in a short-person direction." / "We've always been keeping score. You're losing."
- The faces catalogue: "That one's number four. 'Somebody wants to fight me.'" / "What's number one?" / "Secret." And later: "That one's new... I'll have to give it a number."
- The restaurant: "Do you check ID for the kids' plate?", the "Lifesaver!" counter, "…Wait. Do you work here?" / "I'm new."
- The whiteboard lesson: "Somebody hits you in the head, and the referee goes, 'There was a head.'" / "He's the 'before' picture!" / "You're doing great, visual aid."
- Takatsuki throughout, especially "I am telling you so that it is fair." Kirishima's "That was a joke." / "From memory."
- The window: "Your authority is a plushie." / "My authority is absolute." / "His sentence has been reduced. For good behavior."
- Akane: "I know. That's why I didn't call." / "A terrible doctor. Handsome, though."
- Mio: "Kagero is for cowards." / "Natsuki-nee, again!"

### Flat lines (6)
1. Natsuki's self-intro: "I do kendo, and I'm looking for a dojo that'll put up with me. I also love horror movies, the cheesier the better, so if anyone wants to watch a bad one with me, come find me." It reads like a character sheet, and the horror movies never come back in three chapters.
2. Nanoha: "They've checked everything. Twice. They couldn't find anything wrong. It isn't dangerous." Exposition for the reader, told to someone who already knows all of it.
3. Natsuki on the bridge: "I just mean it was a good match. I haven't had one like that in a long time." Generic, and the narration around it ("like a cup filled too full") does the real work.
4. Auntie Emi: "Barging? You're saving me. Come in, come in, there's karaage that needs eating." A stock TV mom. Emi has no voice of her own yet.
5. Genji: "And you're the young lady with the camera. Take as many as you like. The tree doesn't mind." A stock wise grandpa (though "The tree doesn't mind" may be a deliberate wink).
6. Kōhei reciting the schedule: "Monday, Wednesday, Friday, Saturday morning. Shifts Tuesday, Thursday, Sunday." Nanoha's "So you'd hear yourself say it" excuses it once, but it's the third time we get the schedule (whiteboard, the walk, "I work Tuesdays and Thursdays. And Sundays." on the bridge).

### With the names covered, could I tell them apart?
Mostly yes:
- **Natsuki:** breezy, bossy, and a physical noticer ("Hand me a rag." "Four hours." "Eh, 'san'?"). Very distinct.
- **Nanoha:** formal deadpan ("I've prepared a theory." "It's a sickness." "We don't discuss it."). Distinct.
- **Rika:** exclamation-point triplets. **Ishida:** pseudo-science. **Takatsuki:** no contractions, painfully fair. **Mio:** refers to herself in the third person. All instantly identifiable.
- **Akane sounds like Nanoha.** Without "Onii-chan," lines like "Then kneel somewhere else. I'm asleep." and "Only if you stop hovering." could be Nanoha's. They share the same dry, teasing, two-against-one register. The text even notices it ("It's always two against one in this house"), but Akane needs one more trait of her own.
- **Kōhei and Nanoha** share a clipped deadpan in banter ("You're already in." / "Paperwork."). Fine for old friends, but worth watching.
- **Kirishima vs. Takatsuki:** both terse, but Kirishima uses contractions and Takatsuki doesn't, so they stay separable.
- **Emi and Genji** are generic (see the flat lines).
- Content overlap: Nanoha and Natsuki BOTH catalogue Kōhei (faces vs. body tells). Their voices differ, but the "girl who reads him perfectly" function is duplicated. That may be the point of the triangle, but it blurs what each girl uniquely offers.

### Would I keep reading?
Yes. Honestly, more for the characters than the mystery.
- **What hooked me:** the window scene with Usagi-san; Nanoha's "…You came back" and the "almost sure it's real" smile; Natsuki's interlude ("I'm amazing at first days. It's the second semesters I never get to."); the grief details (Dad's handwriting on the trash day, "counting in months"); Takatsuki. The two girls genuinely liking each other is a refreshing change from the usual triangle.
- **What would make me stop:**
  - The supernatural staying this coy for another chapter. After three chapters I have one dream, one fog prologue that never comes back, one point of light and one smell of snow.
  - Nanoha becoming a pure self-sacrificing doormat. She has given Natsuki the tour, the broom and her family's lunch table while hiding her own feelings. One moment where it costs her on screen (alone, in her own POV) would keep her a person and not a martyr.
  - Translator's notes continuing to lecture mid-scene.
  - Natsuki's uncanniness being explained away piece by piece until there's nothing strange left.
- **Pacing:** three chapters cover six days. The character work carries it, but the plot proper only starts on the last page.

**What's going on / what kind of story:** Slice-of-life school romance on top of a family in quiet crisis: dead or absent father, mother withdrawn (depression?), little sister Akane with some chronic illness (dizziness, "the room starts tilting," clinic day on the 18th). ## Hints that felt heavy-handed, too obvious or too coy

**Heavy-handed / too obvious:**
1. The Natsuki interlude spells out the mystery after the scenes already showed it: "She didn't know why she'd cried. She didn't know how she'd known where he would move, either." Then the texts say it again: "i knew what he was going to do before he did it. every time." The tear scene and Takatsuki's "I have never once been there first" already made this clear. One statement too many.
2. The wrong-foot dō is flagged twice back to back: the panel description says "left foot forward (the wrong foot)," then Takatsuki says "Your left foot was forward on that dō." Pick one. Takatsuki's is better, because it reads as character.
3. Natsuki's "déjà vu" stack in Part 2–3 (grin stutter, plus "met him somewhere before," plus the question about Sendai) is fine individually but arrives fast. By lunch on day one the reader already has the whole premise.

**Too coy:**
4. The prologue (foggy modern crossroads, convex mirror, a hand in a modern zip jacket gripping a katana, fog rolling down the road like a wave) is never referenced again in three chapters. The later dream is a different visual world (kimono, cherry branch, river). I genuinely don't know if the prologue is "the dream," a different dream, the future, or something else. That's more confusing than intriguing at this point.
5. Mom. She gets two beats in Part 1 (the closed fusuma, the creak) and then vanishes from Chapters 2 and 3 entirely. With Auntie Emi saying "Kōhei-kun, welcome home!" the contrast is powerful, but only if the story touches Mom again. Three chapters is a long time for a closed door.
6. Nanoha's "…You came back." I liked it, but the referent is so ambiguous (the light? Kōhei? someone else?) that it doesn't push my guesses in any direction. It's coy in a "this will matter later" way rather than in a "here's a clue" way.

**Calibrated well (for contrast):** the drawer that sticks, the dad's handwriting on the whiteboard, the hakama "off center for a year," "Just stopping for a rest" / "Looks like rain tomorrow," the snow smell under the branch, Natsuki not taking the shot.

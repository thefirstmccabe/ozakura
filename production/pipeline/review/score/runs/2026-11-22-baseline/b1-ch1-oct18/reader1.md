# Reader 1 notes: Chapter 1, "The House Next Door"

## Checkpoint 1 (after part 1)

**Who I think Kōhei ends up with:** Nanoha, 45%. She's the only girl in the running so far, and the scene is built entirely around her. But the chapter title, "The House Next Door," makes me suspect a new girl is about to move in next door, and in this genre the girl who arrives in chapter 1 is often the main route. So: Nanoha 45%, an unseen new arrival 40%, someone else 15%.

**Who I want him to end up with:** Nanoha, fairly strongly (7/10). The morning routine is warm and funny. She knows the kitchen better than he does, and the collar moment works. She's the one taking care of *him* while he takes care of everyone else.

**Who I think the woman in the dream is:** I didn't see a woman. The opening is four wordless images: a foggy rural crossroads with a curved traffic mirror, a close-up of a hand holding a sheathed sword, fog rolling down a road like a wave, then the crossroads again. Nothing told me it was a dream, either. It's just a cold open before the title card. If there's a woman in it, she isn't visible yet. The hand on the sword is the only person shown, and I read it as a man's hand (big knuckles, plain dark zip jacket). If I'm forced to guess who the "dream woman" is: no idea, 5% on anyone. Maybe the hand belongs to her, maybe it's Kōhei's. I'd put maybe 15% on Nanoha just because she's the only girl, and 10% on the mother.

**What the dream has to do with the present day:**
- Sword means kendo, so the hand could be Kōhei's (he does kendo four days a week).
- The curved mirror at a blind corner in fog made me think *car accident*. Dad's handwriting "from last spring" that he's "never written over," Mom shut in her room, and Kōhei doing all the parenting add up to Dad dying (or leaving) a year ago. My working guess: Dad died at that foggy crossroads, and the cold open is Kōhei's memory or nightmare of it. 35% confident.
- The mountains with mist in the collar illustration match the cold open's mist, so the dream may be set in this same town (Shirasawa).

**What I think is going on / what kind of story:** A slice-of-life school romance with a grief undercurrent. Dad gone, Mom depressed and barely responsive, the little sister with some recurring illness (dizziness, "clinic day" circled in red), and Kōhei as the over-responsible older brother. The giant old shrine cherry tree ("the Ōzakura," older than anyone's records) is set up like a supernatural anchor. I'd bet there's a legend about it, and Nanoha's grandpa seems to be the shrine's caretaker. My guess is it's a mostly grounded romance with one mystical thread tied to the tree.

**Lines and images that drove my guesses:**
- "All of it except the bottom line, which still says 'Garbage: burnables, Mon/Thu' in Dad's. He wrote it last spring. I've never written over it." This is the best line in the part. It tells me Dad is gone without saying so.
- "The fusuma to Mom's room is closed. It usually is." / "That's an answer. Sort of."
- "She isn't holding her head the way she does when the room starts tilting on her." So Akane is sick, maybe something serious.
- Akane: "…You always are." "She says it to the rice cooker, not to me." Nice. Akane worries about her brother as much as he worries about her.
- Nanoha's kitchen choreography (mugs cupboard, bumping the drawer, the window latch). This is the strongest "she belongs here" signal.
- "Her hands stay a second longer than the collar strictly needs." This is the romance flag for Nanoha.
- "So you'd hear yourself say it." She's worried he's overloaded.
- "Saturday afternoons are free." / "Mm." / she invites him to the shrine Saturday / "She walks a little faster after that." I read this as her engineering a date and being pleased about it. It works.
- Chapter title "The House Next Door." Either Nanoha's house or someone new. I'm watching for a new neighbor.

**Small reactions so far:**
- The banter is genuinely good. "You thought it in a short-person direction." "That's what it says in the minutes." "The pool is green."
- Akane's "Geez, Onii-chan! Every single morning!" reads a bit stock little-sister, but it's fine.
- Collar illustration vs text: the text says she's "up on her toes, folding my shirt collar back down over my tie." The picture shows her hands at his tie knot, not his collar, and her head reaching about his chin. That's shorter, but not as dramatically short as the "short-person" jokes made me picture. The petal on her braid is there, which is a nice match. The text also says "Nanoha walks on the river side," but in the picture he's the one nearer the river. That's minor, since they've stopped and turned to face each other.

## Checkpoint 2 (after part 2)

**Who I think Kōhei ends up with:** Now it's a two-horse race, childhood friend vs. transfer student, the oldest setup in the genre. Natsuki 50%, Nanoha 40%, other 10%. I moved toward Natsuki for three reasons. She gets the dramatic entrance and two full-screen illustrations. Something passes between them that nobody else sees. And Kōhei has a physical reaction to her before they've exchanged a word ("holding my pen hard enough to hurt"). The genre's usual rule is that the childhood friend loses, so my head says Natsuki even though my heart says Nanoha.

**Who I want him to end up with:** Still Nanoha (6/10, down from 7). Natsuki is instantly more interesting as a *mystery*, but Nanoha is more interesting as a *person*. Finding his name on the board before her own, and lying about it, is lovely.

**Who I think the woman in the dream is:** Natsuki, 40%. She does kendo, the opening showed a hand on a sword, and she clearly recognized Kōhei (or someone who looks like him). Second choice is still "someone we haven't met" at 30%. Nanoha 15%, Mom 10%, other 5%. Note: I still haven't actually *seen* a woman in the dream, so I'm assigning the hand to her on genre logic alone.

**What the dream has to do with the present day:** New theory. Natsuki has met Kōhei before, or dreamed of him. The sword in the opening is a real katana in a scabbard, with a guard and wrapped hilt, not a bamboo practice sword. So the opening might be less "Dad's accident" and more "something supernatural or fated, tied to swords and fog." The giant ancient cherry tree plus a sword plus fog could point to a past-life or reincarnation angle, but the opening had power lines and a modern traffic mirror, so the dream is set in the present day, or in this town. I'm dropping the car-accident theory to 20%.

**What I think is going on / what kind of story:** Standard harem-route setup, executed well. The childhood friend who's quietly in love, the bubbly best friend who knows it ("Hm. Hmmm."), the pedantic guy friend, the cool transfer student with a secret. The extra layer is that Natsuki's "grin stutters" when she sees him. That's the hook for the mystery. My guess is she either knew him as a kid, has seen him in a dream, or he looks like someone she lost. "The House Next Door" now makes me think Natsuki is moving into the house next door. That would be a very typical twist for later this chapter.

**Lines and images that drove my guesses:**
- "Her grin stutters. Just for a second. Just long enough that I'm sure I'm the only one who sees it." This is the key line of the part.
- "I realize I've been holding my pen hard enough to hurt." This is ambiguous in a good way. Is that attraction, or recognition?
- "I raise my left hand." → "Left palm. You've got the callus right where the tsuka (hilt) sits." Clever. The teacher's instruction plants exactly the hand she reads. But it also made me wonder whether the callus line is a *cover*: she stuttered at his face first, then gave a normal-sounding reason to talk to him.
- "Takes one to know one." Sets them up as matched rivals or equals.
- "When I finally get to the board, Fukuda's not in Two-B this year. He's in Two-E. She went looking for mine before her own." / "I don't know why that's the thing that sticks with me." Nanoha's feelings are showing, and he half-notices. Good restraint.
- Rika: "And you walked in with Fujisawa-kun. Hm. Hmmm." Friends see Nanoha's crush.

**Small reactions:**
- Ryōsuke/Tokiwa's taiyaki thermodynamics and Rika's "Then we've already narrowed it down!" are both fun. The ensemble feels alive.
- "the expression of a man who was born in a cardigan." Good.
- Mild speed bump: the name tag says "Ryōsuke," but the narration only ever calls him "Tokiwa." I worked out they're the same person, but for a beat I thought two people had arrived. Same thing, to a lesser degree, with "Fujisawa" for Kōhei, though that one was easier because of "the Fujisawa kitchen."
- Natsuki's self-intro (kendo, horror movies, "the cheesier the better") is a little character-sheet-ish, but class self-intros in Japan really do sound like that, so it's forgivable. "So please don't go easy on me" is a nice personality tell.
- **Illustration vs text:** the illustrations match her description well (copper hair, shoulder length, tucked behind one ear). But in the "grin stutters" picture (image_06), her eyes slide off toward the right side of the room. The narration has her looking at Kōhei, who sits by the window, and the window is on the *left* of the frame. So the picture reads as her being startled by someone or something on the far side of the room, not by the viewer/Kōhei. For the most important beat of the part, the eyeline should point at the camera or toward the window side.

## Checkpoint 3 (after part 3)

**Who I think Kōhei ends up with:** Natsuki 50%, Nanoha 45%, other 5%. Nanoha gained ground because the story handed her the only interior POV so far, a whole third-person interlude. That tells me she's a lead, not a side character. But that interlude is also full of classic "childhood friend who will get hurt" signals ("It was fine. / It was fine."). So the text is telling me that Nanoha *loves* him and Natsuki is *fated* to him. In this genre the fated one usually wins, at least on the main route.

**Who I want him to end up with:** Nanoha, 7/10, back up. The interlude made me root for her. Natsuki is likable too, especially the small, costly smile at the end. Honestly I'm now a little worried for both of them, which is a good sign for the writing.

**Who I think the woman in the dream is:** Natsuki, 55%. "I feel like I've met him somewhere before. I just can't place it." plus the stutter in part 2 plus kendo plus the sword image. Another small clue I may be over-reading: she sees the mountains from the bathroom window and says "Oh," softly, "like she'd been handed something." The dream was misty mountains. Maybe she's dreamed this landscape. Someone not yet introduced: 25%. Nanoha 10%, Mom 5%, other 5%.

**What the dream has to do with the present day:** I now think the dream (or the opening) is a shared or prophetic dream connecting Kōhei and Natsuki. Either they met once as small children, or something spooky links them, possibly through the old cherry tree. Natsuki's "Does Fujisawa ever compete up north? Around Sendai?" gives the mundane explanation (they crossed paths at a tournament), which the story will presumably rule out. I still haven't seen a *woman* in the opening images, so I'm partly guessing that the "dream" is even the opening sequence.

**What I think is going on / what kind of story:** A love triangle where the two girls are going to become real friends first, which is the more painful and more interesting version. The interlude ending ("She'd decided it firmly, the way you shut a window against the wind. / She did not let herself think about why it had needed deciding at all.") tells me the story is going to be about Nanoha's feelings as much as Kōhei's. And Kōhei's "I don't have time" for girlfriends is set up as the obstacle. His caretaker load at home is the real reason, and I expect a route to be about someone getting him to put it down.

**Lines and images that drove my guesses:**

## Checkpoint 4 (after part 4, end of chapter)

**Who I think Kōhei ends up with:** Nanoha 50%, Natsuki 45%, other 5%. The chapter *ends* on Nanoha: her catalogue of his faces, "Night, Chibi," her window lighting up across a gap "you could pass a book through." Then "I stand there for a while longer than the garbage requires," which mirrors her hands lingering on his collar in part 1. That pulled me back toward her. Natsuki still has the fate and mystery signals. I genuinely can't call it, which for chapter 1 I think is right.

**Who I want him to end up with:** Nanoha, 8/10. The trash scene sealed it. "You have several. I've catalogued them. That one's number four." / "What's number one?" / "Secret." That exchange is the most charming thing in the chapter.

**Who I think the woman in the dream is:** Nanoha 45%, Natsuki 35%, someone else (a woman from the past who isn't either girl, or something tied to the old tree) 20%. This flipped from checkpoint 3. Why:
- For Nanoha: "She says it like a scolding. She says it like she's laughing." That is exactly Nanoha's register ("You're taking out the trash on the wrong day."). The hands carefully tying a knot at his wrist ("like the knot matters more than anything else in the world") visually rhymes with Nanoha's hands at his collar and tie knot in the riverside illustration. "She says it like she's been waiting a very long time" fits the girl who has "met him before. Every day. For sixteen years." Cherry branch, river close by, petals: that's Nanoha's setting (the river path, her grandpa's shrine and the Ōzakura).
- For Natsuki: she's the one who said "I feel like I've met him somewhere before," and she's the swordswoman. Also, she wrote 水 on the inside of her left wrist, and the dream cut is on the inside of the dreamer's wrist (the left one, judging by the illustration's point of view).
- The catch: the wrist clue ties Natsuki to the *wounded swordsman* (Kōhei's position in the dream), not to the woman doing the bandaging. If that's meant to point at Natsuki as the woman, it misfires for me. If it's meant to say Natsuki is somehow a swordsman figure (a rival), then it's intriguing.

**What the dream has to do with the present day:** I'm fairly sure now that it's a past-life or reincarnation thread. The final illustration shows a woman in a striped period kimono with an obi binding a man's bleeding wrist under a cherry branch, with a katana hilt in the corner. The hilt matches the one in the cold open. So Kōhei is (or was) the swordsman, someone waited for him "a very long time," and "I kept them waiting far too long" suggests he failed to come back. In the present, someone is waiting for him again. The obvious reading is Nanoha (sixteen years of waiting while he says he "doesn't have time"). The plot reading is Natsuki (recognition, swords). My guess is the Ōzakura tree on the hill is where it all happened.

Still unexplained: the cold open. It's set in the *present* (power lines, a convex traffic mirror, a hand in a modern zip-up jacket holding that same katana, fog rolling down a road). If that's also the dream, why is it modern? If it's someone else's vision (Natsuki's?) or a flash-forward, nothing tells me. I came out of the chapter not knowing what the first four images were.

**What I think is going on / what kind of story:** A reincarnation romance hidden inside a grounded slice-of-life about a boy holding a grieving family together. Dad gone, Mom shut in her room, Akane ill, Kōhei with no time for himself. Two heroines: the childhood friend who has waited all her life, and the transfer student who "recognizes" him. The real question is whether the past-life bond belongs to the one who was there all along or the one who just arrived. The grief and caretaking material is the strongest part. I'd keep reading for that even if the supernatural thread flopped.

**Lines and images that drove my guesses:**
- "There's a dream I've had since I was small." / "Tonight, it comes back." This signals that Natsuki's arrival triggered it, which counts for Natsuki.
- "???: 'You're late.'" / "She says it like she's been waiting a very long time." This counts for Nanoha.
- "Hands that aren't mine wind a strip of cloth around a wrist that is." The best sentence in the chapter.

- "Dad's handwriting on the whiteboard still says Monday, and tonight I went with the handwriting." This pays off part 1 beautifully.

# Final answers

## When I became fairly sure (70%+)
- **That Dad is gone (dead or left):** part 1, "He wrote it last spring. I've never written over it." (about 85%; nothing ever confirms which.)
- **That Nanoha is in love with Kōhei:** part 1, "Her hands stay a second longer than the collar strictly needs." (about 80%), then near-certain at "She did not let herself think about why it had needed deciding at all."
- **That I want him with Nanoha:** part 1, at the collar scene; locked in at "That one's number four. 'Somebody wants to fight me.'"
- **That Natsuki has some prior connection to Kōhei:** part 3, "I feel like I've met him somewhere before. I just can't place it." (about 80%.)
- **That the dream is a past life tied to swords and the cherry tree:** part 4, image_07 together with "There's a dream I've had since I was small." (about 75%.)
- **Never reached 70%** on who he ends up with (peaked at Natsuki 50%, ended at Nanoha 50%) or on who the dream woman is (peaked at Natsuki 55%, ended at Nanoha 45%). For a first chapter I think that's the right amount of uncertainty.

## Hints that felt heavy-handed, too obvious, or too coy
- **Too obvious:** Natsuki's "I feel like I've met him somewhere before. I just can't place it." It's a stock line, and it repeats what the "grin stutters" moment already did well. One of the two is enough. The stutter is the better one.
- **Too neat:** Natsuki choosing, out of nowhere, "Someone who knows which drawer sticks in your kitchen," the exact detail planted in the morning scene. It lands, but I felt the author's hand.
- **Slightly over-explained:** the dream ending states the "waiting" idea twice, first "She says it like she's been waiting a very long time," then "That someone was waiting for me. / That I kept them waiting far too long." The second pair would hit harder if the first were trimmed.
- **Too coy:** the cold open. Four wordless images, no woman, not marked as a dream, and set in the present day, while the actual dream at the end is a period scene. I couldn't connect them. If the opening is meant to be the same recurring dream, it doesn't read that way. If it's something else, I don't know whose it is.
- **Mixed signal:** 水 written on the inside of Natsuki's left wrist vs. the cut on the inside of the dreamer's wrist. It ties her to the wounded swordsman, not to the woman. Fine if intended; confusing if it's meant to point at her as the dream woman.
- **Tacked on:** the haunted music room ("Something in the music room was knocking." / "The pipes sound like a ghost."). It reads as a horror-hobby callback with no payoff in this chapter.
- **Pitched right:** the Nanoha-in-love signals (collar, finding his name first, "It was fine. / It was fine.", number one is "Secret"). Clear without anyone saying it.

## Flat lines (8)
1. Akane: "Geez, Onii-chan! Every single morning!" Stock little-sister line. It could come from any VN.
2. Akane: "Yaaay." Same problem. Her next line ("…You always are.") is great, so she doesn't need the generic cuteness.
3. Natsuki: "I do kendo, and I'm looking for a dojo that'll put up with me. I also love horror movies, the cheesier the better, so if anyone wants to watch a bad one with me, come find me." A character-sheet dump. Realistic for a class self-intro, but it reads like the author listing traits.
4. Natsuki: "Looking forward to this year." It's meant to be a recovery line after the stutter, but on the page it's just filler. Something with a crack in it would sell the recovery.
5. Natsuki: "Serious question. Is this building haunted? Something in the music room was knocking." Comes out of nowhere and goes nowhere.
6. Nanoha: "It's old. The pipes sound like a ghost." Flat answer to a flat setup.
7. Natsuki: "Ha— okay. Then it's his face. He's got kind of a normal one, right? Don't tell him I said that." The joke doesn't land in English. "Kind of a normal one" reads oddly. I think it means "a generic face," but I had to stop and work it out.
8. Nanoha: "Oh. Grandpa says the shrine's cherries are the best they've been in years. I'm helping him sweep up there Saturday." It's exposition wearing a bow. It works as her angling for Saturday, but the first sentence is there to plant the shrine for the reader.

## Names covered: could I tell them apart?
Mostly yes.

- **Ryōsuke:** unmistakable (lectures, "That isn't an opinion").
- **Akane:** distinct ("Onii-chan," "Geez"), but generic.

## Would I keep reading?
Yes. **What hooked me:**
- The quiet grief at home. The whiteboard handwriting, and "I went with the handwriting," is the best writing in the chapter.
- Nanoha's interlude.
- The grin stutter.
- The dream's last image.
- The ensemble banter, which is genuinely funny.

**What would make me stop:**
- If the past-life thread stays this coy for several chapters without one concrete reveal.
- If Akane's illness turns into a melodrama countdown.
- If Nanoha gets reduced to the loyal friend who loses to the fated girl. I'd feel cheated after this chapter invested so much in her.
- If the cold open never gets explained.

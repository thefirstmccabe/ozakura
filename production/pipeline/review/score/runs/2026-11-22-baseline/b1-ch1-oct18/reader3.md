# Reader 3: cold read, Chapter 1 "The House Next Door"

## Checkpoint 1 (after part 1)

**Who I think Kōhei ends up with:** Nanoha, 45%. She's the only girl on the board so far, and she's written with a lot of care. But I've read too many of these: the childhood friend who knows which cupboard holds the mugs is usually the one who *doesn't* win. The title "The House Next Door" plus "Up on the hill… the Ōzakura" sets off my new-arrival alarm. I'm holding the other 55% for a heroine we haven't met yet (a transfer student, a new neighbour, or someone tied to the shrine tree).

**Who I want him to end up with:** Nanoha, 70%. She's funny, she pays attention, and she isn't helpless. "You thought it in a short-person direction" won me over. The 30% I'm holding back is only because I haven't seen her want anything for herself yet.

**The woman in the dream:** I didn't see a woman. The opening dream is four images: a foggy country road with a curve mirror, a hand in a dark zip jacket gripping a katana, a wall of fog rolling down the road like a wave, then the first road again. The only person in it is the sword hand, and it reads as male to me (big knuckles, broad wrist). If there's supposed to be a woman, she isn't on screen. If I have to name someone: the spirit or "lady" of the Ōzakura, or Nanoha (shrine grandfather), at about 10%. That's a pure guess.

**What the dream has to do with the present:**
- The sword ties to Kōhei's kendo.
- The misty mountains match Shirasawa ("the wind comes down off the mountains", "the Tanzawa").
- The curve mirror at a blind junction makes me think of a road accident, and Dad stopped existing "last spring". My working theory is that the dream is about whatever happened to Dad, or a warning of something on that road.
- The katana is a real sword, not a shinai, so there's a supernatural or "fated" layer too, probably tied to the shrine and the Ōzakura.

**What I think is going on / what kind of story:** A slice-of-life romance about a young carer, with a supernatural or fate thread underneath. Kōhei runs the house: Dad is gone (dead or left, about a year ago), Mom hasn't come out of her room in a long time, and Akane has a recurring dizzy/fainting illness with clinic visits. Nanoha has quietly propped the family up for a year. This chapter feels like the "normal before the inciting incident" morning. I expect Saturday at the shrine to be where the plot starts.

**Lines and images that drove this:**
- "All of it except the bottom line, which still says 'Garbage: burnables, Mon/Thu' in Dad's. He wrote it last spring. I've never written over it." The best line in the part. It quietly tells me Dad is gone and Kōhei hasn't processed it.
- "The fusuma to Mom's room is closed. It usually is." / "That's an answer. Sort of." This is Mom's withdrawal or depression.
- "She isn't holding her head the way she does when the room starts tilting on her." / "A red circle around the 18, Akane's clinic day." Akane is ill, probably with something that becomes a plot point.
- "Akane: '…You always are.' She says it to the rice cooker." The sister sees he's carrying too much.
- "Nanoha: 'So you'd hear yourself say it.'" Nanoha is worried he's overloaded. That's the emotional core of the route.
- "Her hands stay a second longer than the collar strictly needs." and "She walks a little faster after that." These are her feelings, stated clearly enough that I'm sure she likes him. Kōhei seems oblivious, or careful not to notice.
- "one cherry tree older than anyone's records". It's the title. It will matter.
- image_04 (collar CG): cute, warm, petal on the braid as described.

**Problems noticed so far:**
1. **Dream / prompt mismatch.** The opening dream shows no woman, only a sword hand that reads as male. If a "woman in the dream" is meant to register, nothing on screen gives her to me.
2. **image_04 vs text:** the text says she's "folding my shirt collar back down over my tie" and is "up on her toes". The art shows her straightening the **tie knot**, flat-footed as far as I can tell, with her head at about his chin. She doesn't look like she needs the extra inches the text jokes about.
3. **image_04 vs text:** "Nanoha walks on the river side," but in the CG Kōhei is on the river side and she's on the inland side.
4. **The dream sequence isn't framed.** It runs straight into the title card with no "I dreamed…" line and no wake-up beat. Then he wakes to his alarm and never mentions it. Either that's deliberate restraint or the bridge got dropped. As a reader I wanted one line ("The fog again.") to tell me it was his dream and whether it recurs.
5. **Mild:** Akane's lines ("Geez, Onii-chan! Every single morning!", "Yaaay.") are stock imouto. They're fine as a first impression, but she could be anyone's little sister. Her one good line is "…You always are."

## Checkpoint 2 (after part 2)

**Who I think Kōhei ends up with:** Natsuki 50%, Nanoha 40%, someone else 10%. The transfer student arrived exactly on cue. She does kendo, she has some kind of prior connection (her grin stutters when she sees *him*), and she's seated directly behind him by the window. That's the protagonist seat and the main-heroine seat. Meanwhile Nanoha just got her most devoted-childhood-friend beat ("She went looking for mine before her own"), which in this genre is as much a losing flag as a winning one. I still half-expect Natsuki to turn out to be the "house next door" of the title.

**Who I want him to end up with:** Nanoha, 60%. Natsuki is fun ("the cheesier the better", "Takes one to know one") and I'd happily read her route. But Nanoha has done a year of unglamorous caring, and I want the story to pay that off rather than reward the shiny new arrival.

**The woman in the dream:** Natsuki, 40%. The dream still shows no woman, but the sword now points at her: kendo, her first move is reading the callus on his **left** palm, and the dream figure appears to be gripping the katana in his/her **left** hand (the jacket front faces us, so the arm on the right of the frame is the figure's left). Her stutter of recognition makes me think she's seen him before: in the same dream, in a past life, or in a childhood we haven't been told about. The other 60%: the Ōzakura's spirit or someone from the past (20%), Nanoha (10%), "it's Kōhei's own hand and there's no woman at all" (30%).

**What the dream has to do with the present:** It's now very likely a shared-dream or fated-meeting device. Kōhei dreams of fog and a sword, and the next morning a swordswoman who seems to know him walks in. The fog-and-road imagery may still point to Dad's accident or a future event, but the sword is now plainly tied to Natsuki and kendo.

**What I think is going on / what kind of story:** A love triangle with a fate element. Childhood friend vs. mysterious transfer student, with the Ōzakura and the shrine (Nanoha's grandfather's) as the supernatural anchor. I expect the Saturday shrine sweep to be where the three of them collide, and Natsuki to turn up at Kōhei's kendo club on Wednesday.

**Lines and images that drove this:**
- "Her grin stutters. Just for a second. Just long enough that I'm sure I'm the only one who sees it." (with image_06): she recognizes him.
- "I realize I've been holding my pen hard enough to hurt." Kōhei is shaken too, and I don't know why. Is it recognition, attraction or dread? It's nicely coy, but see problem 8.
- "Left palm. You've got the callus right where the tsuka (hilt) sits." This ties to the sword hand in the dream.
- "Nanoha has already slipped through somewhere at elbow height…" / "When I finally get to the board, Fukuda's not in Two-B this year. He's in Two-E." A neat little exposed white lie.
- Rika: "And you walked in with Fujisawa-kun. Hm. Hmmm." The friend character who teases about the ship is a confirmation tag that Nanoha → Kōhei is visible to everyone except Kōhei.

**Problems noticed in this part:**
6. **image_06 eye line.** The beat is "her eyes… get to me", and Kōhei sits "second-to-last row by the window". In the CG the window is on the left of the frame, but her eyes slide to the **right**, away from the window side. The camera also sits behind front-row students, not near the back row. It reads as her glancing at someone else.
7. **Over-explained subtext:** "She went looking for mine before her own." The Fukuda detail had already landed. Spelling it out, then adding "I don't know why that's the thing that sticks with me", is the genre's dense-protagonist cliché twice in a row. Cut one of the two lines.
8. **Kōhei's reaction to Natsuki is unanchored.** The pen-gripping is strong, but nothing in his narration says whether he recognizes her, is attracted, or is reacting to the fog dream. One concrete sensory hook (copper hair → something from the dream? a smell? the fog?) would make it a clue instead of noise. As it stands I can't tell whether *he* knows her.
9. **Art-style drift:** image_05/06 is noticeably glossier and more realistic, with elongated legs and a lower-angle framing that leans fan-service, than the soft watercolor of image_04. The two heroines look like they're from different artists.
10. **Inline gloss in speech:** "the tsuka (hilt)" is a translator's parenthesis inside spoken dialogue, which jars. Use "hilt" or "grip", or put the gloss in a TL note.

## Checkpoint 3 (after part 3)

**Who I think Kōhei ends up with:** Natsuki 55%, Nanoha 40%, other 5%. The Nanoha interlude is beautifully done, and that's exactly what worries me. Giving the childhood friend a quiet third-person chapter where she smiles through it, decides to like her rival, and tells herself "It was fine" is the classic losing-heroine move (Blue Box's Hina, Toradora's Minori). Natsuki also now says out loud that she feels she's met him. Fate plus the protagonist's own sport plus the seat behind him all point the same way. I'm not higher than 55% because the story is spending a lot of real feeling on Nanoha, and it might be setting up a subversion.

**Who I want him to end up with:** Nanoha, 65%. The interlude moved me further toward her. Natsuki gained sympathy too ("a different smile… It looked like it cost her something"), so I'd now be fine with her as well. But "She did not let herself think about why it had needed deciding at all" is the line of the chapter for me, and I want that girl to get something.

**The woman in the dream:** Natsuki, 50%. "I feel like I've met him somewhere before. I just can't place it" plus the sword makes her the obvious candidate. She offers a mundane explanation herself (a tournament "up north, around Sendai?"), which in this genre almost guarantees the real answer isn't mundane. Remaining: a figure tied to the Ōzakura or the past (20%), no woman at all and the hand is Kōhei's (20%), Nanoha (10%).

**What the dream has to do with the present:** I now think it's either a shared dream or a memory from a previous meeting, possibly a previous life, that both Kōhei and Natsuki carry. Kōhei's tight grip on his pen, plus Natsuki's stutter and "I've met him somewhere", suggests both of them feel it and neither can name it. The fog-on-the-road imagery still feels like it's about a specific event, maybe the one that took Dad, but I have nothing new on that.

**What I think is going on / what kind of story:** A three-way romance with genuine affection between the two girls, which raises the cost: Nanoha chose to befriend the rival before she let herself feel threatened. Kōhei's real obstacle is now stated: "I don't have time" (three confessions turned down). The arc is about him letting someone carry part of the load. That fits Nanoha better than Natsuki, and it's why my "want" and my "think" are pulling in different directions.

**Lines and images that drove this:**
- "Natsuki: 'I feel like I've met him somewhere before. I just can't place it.'"
- "Nanoha: 'I have met him before. Every day. For sixteen years.'" The best line in the part. It's funny, sad and territorial at once.
- "She'd decided it firmly, the way you shut a window against the wind. / She did not let herself think about why it had needed deciding at all." Jealousy, suppressed. (I did briefly wonder whether it hints Nanoha is drawn to Natsuki herself. I don't think that's intended, but the line is open enough to allow it.)
- "Nanoha's standing by my desk with her lunch held in front of her in both hands, the way she holds things when she's about to do something brave." I love this. It's the only time Kōhei shows he's been watching her closely.
- "It's true that I don't have time." / "I eat my rice. It's good rice. I made it myself." Kōhei's whole problem in two lines.
- "writes 水 (Wednesday) on the inside of her wrist". Natsuki commits to him (or to sparring him) physically and privately. A nice beat.

**Problems noticed in this part:**
11. **The drawer echo is too neat.** Natsuki happens to say "Someone who knows which drawer sticks in your kitchen", the exact detail from part 1's kitchen. Then the narration underlines it ("Nanoha didn't say that Kōhei's kitchen drawer stuck. She didn't say she'd known it since she was six."). One coincidence plus one underline reads as the author's hand. Make Natsuki's example something generic (which step creaks, where the spare key is) and let Nanoha's silence do the work, or keep the drawer and cut the explanation.
12. **The inline-gloss pattern repeats** (see #10): "shodan (first dan)" in speech and "水 (Wednesday)" in narration. Three in one chapter makes it a house style, and it pulls me out of the voice every time. (水 also literally means "water". A reader who knows that gets it; one who doesn't may think 水 *means* Wednesday.)
13. **Kōhei's "three confessions" reputation** arrives as gossip with no setup. Nothing in parts 1–2 suggested he was someone girls confess to, and nobody in the entrance-hall scene reacts to him that way. Fine as a reveal, but it nudges him toward the "secretly popular dense hero" template. One glance or whisper from a classmate in part 2 would make it feel earned rather than convenient.

## Checkpoint 4 (after part 4, end of chapter)

**Who I think Kōhei ends up with:** Nanoha 50%, Natsuki 45%, other 5%. The last part swung me back. "Back from walking Mizuno home to her place by the station" kills my theory that Natsuki is the house next door, so the chapter title belongs to Nanoha. The chapter then ends on her house: the windows "you could pass a book through", Kōhei lingering "longer than the garbage requires", and the "number one" face she won't name. But the dream gives Natsuki the destiny card, and genre instinct still tells me destiny usually beats the girl next door. It's close to a coin flip, which I suspect is the intent.

**Who I want him to end up with:** Nanoha, 70%. The face catalogue did it: "You have several. I've catalogued them. That one's number four." She has paid closer attention to him than anyone in the story, and he's only starting to notice.

**The woman in the dream:** Natsuki 45%, Nanoha 40%, someone else (a woman from the past tied to the Ōzakura, whom neither girl simply "is") 15%. Two sets of clues pull in opposite directions, and I think that's deliberate:
- **For Natsuki:** the wound is on the **inside of his left wrist**. Natsuki wrote 水 "on the inside of her wrist, small, just under her cuff", on her **left** hand. She read the callus on his **left** palm, and he rubs that callus in bed right before the dream. She's the one who said "I feel like I've met him somewhere before." She's tied to swords.
- **For Nanoha:** everything about how the woman *behaves*. "You're late." "Hurt again. Give me your hand… Hold still." "She says it like she's been waiting a very long time." Tending him, scolding him, waiting for him: that's Nanoha's whole year, in one gesture. It also echoes "Her hands stay a second longer than the collar strictly needs." The setting (cherry branch overhead, river close by) is the riverside and the Ōzakura, which is her grandfather's shrine.

**What the dream has to do with the present:** I now read it as a past-life memory. There's a kimono, a real sword, a swordsman's weathered adult arm, and a cherry tree that's almost certainly the Ōzakura. The present-day triangle is replaying or completing it, and the theme is stated outright: "That I kept them waiting far too long." The **opening** fog sequence now seems to be something different: a modern road, a modern zip jacket, a real katana, fog rolling in like a wave. I'd guess it's a flash-forward to a present-day supernatural threat Kōhei will face with a sword. So I think the story has two supernatural layers: the past-life love (Ōzakura) and something coming down off the mountains (the fog).

**What I think is going on / what kind of story:** A grief-and-caregiving romance (dead or absent father, withdrawn mother, ill sister, a boy who "doesn't have time") with a reincarnation/fate frame, probably heading toward a supernatural threat. The central question looks like "the person who has been waiting all along vs. the person fate sent back", with both girls likeable and already friends. If it handles that honestly, it could be very good.

**Lines and images that drove this:**
- "back from walking Mizuno home to her place by the station." The title is Nanoha's.
- "Nanoha: 'You have several. I've catalogued them. That one's number four. "Somebody wants to fight me."' … 'What's number one?' / 'Secret.'"
- "I stand there for a while longer than the garbage requires." This mirrors "Her hands stay a second longer than the collar strictly needs." Now he's the one lingering.
- "In bed I run my thumb over the callus on my left palm." He's thinking about Natsuki going into the dream.
- "My wrist stings. There's a cut on the inside of it…" with image_07: wound on the inside of the left wrist, a woman in a striped kimono binding it, cherry branch above, katana at the edge of the frame.
- "She says it like she's been waiting a very long time." / "That I kept them waiting far too long."
- "Pickup moved to Tuesdays and Fridays last fall. Dad's handwriting on the whiteboard still says Monday, and tonight I went with the handwriting." This quietly pays off part 1's whiteboard, and it's devastating. Probably the best-crafted beat in the chapter.

**Problems noticed in this part:**
14. **The two dream sequences don't read as the same dream.** Part 4 says "There's a dream I've had since I was small… Tonight, it comes back." But the only dream we've *seen* (the prologue) was a modern foggy road, a modern zip jacket, a curve mirror. The new one is a kimono-era cherry tree. Either the prologue is a different dream or vision, in which case one line should say so, or it's the same dream, in which case the art contradicts it. Right now I have to guess whether "it comes back" means the prologue.
15. **The ending restates its own subtext.** "She says it like she's been waiting a very long time" already lands the waiting theme. The wake-up lines "That someone was waiting for me. / That I kept them waiting far too long" say it twice more. I'd keep the second one and cut "That someone was waiting for me."
16. **Small address flicker:** in the same short scene Nanoha says "She's nice, **Kōhei**. I like her." and then "Good night, **Kō-chan**." I read it as deliberate (serious vs. affectionate), but it's the only time she uses the full name to his face, and it isn't signalled. If it's intentional, one narration beat ("She uses my whole name. She only does that when…") would turn it into a clue.

# Final answers

**When (quote) I became fairly sure (70%+) of anything the checkpoints asked:**
- **Who I want:** Nanoha reached 70% in part 1 on "You thought it in a short-person direction." She was back at 70% at the end on "That one's number four. 'Somebody wants to fight me.'"
- **Who he ends up with:** I never reached 70%. My peak was Natsuki at 55% after "I feel like I've met him somewhere before. I just can't place it." Then "her place by the station" and the lingering-at-the-garbage beat pulled it back to roughly even.
- **The woman in the dream:** I never reached 70%. My peak was 50% (Natsuki). The Natsuki wrist rhyme and the Nanoha behaviour cancel out.
- **Not asked, but I was 85–90% sure of these from early on:** Nanoha is in love with Kōhei ("Her hands stay a second longer than the collar strictly needs"), and Natsuki recognizes him ("Her grin stutters").

So the central romantic and dream questions are still genuinely open at the end of chapter 1. I think that's right for a first chapter.

**Hints that felt heavy-handed, too obvious or too coy:**
- *Heavy-handed:* "She went looking for mine before her own." The Fukuda detail already did the work, and the follow-up "I don't know why that's the thing that sticks with me" doubles it.
- *Heavy-handed:* Natsuki happening to say "which drawer sticks in your kitchen", then the narration explaining that Nanoha knows that drawer.
- *Heavy-handed (mild):* the triple "waiting" at the end (problem 15).
- *Slightly neat:* the "a second longer than the collar strictly needs" / "longer than the garbage requires" rhyme. It works, but if that construction shows up a third time it'll become a tic.
- *Too coy:* the prologue. There's no caption, no woman, it's never referenced again, and it's visually incompatible with the dream the text later calls recurring.
- *Too coy:* Kōhei's own reaction to Natsuki ("holding my pen hard enough to hurt"). I can't tell whether he recognizes her, is attracted to her, or is just rattled. One sensory hook would make it a clue.
- *About right:* Natsuki's stutter plus "I've met him somewhere", Mom behind the fusuma, Akane's illness, Dad's handwriting. Those are all calibrated well.

**Dialogue that felt lively:**
- Nanoha's class-assignment conspiracy: "A file has been opened. There have been meetings." / "The pool is green." / "That's what it says in the minutes."
- "You laughed. That's two points for me this morning." / "We've always been keeping score. You're losing."
- Ryōsuke: "That isn't an opinion. It's thermodynamics." Rika: "Then we've already narrowed it down!"
- Natsuki: "Big talk for someone who sits like he's being graded." / "Takes one to know one."
- Nanoha: "I have met him before. Every day. For sixteen years."
- "You're taking out the trash on the wrong day." / "I'm moving it closer to the right day."
- "I'm working up to it." (Nanoha, on dropping "-san")

**Up to 8 flat lines, and why:**
1. Akane: "Geez, Onii-chan! Every single morning!" Generic imouto exasperation that any little sister in any VN could say.
2. Akane: "Yaaay." The same stock register.
3. Akane: "Nanoha-chan brought kinpira!" It repeats what we just watched happen. It's meant as a topic-change dodge, but it reads as filler.
4. Nanoha: "A transfer student?" An echo question whose only job is to cue Rika.
5. Natsuki: "Looking forward to this year." It comes straight after the stutter, the most charged moment of her intro, and it's a pure formula line. A tiny crack ("…this year," a beat late) would carry the recovery.
6. Nanoha: "It's old. The pipes sound like a ghost." Everywhere else Nanoha meets a straight question with a deadpan bit. Here she's just informational. If that's because she's off-balance, the narration doesn't say so.
7. Kōhei: "Good morning, Tokiwa." A placeholder greeting. Ryōsuke's entrance is the joke, so Kōhei's line could be cut or made dry.
8. Nanoha: "She's nice, Kōhei. I like her." The interlude already showed the decision. Saying it aloud is in character (she's performing it), but on the page it duplicates the interlude's best line instead of adding to it.

**With the names covered, could I tell the characters apart, and who sounds like whom:**
- **Easily:** Kōhei (terse straight man), Natsuki (casual, sporty, teasing, opens with "So.", "Eh," "Okay, okay."), Ōno-sensei (weary adult), and Ryōsuke (lecture voice).
- **Nanoha:** mostly yes, from her formal-comic register ("Now then." "I've prepared a theory." "I do supervise beautifully."). But her absurd-logic bits overlap with **Ryōsuke**. "That's what it says in the minutes" could be his, and "'Somewhere far' covers most of the planet" could be hers. They run on the same deadpan pseudo-logic engine.
- **Akane vs. Rika:** without "Onii-chan" and "Nano-chan", the two exclamation-heavy girls blur. "Then go hover over the eggs!" could be Rika, and "Oh! Oh oh oh" could be Akane. Akane's only distinct line is the quiet "…You always are."

**Mismatches between the text and the illustrations:**
- image_04: the text has her folding his **collar** down, "up on her toes". The art has her straightening his **tie knot**, apparently flat-footed.
- image_04: the text says "Nanoha walks on the river side". In the art Kōhei is on the river side.
- image_06: her eyes should land on Kōhei, who sits by the window near the back. In the art her eyes slide to the side **away** from the window, and the camera is behind the front row.
- image_05/06 vs image_04: a clear style drift. Natsuki's CG is glossier and more realistic, with elongated legs and a lower, more fan-service framing. Nanoha's is soft watercolor. They don't look like the same artist.
- Prologue (image_01–03) vs image_07: the text calls the dream recurring, but the art shows two different eras and two different arms (a smooth young hand in a modern zip jacket vs. a weathered adult arm in traditional dress).
- Prologue: if a woman is meant to be in the opening dream, she isn't in any of the three images.
- What matches well: image_07's cut is on the inside of the left wrist, as written. The petal on Nanoha's braid, Natsuki's copper hair tucked behind one ear, and the uniforms are all consistent.

**Would I keep reading?** Yes, definitely to chapter 2.
- **What hooked me:** the caregiving details, above all the whiteboard in Dad's handwriting and its garbage-day payoff. Also the Nanoha interlude ("She did not let herself think about why it had needed deciding at all"), and the fact that both girls are likeable and became friends before anything romantic happened. The dream-woman question is genuinely split for me, and that's a good hook.
- **What would make me stop:**
  - destiny settling the romance *for* Kōhei, so that a past-life bond simply overrides what Nanoha has earned
  - Akane's illness turning into a tear-jerk device
  - Kōhei staying a passive "I don't know why that sticks with me" protagonist for long
  - the fog/sword prologue turning the romance into an action series without the setup earning it

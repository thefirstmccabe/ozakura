# Reader 2 notes: Chapter 1, "The House Next Door"

## Checkpoint 1 (after part1.txt)

**What I saw in the opening illustrations (before the title card):**
- image_01: an empty foggy country road at a T-junction, orange convex traffic mirror, block wall, telephone poles, hills lost in mist. No people.
- image_02: close-up of a man's hand (dark zip-up jacket, modern clothes) gripping a real katana by the hilt. Wrapped grip, tsuba, scabbard. Not a bamboo kendo sword. No face.
- image_03: the same kind of road, with a wall of fog (drawn almost like waves or smoke) rolling in from both sides.
- image_01 again, then black, then the title card.

There is no text at all in the opening and nothing says it's a dream. Nobody is shown except a hand. **I did not see a woman anywhere in it.**

**Who do I think Kōhei ends up with?** Nanoha, about 50%. She's the only possible love interest introduced so far, and the scene is built around her (the collar CG, "Her hands stay a second longer than the collar strictly needs," she speeds up after he offers to come Saturday). The 50% and not higher is because she's the textbook childhood friend from next door, and in the manga I read the childhood friend usually loses. The chapter title "The House Next Door" also makes me wonder whether someone new is about to move in next door, which would be the classic setup for her rival.

**Who do I want him to end up with?** Nanoha, moderately (about 65/100). She's funny and I like her ("You thought it in a short-person direction"). It's a soft "want" because I haven't met anyone else.

**Who is the woman in the dream?** I can't answer from what I've seen. There was no woman in the opening, only a hand holding a sword in fog. If you made me guess who a dream woman would be, I'd say something tied to the shrine and the Ōzakura tree, so maybe Nanoha (her grandpa looks after the shrine), but that's about 10% and really a guess about the genre, not the text.

**What does the dream have to do with the present story?** Unclear. Possible links:
- The sword. Kōhei does kendo (on the fridge whiteboard), so the hand could be his. But it's a real katana and a modern jacket, so it reads more like something ominous (a fight? a future event?) than like club practice.
- The fog. The mountains around Shirasawa are misty (the morning walk mentions wind "off the mountains"), so the foggy road might be somewhere local, maybe on the same road. I'd guess a premonition, or a memory of the night something happened to Dad.
- I'm not even sure it's a dream. It could be a flash-forward.

**What's going on, and what kind of story is this becoming:**
- A quiet slice-of-life school romance with a grief or caretaker undertow. Dad is gone, probably dead, since "last spring" (his handwriting on the whiteboard that Kōhei won't write over). Mom is shut in her room and barely responds (depression?). Kōhei runs the house: he cooks, packs two lunches, juggles kendo and a part-time job at "Hoshino Kitchen," and watches his little sister Akane, who has some medical condition (a dizzy spell, "the room starts tilting," a clinic day circled in red).
- Nanoha has been bringing food "for about a year," so since Dad. She knows the kitchen better than a guest would. She's the warm center of the household.
- The Ōzakura tree on the shrine hill is set up hard as something that will matter (it's the title of the whole thing, presumably). Saturday's shrine sweeping is clearly where the next beat happens.
- The opening hints at something supernatural or dramatic (fog, sword) under the slice of life.

**Lines, images and moments that drove my guesses:**
- "All of it except the bottom line, which still says 'Garbage: burnables, Mon/Thu' in Dad's. He wrote it last spring. I've never written over it." Strong, quiet, and told me Dad is gone without saying it.
- "The fusuma to Mom's room is closed. It usually is." and "That's an answer. Sort of."
- Akane: "…You always are." / "She says it to the rice cooker, not to me." Akane sees how much he's carrying.
- Nanoha: "We've always been keeping score. You're losing." Flirty, and sounds like a line that will come back.
- image_04 (collar CG) plus "Her hands stay a second longer than the collar strictly needs." Plainly romantic.
- Nanoha: "So you'd hear yourself say it." She's worried he's overloaded, and Saturday afternoon is the one free slot, so the shrine-sweeping "invitation" reads as her making a plan to be with him.
- "She walks a little faster after that." She's pleased or flustered he said yes.

**Small notes so far:**
- image_04 vs text: the text says she's "up on her toes" and has "needed the extra inches since middle school," and the jokes make her very short. In the picture she's only moderately shorter (her eyes are about level with his chin), and you can't see her toes. The petal on her braid is there, which is nice. She's working on his tie knot more than "folding my shirt collar back down," but that's close enough.
- The text says Nanoha "walks on the river side," and in the CG she's on the side away from the river. Not a big deal since she's stopped in front of him.

## Checkpoint 2 (after part2.txt)

**Who do I think Kōhei ends up with?** Now it's a two-horse race.
- Natsuki: about 45%. A transfer student who walks in, falters only when she sees the hero, and turns out to share his sport is the setup of the "destined" heroine in nearly every romance I've read. Her grin stuttering at him in particular is the kind of moment a story pays off.
- Nanoha: about 40%. The class-board moment ("She went looking for mine before her own") is a strong point in her favor, but it's also the classic childhood-friend move that makes me worry for her.
- Someone else / unknown: about 15%.

**Who do I want him to end up with?** Still Nanoha, about 55/100. Natsuki is more striking, and "Takes one to know one" is a good flirty exit, but I'm already attached to Nanoha's deadpan. Natsuki's at about 35. I'd like to see them spar, literally.

**Who is the woman in the dream?** Still no woman has appeared in the opening, so I'm guessing from the outside: **Natsuki, about 25%.** My reasons:
1. She does kendo, and the opening is all about a sword.
2. She seems to recognize Kōhei ("Her grin stutters… long enough that I'm sure I'm the only one who sees it").
3. She spots his sword-hand callus right away.

But the hand in image_02 looked like a man's hand to me, so if that's the dreamer or the person in the dream, it could just as easily be Kōhei, or his dad. Nanoha drops to about 10%.

**What does the dream have to do with the present-day story?**
- New theory: the sword and fog might be about a past meeting between Kōhei and Natsuki. Maybe they knew each other as kids (she's moved six times, so maybe Shirasawa was once one of them). Or something supernatural (a past life, a shared dream) makes her recognize him.
- Kōhei's own reaction ("I realize I've been holding my pen hard enough to hurt") suggests he felt something too, though the text doesn't say what. It could be recognition, nerves, or the word "kendo" hitting a sore spot (Dad?).

**What's going on, and what kind of story is this becoming:**
- The ensemble is filling out:
  - Rika is the hyper friend who ships Nanoha and Kōhei ("Hm. Hmmm.").
  - Tokiwa Ryōsuke argues about taiyaki like it's physics.
  - Ōno-sensei is the cardigan teacher.
- The main shape is a love triangle: Nanoha (the girl next door, the shrine, the home) versus Natsuki (outsider, kendo, mystery).
- The opening's fog and sword suggest a fate or mystery layer, probably tied to the Ōzakura and Natsuki.

**Lines, images and moments that drove my guesses:**
- "Yours was easier. It's always right under Fukuda-kun's." Then: "Fukuda's not in Two-B this year. He's in Two-E. She went looking for mine before her own." A lovely bit of showing. (Slightly undercut by the next line, "I don't know why that's the thing that sticks with me," which is the narrator nudging us when we already got it.)
- image_06: Natsuki's smile dropping into a startled sideways look toward the window side, where Kōhei sits. Coming straight after image_05's full smile, it's effective.
- "Her grin stutters. Just for a second. Just long enough that I'm sure I'm the only one who sees it."
- "Left palm. You've got the callus right where the tsuka (hilt) sits." / "Takes one to know one." Cool, and it echoes the hand-on-a-hilt image in the opening.
- "I realize I've been holding my pen hard enough to hurt."

**Small notes:**
- image_05 and image_06 match the text well: copper shoulder-length hair tucked behind one ear, neat uniform, eyes sliding toward the window rows. Her hand stays up mid-wave in image_06, which looks a little frozen, but it reads as the moment the wave falters.
- "(hilt)" after "tsuka" is a helpful gloss, but it's the first in-line translation note I've hit. Everything else ("fusuma," "kinpira," "karaage") goes unexplained, so this one stands out a little.

## Checkpoint 3 (after part3.txt)

**Who do I think Kōhei ends up with?**

- Nanoha: about 40%. The interlude makes it clear she's in love with him and hiding it ("She'd had practice"). But it also shows her stepping aside and choosing to like the rival, which is exactly how the childhood friend gets her heart broken in this genre.

**Who do I want him to end up with?** Nanoha, about 60/100, up from 55. The interlude did that. "Nanoha didn't say that Kōhei's kitchen drawer stuck. She didn't say she'd known it since she was six." hurt in a good way. I like Natsuki more now too (the smaller smile "that cost her something" made her more than a cool girl), so about 40 for her.

**Who is the woman in the dream?** **Natsuki, about 35%.** Still no woman has been shown in the opening, so it's indirect. But "I feel like I've met him somewhere before. I just can't place it." together with the sword imagery and her being a kendo girl points that way. My guess is that someone (maybe Kōhei, maybe Natsuki herself) has been dreaming of the other. Nanoha about 10%. The rest is "someone not introduced yet," for example a figure tied to the Ōzakura tree or the shrine.

**What does the dream have to do with the present-day story?** My working theory: Natsuki and Kōhei have some prior link she can't explain. She checks whether he competed around Sendai, he didn't, and so the "somewhere before" has no ordinary explanation. That makes the foggy road and the sword feel like a dream or a memory they share, or a past-life echo, probably tied to the old cherry tree. The 水 she writes on her wrist is also the 水 of her surname, Mizuno, and the opening is all mist and water. That might be a coincidence, or it might be deliberate.

**What's going on, and what kind of story this is becoming:** A love triangle in which both girls are sympathetic and genuinely like each other. That's nicer than catfighting, and it makes it hurt more. Kōhei has turned down three confessions because he "doesn't have time," so his arc is clearly about letting himself want something while he holds his family together. There's a slow supernatural/fate layer underneath, which isn't revealed yet.

**Lines and moments that drove my guesses:**
- "The guy who turned down three confessions last year." / "It's true that I don't have time." That's his wall.
- "She props her chin on her hand and looks me over. Shoulders. Hands. Like she's working out my reach." Great; she reads him as an opponent first.
- She writes 水 on the inside of her wrist, small, under her cuff. A good physical character detail, and a small promise that she'll show up Wednesday.
- Nanoha holding her lunch "the way she holds things when she's about to do something brave." She's brave enough to befriend the rival before the rival becomes one.
- Kōhei: "I feel like furniture. / I eat my rice. It's good rice. I made it myself." Funny and a little sad.
- Natsuki: "Does Fujisawa ever compete up north? Around Sendai?" … "I feel like I've met him somewhere before."
- Nanoha: "I have met him before. Every day. For sixteen years." The best line in the chapter so far.
- "She'd decided it firmly, the way you shut a window against the wind. / She did not let herself think about why it had needed deciding at all."

**Small notes:**
- Gloss quirk: "水 (Wednesday)." 水 by itself means "water" and is shorthand for Wednesday. The gloss is fine for a reader, but it hides the possible Mizuno/water pun. If the author wants readers to notice that, "(Wednesday, 'water')" or a narration beat would land it. If it's not meant to be noticed, ignore this.
- Recognition beats: the stutter in part 2, the Sendai question and "I feel like I've met him somewhere before" in part 3. That's three beats on the same point, and the third one says it outright. It still works, but it's at the edge of over-explaining.
- The narration voice switches cleanly for the third-person interlude. Kōhei's narration calls her "Mizuno" and the interlude calls her "Natsuki," which is a nice touch.

# Final answers

### When did I become fairly sure (70%+) of anything?
- **That Nanoha is in love with Kōhei:** about 70% at "When I finally get to the board, Fukuda's not in Two-B this year. He's in Two-E. She went looking for mine before her own." Then 90%+ at "Nanoha said it as lightly as she knew how. She'd had practice."
- **That the dream is an old-Japan / past-life memory tied to the cherry tree and to the romance:** about 75% at image_07 together with "Somewhere above us, a branch moving in the wind. / Petals." Then firmer at "That I kept them waiting far too long."
- **Never reached 70%:** who he ends up with (my top was 50%) or who the dream woman is (top 45%). If that split is intended, it's working. See problem 12 for why my split may come from signals cancelling rather than from deliberate balance.

### Hints that felt heavy-handed, too obvious, or too coy
- **Heavy:** Natsuki's recognition is hit three times: the grin stutter (part 2), the Sendai question, then "I feel like I've met him somewhere before. I just can't place it." The stutter alone was enough. The Sendai question is good because it rules out a normal explanation. The explicit line spells it out.
- **Heavy:** "I don't know why that's the thing that sticks with me all through the opening ceremony." The Fukuda reveal already did the work, and this line explains the joke.
- **Heavy (mild):** the dream ending says "waiting" three times in a few lines: "She says it like she's been waiting a very long time." / "That someone was waiting for me." / "That I kept them waiting far too long." One or two would hit harder.
- **Too coy:** the cold open (image_01–03). With no text, no label and nothing paying it off by the end of the chapter, it doesn't connect to anything. It isn't foggy in the dream, the dream is period-dress while the opening is a modern jacket and road, and there's no woman. It reads like a trailer for a different, darker show.
- **Coy (mild):** "I realize I've been holding my pen hard enough to hurt." I can't tell whether that's attraction, recognition, or the word "kendo" hitting a nerve. One more beat of interiority would help. Or leave it if it's meant to be a hook, but right now it slides by.
- **Possibly too subtle, if intended:** 水 is glossed only as "(Wednesday)." 水 is "water," the 水 in Mizuno, and the opening is all mist and river. If that's a planted clue, nobody who doesn't read kanji will catch it.
- **Just right:** "What's number one?" / "Secret." Also Dad's handwriting and the garbage day. Also "She did not let herself think about why it had needed deciding at all."

### Dialogue that felt lively
- Nanoha's whole class-assignment conspiracy: "In a small room with no windows… The pool turns green." / "The pool is green." / "That's what it says in the minutes."
- "You thought it in a short-person direction." / "He totally did." / "Traitor."
- Ryōsuke: "That isn't an opinion. It's thermodynamics." / Rika: "…Oh no." / "We'll resume this at lunch. I'll need a diagram."
- Rika: "A girl! From somewhere! Far!" / Ryōsuke: "'Somewhere far' covers most of the planet." / Rika: "Then we've already narrowed it down!"
- Natsuki: "…six times now? So please don't go easy on me." / "Big talk for someone who sits like he's being graded."
- Nanoha: "I have met him before. Every day. For sixteen years."
- Natsuki: "He's got kind of a normal one, right? Don't tell him I said that."
- "You're taking out the trash on the wrong day." / "I'm moving it closer to the right day."

### Flat lines (with why)
1. Kōhei: "Monday, Wednesday, Friday, Saturday morning. Shifts Tuesday, Thursday, Sunday." This is the whiteboard narration from about 80 lines earlier, nearly word for word. Nanoha's "So you'd hear yourself say it" justifies it in-story, but the reader hears the schedule twice.
2. Akane: "Nanoha-chan brought kinpira!" It restates what we just watched happen. If it's meant as Akane changing the subject after "Traitor," it needs a beat to show that.
3. Akane: "Geez, Onii-chan! Every single morning!" Stock dubbed-anime little sister. Her later lines ("Then go hover over the eggs!", "…You always are.") are much better.
4. Nanoha: "Good. She noticed the squeaky stairs. Both of them." We just saw this in the interlude. It does set up "Nobody notices the eleventh step." / "She did.", which is good, so it's only half flat.
5. Kōhei: "Club starts Wednesday." This is the third Wednesday mention. It's functional, and it's there to set up the wrist-writing, so it's minor.
6. Nanoha: "It's old. The pipes sound like a ghost." A little limp for a character this witty. Natsuki's "Perfect." carries the exchange.

### With names covered, could I tell them apart?
Mostly yes.
- **Nanoha** (deadpan, formal-absurd: "Now then.", "I do supervise beautifully.") is the most distinctive voice. She switches to hesitant ("Um—", "That's—yes. That's fine.") with Natsuki, which reads as nerves, not inconsistency.
- **Natsuki** (casual, direct, opens with "So.", "Eh," "Okay, okay.", "Ha—") is distinct from Nanoha.
- **Ryōsuke** (lecturing) and **Ōno-sensei** (dry adult) are unmistakable.
- **Blur 1: Akane and Rika.** Both are exclamation-heavy and bubbly. Without the "Onii-chan," lines like "He totally did." or "Then go hover over the eggs!" could be Rika.
- **Blur 2: Kōhei and Nanoha** sometimes share the same dry register. "I'm standing in my own kitchen." and "The pool is green." could go to either.
- **The dream woman** ("You're late." / "Hurt again. Give me your hand… Hold still.") sounds most like Nanoha, because her clipped imperative matches "Kō-chan. Your collar." / "There." Natsuki's voice is chattier.

### Mismatches between text and illustrations
- **The opening (image_01–03) vs. the dream (image_07).** The opening is a modern road with a traffic mirror and power lines, a modern zip-up jacket, a real katana, and heavy fog. The dream text and image_07 are period: a striped kimono and obi, a cloth bandage, a cherry branch, clear light, no fog. Nothing in the text acknowledges the opening, so I don't know whether it's the same dream, another dream, or a flash-forward.
- **image_04 vs. Nanoha's height.** The text sells her as very short: "up on her toes," "needed the extra inches since middle school," "Chibi," the short-person jokes. In the CG she's only moderately shorter (eyes about level with his chin) and her toes aren't visible. She's also working on his tie knot rather than "folding my shirt collar back down over my tie." (The petal on her braid matches nicely.)
- **image_05/06:** consistent with the text, including the sideways look toward the window rows. The hand stays frozen mid-wave in 06, which is a little stiff, but fine.
- **image_07:** matches the text well (the cut on the inside of the wrist, the cloth, the petals, the branch, the katana). The forearm looks like a grown man's (hairy, veiny), not a teenager's. That's fine if it's a past self, and it actually helped me read it as a past life.

### Would I keep reading?
Yes. What hooked me:
- Nanoha's interiority (the third-person interlude is the strongest section).
- A triangle where the two girls genuinely like each other.
- The Dad's-handwriting thread.
- The cherry-tree dream.

What would make me stop:
- If the foggy katana opening turns out to be the start of an action or occult plot that pushes out the slice of life.
- If Nanoha becomes a pure self-sacrificing doormat.
- If Kōhei's "I don't have time" wall goes on for many chapters without cracks.
- If Akane's illness gets milked for melodrama.

### Problems reported (numbered for count)
1. The cold open (image_01–03) is unexplained and visually contradicts the end-of-chapter dream (modern, fog, jacket vs. period, petals, kimono). It doesn't pay off within the chapter and contains no woman.
2. image_04: Nanoha's height difference is understated compared with the text, and she's adjusting the tie rather than folding the collar down.
3. Natsuki's recognition is over-explained ("I feel like I've met him somewhere before. I just can't place it." is the third beat on the same point).
4. Narrator lampshade: "I don't know why that's the thing that sticks with me all through the opening ceremony."
5. Kōhei's weekly schedule is given twice almost verbatim (whiteboard narration, then dialogue).
6. The dream close says "waiting" three times. It's on the nose.
7. "I realize I've been holding my pen hard enough to hurt" is unexplained enough that it reads as a dropped beat, not a hook.
8. Glossing is inconsistent: tsuka, shodan and 水 are glossed; fusuma, kinpira, karaage and taiyaki aren't. The 水 = "Wednesday" gloss also hides a possible water/Mizuno resonance, if that's intended.
9. Akane and Rika's voices overlap (both bubbly and exclamation-driven).
10. Filler and stock lines: Akane's "Nanoha-chan brought kinpira!" and "Geez, Onii-chan! Every single morning!", and Nanoha's partial recap "She noticed the squeaky stairs. Both of them."
11. Address check: Nanoha calls him "Kō-chan" to his face everywhere else but "She's nice, Kōhei." in part 4. It read to me as a deliberate serious shift, but if it isn't deliberate, it's a slip.
12. The dream-woman clues may cancel each other out. The wrist echo (Natsuki writes on the inside of her wrist, and the dream cut is on the inside of a wrist) maps Natsuki onto the *swordsman*, not the woman. Meanwhile the woman's voice and caretaking map onto Nanoha. If the author wants readers to lean one way, these signals pull in opposite directions. If a deliberate split is the goal, it works, but it came from noise as much as design.

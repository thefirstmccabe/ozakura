# Reader 2 notes: Chapter 1, "The House Next Door"

## Checkpoint 1 (after part 1: the dream prologue)

**Who does Kōhei end up with?** No idea, 0% on anyone. The name "Kōhei" hasn't appeared yet; I'm assuming he's the narrator. The only woman on stage is the dream woman, and she's anonymous.

**Who do I want him with?** No preference yet. The dream woman is the only candidate I've met, and I like her a little ("She says it like she's laughing"), maybe 20/100 interest.

**Who is the woman in the dream?** Unknown. My guesses, low confidence:
- The narrator's lover or wife in a past life (samurai-era). About 50%. The katana in the illustration, the kimono, the "home from training" line and "waiting a very long time" all point that way.
- A girl he'll meet (or already knows) in the present, who is that past-life woman reborn. About 40% that this is where it goes. The chapter title "The House Next Door" makes me guess the neighbour girl.
- Pure symbol or a childhood memory that's been dressed up: 10%.

**What does the dream have to do with the present?** Probably reincarnation or fated lovers: a promise or debt from a past life ("I kept them waiting far too long") that the present-day story pays off. The bandage on the wrist reads like a motif that will come back (a present-day wrist injury, or someone bandaging him the same way).

**What kind of story is this becoming?** A school romance with a past-life or fate layer under it. Melancholy and soft so far. The title "The House Next Door" says childhood friend or new neighbour.

**What drove the guesses:**
- "There's a dream I've had since I was small." The recurring dream is the hook.
- "She says it like she's been waiting a very long time." / "That I kept them waiting far too long." Guilt plus waiting suggests a broken or overdue promise.
- "Hurt again. Give me your hand… Hold still." "Again" means this happened often; she's used to patching him up. That reads as intimacy and routine (wife or long-time sweetheart, not a stranger).
- The illustration (image_01): a man's rough, grown forearm with a sword hilt beside it, being bandaged by a woman in a blue-grey striped kimono under cherry blossoms. The man is clearly adult, so it isn't a "small boy" memory; it's someone else's life. No faces shown, so it's deliberately hiding who she is.

**Small notes:**
- Text says the cut is "across the back of" the wrist; in the image the blood spot is on the top of the forearm near the wrist, which roughly fits. Fine.
- "The sound of a river, close by": no river in the picture, but that's a sound, so no problem.
- "Hands that aren't mine wind a strip of cloth around a wrist that is." Nice line; I had to reread it once.

## Checkpoint 2 (after part 2: Monday morning, walk to school, class lists)

**Who does Kōhei end up with?** Nanoha 50%, the unseen tall transfer student 40%, someone else 10%. Nanoha is set up as the childhood friend who's plainly in love with him. In the manga I read, that girl loses to the mysterious new arrival about half the time, so I'm hedging hard. Overall confidence is low, about 50.

**Who do I want him with?** Nanoha, fairly strongly (70/100). She's the funniest person in the chapter, and the quiet bits (finding his name before her own, the collar) land. I haven't met the transfer student, so she can't compete yet.

**Who is the woman in the dream?** Nanoha 45%, the transfer student 40%, someone else (his mother? a past-life figure with no present-day match) 15%.
- For Nanoha: the dream has a river "close by", and "Nanoha walks on the river side. She always walks on the river side." The dream woman bandages him and scolds him for being hurt "again"; Nanoha fixes his collar, brings food, and knows his schedule better than he does. Her grandfather looks after "the Ōzakura" (a big cherry tree, I assume), and the dream is under a cherry branch. The kendo club matches "home from training" and the sword.
- For the transfer student: genre logic. A girl "from somewhere far" arriving on day one of a story that opens with a destiny dream is usually the destiny. "She's been waiting a very long time" fits someone coming back from far away better than someone who's been next door all along. Rika also says "It's destiny! It's in the stars!" about the class, which feels like a wink.
- Against Nanoha: the dream woman is gentle and slow ("like the knot matters more than anything else in the world"), while Nanoha is quick and teasing. But the dream woman also says "You're late" like a scolding and like she's laughing, which is very Nanoha.

**What does the dream have to do with the present?** Now I'm fairly sure the dream's place is this town: river, cherry trees along it, maybe the Ōzakura itself. Kendo makes the samurai link. Either Kōhei is a reincarnated swordsman and one of these girls is the woman who waited, or the dream is about Nanoha and he's too dense to see it. The "I kept them waiting far too long" guilt also echoes how he treats Nanoha now: she's been waiting a year-plus (or since childhood) for him to notice.

**What's going on, and what kind of story?** A quieter, sadder school romance than I expected. The family background is heavy and handled with restraint: Dad gone (dead, I think; "He wrote it last spring. I've never written over it."), Mom shut in her room, probably depressed, and Akane has some chronic illness with dizzy spells and a clinic day. Kōhei is the de facto parent, packed schedule, "I like being busy" as a shield. Nanoha's family has been quietly feeding them "for about a year now", which lines up with Dad's death. So the romance will probably be about him letting someone take care of him. Comedy relief from Rika and Tokiwa.

**What drove the guesses (quotes):**
- "The back door slides open. No knock. There's never a knock." She's the girl next door, which matches the chapter title.
- "Nanoha walks on the river side. She always walks on the river side. I don't remember when we decided that." This is the strongest dream link. It felt deliberately planted.
- "Her hands stay a second longer than the collar strictly needs. / Probably a stubborn crease." The dense-narrator joke. It tells me the author wants me to see what he doesn't.
- "She went looking for mine before her own." Good beat. It's the moment I started wanting Nanoha.
- "She walks a little faster after that" after he offers to come Saturday. She's pleased and hiding it.
- Rika's "A completely neutral hmmm." Rika knows Nanoha likes him.
- "A girl! From somewhere! Far! I heard she's super tall." Rival setup.
- "Grandpa says the Ōzakura's the best it's been in years." The location hook. I expect the climax of the chapter, or the meeting with the transfer student, to happen there on Saturday.

**Notes on the writing so far:**
- Lively: Akane's "Two nights in a row. Medical science is baffled." / "The doctor face. You don't have a license." Nanoha's "You thought it in a short-person direction." and the windowless-meetings theory. Tokiwa's "That's not a counterargument. That's a timestamp."
- The pool gag is muddled: "Because last year they put you in 1-C for five days during the exchange program." I didn't follow why that makes the pool green, or what the "exchange program" was. It reads like a beat got cut.
- "fat with snowmelt from the Tanzawa": I don't know what the Tanzawa is (mountains, I guess from context). Fine for a translated-manga feel, but a reader might trip.
- "The vice-principal is five feet tall. In shoes." This is the first time I noticed feet and inches in a Japanese setting. It's fine in an English localization.
- "Two hundred students trying to read six sheets of paper" works.
- Nanoha is apparently short; the dream art doesn't show the woman's height, so no conflict.

## Checkpoint 3 (after part 3: Natsuki's introduction, lunch)

**Who does Kōhei end up with?** Natsuki 50%, Nanoha 45%, someone else 5%. Confidence is still low, around 50. Natsuki arrives with every "she's the one" signal the genre has: a recognition flicker, shared kendo, the seat right behind him, his physical reaction. But that much signalling in chapter 1 can also mean she's the misdirect and the quieter girl wins. It's a coin flip with a slight lean toward the newcomer.

**Who do I want him with?** Still Nanoha, but less decisively: 55 Nanoha / 45 Natsuki. Natsuki is fun ("Okay, you're fun."; "I hate practicing with polite people"), and her startled "…Really? You'd do that?" gave her a soft spot I didn't expect. I'd also happily read a story where those two become real friends, which the lunch scene sets up nicely.

**Who is the woman in the dream?** Natsuki 55%, Nanoha 35%, someone else 10%. This flipped from the last checkpoint.
- For Natsuki: "Her grin stutters. Just for a second." She recognises something in him, or he in her. "I realize I've been holding my pen hard enough to hurt." His body reacts the way it would to the dream (the wrist stings). She does kendo, which matches the sword. And "She repeats it like she's writing it on the inside of her wrist." The word "wrist" can't be an accident in a chapter that opened on a bandaged wrist.
- For Nanoha: everything from checkpoint 2 still stands (river side, caretaker, cherry trees, her grandfather and the Ōzakura). Nothing in this part took it away. She just got less screen focus.
- The doubt: the dream woman is the one who bandages, but Natsuki is the one with the wrist line, and it's on her own wrist. If she's the bandager, the wrist imagery should sit on him. I may be over-reading.

**What does the dream have to do with the present?** I now think the story is deliberately keeping two candidates in play for the dream woman, and the romance question and the dream question are the same question. Natsuki seems to remember or recognise something; Kōhei reacts without knowing why. I'd bet the dream is a past life (sword, kimono) and the woman is reborn as one of these two girls. Natsuki moving around six times and turning up here, near the Ōzakura, fits "she's been waiting a very long time" and "You're late", though in this scene she's the late one.

**What kind of story is this becoming?** A love triangle with a fate or reincarnation thread, where the triangle is gentle (the girls are kind to each other, not catty). The grief-and-duty family plot from part 2 should give it weight. Natsuki's question, "turned down three confessions… didn't have time for a girlfriend", names Kōhei's whole problem.

**What drove the guesses (quotes):**

- "I realize I've been holding my pen hard enough to hurt."

- "She looks almost startled, like she isn't used to being the one who gets asked." Natsuki has a lonely side under the confidence (six moves).

# Final answers

## When I became fairly sure (70%+) of anything
- **That Nanoha is in love with Kōhei:** 70%+ at "Her hands stay a second longer than the collar strictly needs." and about 90% at "She went looking for mine before her own." The interlude ("She did not let herself think about why it had needed deciding at all.") made it certain.
- **That the dream is a real past life tied to this town and to one of these girls:** about 75% at Natsuki's "Does he ever seem like… someone you've met before?" Before that, the river, kendo and Ōzakura clues had me around 60%.
- **Who he ends up with:** never reached 70%. I peaked at 50% for either girl.
- **Who the dream woman is:** never reached 70%. I peaked at 60% for Natsuki, at the end.
- **Who I want him with:** Nanoha, peaking at 70/100 after part 2. Natsuki pulled it back to 55–60.

## Hints that felt heavy-handed, too obvious or too coy
- **Heavy:** "She repeats it like she's writing it on the inside of her wrist." It's an odd simile (people write on the back of a hand), so it draws attention to itself, and in a chapter that opened on a bandaged wrist it reads as the author tapping the glass.
- **Heavy:** Natsuki's "Someone who knows which drawer sticks in your kitchen." She picks the exact detail from Kōhei's kitchen that we saw Nanoha handle that morning. It's a coincidence she can't know about, so it feels authored. Nanoha's silent reaction is good, but the setup is too neat.
- **Obvious, but fine:** "Her hands stay a second longer than the collar strictly needs. / Probably a stubborn crease." A stock dense-narrator joke. It works, but I've read it a hundred times.
- **Obvious, but fine:** "It was fine. / It was fine." A repetition that signals the opposite. Standard but effective.
- **Well calibrated:** the grin stutter plus "holding my pen hard enough to hurt"; "She always walks on the river side. I don't remember when we decided that."; Nanoha's "…" before "I have met him before." These are the best hints in the chapter. They're noticeable without being labelled.
- **Possibly too coy:** nothing in the present day echoes the dream woman's own voice or manner. Nobody says "You're late," nobody bandages anyone, and the striped kimono matches no one. So the dream's identity rests entirely on circumstantial clues split evenly between two girls. If that's deliberate, fine. If the author wants readers leaning one way by the end of chapter 1, it isn't happening for me.
- **Too coy (mildly):** "the Ōzakura" is never explained (a famous tree? at a shrine? why does Nanoha's grandfather look after it?). For something that's plainly the setting of the next big scene, one line of description would help.

## Dialogue that felt lively
- Akane: "And I slept yesterday too. Two nights in a row. Medical science is baffled." / "The doctor face. You don't have a license, Onii-chan." / "Hers. She brought kinpira."
- Nanoha: the whole class-assignment conspiracy ("In a small room with no windows… The pool turns green."), "You thought it in a short-person direction.", "His back is a very convenient back. It only hurts on days with a lot of petals.", "I still own a clipboard.", "I'm working up to it. I need time to adjust.", "You have several. I've catalogued them."
- Tokiwa: "That's not a counterargument. That's a timestamp." / "I chose it theoretically."
- Natsuki: "Is that in the welcome packet?" (Kōhei's line, but the exchange as a whole), "That's either really honest or really sad. I can't decide." / "I will. I'm very decisive. Usually.", "Oh, he's cocky. Good. I hate practicing with polite people.", "Very intense jet lag."
- Kōhei: "I'm moving it closer to the right day." and the narration line "It's very good rice. I made it myself."

## Up to 8 flat lines, and why
1. Natsuki: "I do kendo, and I'm looking for a dojo that'll put up with me. I also really love horror movies, so if anyone wants to watch one with me, I'll be grateful forever." It reads like a character profile read aloud. The horror-movie trait is dropped in and never comes back this chapter.
2. Natsuki: "I've never stayed anywhere long enough to have one of those. You know? Someone who knows which drawer sticks in your kitchen." The coincidence makes it feel scripted (see above).
3. Natsuki: "This school's nice. It feels… lived in. Does that make sense?" Generic. Nanoha's "The heating sounds like a ghost" carries the exchange.
4. Natsuki: "That's really nice." Filler.
5. Natsuki: "Please take care of me." This is the literal yoroshiku. In English it's stiff, and it lands at the exact moment after the grin stutters, where I wanted something loaded. The translated feel is intended, but this spot needed a better line.
6. Kōhei: "It's fine. I like being busy." The stock overworked-hero line.
7. Kōhei: "Looks can be deceiving." Stock. Nanoha's comeback ("That's why I fix them.") saves it.
8. Nanoha: "A transfer student?" A pure feed line to set up Rika.

Also: Ōno-sensei putting her in "the empty seat behind Fujisawa" is the oldest convenience in the genre. Everyone expects it, so it barely registers, but it's there.

## With the names covered, could I tell them apart?
- **Rika:** instantly. Caps, stretched vowels, exclamation marks, "destiny".
- **Tokiwa:** instantly. Pedantic, structural arguments, "theoretically".
- **Natsuki:** mostly. She's blunt, sporty and warm, with "Okay, okay" and "Eh, 'san'?" Some of her lines could be Nanoha's, though ("Very intense jet lag." is Nanoha's kind of joke).
- **Nanoha:** her absurdist deadpan (conspiracies, "It's a sickness", "I supervise beautifully") is distinctive at length, but single lines blur.
- **Akane:** the weak point. With "Onii-chan" removed, I'd confuse her with Nanoha. "Medical science is baffled." / "Stop doing the face." / "Hers. She brought kinpira." are Nanoha's rhythm and register. Two dry teasing girls in the same kitchen scene sound like one voice.
- **Kōhei:** his deadpan comebacks ("That's what the attendance sheet says." / "Is that in the welcome packet?" / "Let me know when you do.") are the same register as Nanoha and Tokiwa. Most of the cast shares one dry, quick wit. It's charming, but it flattens the differences.
- **Repeated device:** the "your face" joke appears three times: Akane's "Stop doing the face… The doctor face", Akane's "I saw his face", and Nanoha's catalogue of faces. The payoff (number four, number one) is good. The earlier two uses dilute it.
- **Address:** Nanoha switching between "Kō-chan" and "Kōhei" (she uses "Kōhei" when talking about Natsuki, then "Kō-chan" at the door) read as deliberate and worked for me. Natsuki jumping straight to "Nanoha" while Nanoha holds at "Natsuki-san" is a nice, clear character touch.

## Mismatches between text and illustrations
- **image_02, hair:** the text says "cut neat at her shoulders and tucked behind one ear"; the art shows layered hair well past the shoulders, not clearly tucked. This is the clearest mismatch.
- **image_02, height:** Rika's "super tall, taller than the vice-principal" isn't sold by the art. Nothing in the frame gives scale, and she reads as average height.
- **image_02, clock:** about 8:10, after a 7:50 board check and a full opening ceremony. Tight, but a nitpick.
- **image_02, classmates:** the three foreground girls have identical long, straight black hair and look cloned.
- **image_01:** consistent with the text (cut on the back of the wrist, cloth strip, petals, a branch overhead). The river is only a sound, so it's fine that it isn't shown. The arm is clearly a grown man's, which I took as intended (a past life, not a childhood memory).
- **Not a mismatch, but a gap in this test:** I never saw sprites, so all I know about Nanoha's looks is "short" and "a clip in her hair". The text never describes her the way it describes Natsuki ("Copper hair…"). If the sprites carry that, fine. In text alone, Natsuki gets a portrait and Nanoha doesn't.

## Other problems I noticed
- The pool gag doesn't parse: "Because last year they put you in 1-C for five days during the exchange program." I couldn't tell what the exchange program was or why it turned the pool green. It feels like a line is missing.
- "fat with snowmelt from the Tanzawa": an unexplained place name. It's guessable (mountains), but it was a small stumble.
- The garbage logic is slightly confusing. If pickup is now Tuesday, taking burnables out Monday night is the night before, so it isn't obviously the "wrong day". The emotional point (he followed Dad's handwriting) lands anyway.
- Class names are written two ways: "Two-B" in words and "1-C" in numerals.
- The inline glosses "tsuka (hilt)" and "shodan (first dan)" are fine for readers, but inside spoken dialogue they read like a textbook. They'd sit better as tooltips or in narration.

## Would I keep reading?
Yes. It's a solid first chapter, better than most VN openings I've played.
- **What hooked me:** the two girls genuinely liking each other, which makes the triangle hurt instead of cat-fight; the Natsuki/Nanoha classroom exchange ("someone you've met before?" / "Every day. For sixteen years."); the quiet family grief (Dad's handwriting, Mom's floorboard creak: "That's an answer. Sort of."); and the Ōzakura sweep set up for Saturday.
- **What would make me stop:** if the dream thread turns into reincarnation lore dumps; if Nanoha turns into a pure self-sacrificing "step aside" character; if Kōhei's obliviousness about Nanoha drags on for many chapters (the last scene suggests it won't, which is good); if Natsuki's "met before" stays a tease without escalation for too long; or if Akane's illness is used for cheap melodrama.

# Reader 1 notes: Chapter 1, "The House Next Door"

## Checkpoint 1 (after part 1: the dream prologue)

**Who does Kōhei end up with, and how sure?** No idea yet. Nobody has been named; I'm assuming the narrator is Kōhei because the prompt calls him that. If forced: "the woman from the dream, or whoever she turns out to be now," 25%. Everything else is a blank.

**Who do I want him to end up with?** Nobody to want yet. Mild pull toward the dream woman simply because she's the only person on the page and the "You're late" / "Hurt again" lines are warm. Interest: 3/10.

**Who is the woman in the dream?** Someone from the past, apparently a past life (the katana hilt and the kimono in the picture say "old Japan," not a kid's memory). Guess: a lover or betrothed from a previous life who bandaged him after sword training, and who will show up in the present as a girl he meets, probably the girl in "the house next door." Confidence that she's a past-life lover: 55%. Confidence she maps onto the neighbour girl: 35% (the title is doing all the work there).

**What does the dream have to do with the present?** Reincarnation / fated-lovers setup is my read. "That someone was waiting for me. That I kept them waiting far too long" reads like a promise he broke in a past life and a reunion this story is going to pay off.

**What's going on / what kind of story?** A school romance with a reincarnation or red-string-of-fate overlay. Feels like it'll be gentle and melancholy rather than action-heavy, even with the sword.

**What drove my guesses:**
- Image: katana hilt lower left, striped kimono, cherry blossoms. Instantly "past life."
- "You're late." / "She says it like she's been waiting a very long time." This is the hook line and it works.
- "Hurt again. Give me your hand… Hold still." The "again" says this happened often; she's used to patching him up. Nice, intimate.
- "Hands that aren't mine wind a strip of cloth around a wrist that is." Good line. Clever.
- Chapter title "The House Next Door," right after the dream, makes me assume the neighbour is the dream girl. That might be a deliberate misdirect or might be too on the nose; can't tell yet.

**Small things I noticed:**
- The arm in the picture looks like a grown man's: thick, veiny, weathered hand. If the narrator is a teenager, that's either deliberate (it's a past-life body) or a mismatch. I'll read it as deliberate for now.
- The text says "the sound of a river, close by," but there's no river in the picture. Minor; it's sound, so fine.
- The text says the cut is "across the back of" the wrist, and the blood spot on the bandage is on top of the forearm/wrist, so that matches.
- The grip in the picture is a little confusing at first glance: it took me a second to work out that the fist at top right is his own hand and not a third person's. The woman's lower hand and his knuckles crowd together.
- No face for her, which I assume is on purpose (so we can't identify her). Good choice.

## Checkpoint 2 (after part 2: morning, walk to school, class lists)

**Who does Kōhei end up with, and how sure?** Nanoha, 50%. The transfer student (tall girl, "from somewhere, far"), 40%. Someone else, 10%. My gut says Nanoha because the whole part is a love letter to her; my genre brain says the childhood friend who's been there all along is exactly the one these stories like to make wait, and a mysterious transfer student announced on day one of the school year is usually The Fated One.

**Who do I want him to end up with?** Nanoha, clearly: 7/10. She's funny, she's competent, and she's quietly holding his life together. The "short-person direction" bit sold me. I haven't met the transfer student, so nothing to weigh against her yet.

**Who is the woman in the dream?** Nanoha, 45%. Transfer student, 35%. Someone not yet introduced or purely a past-life figure, 20%.
- For Nanoha: she's the one who fixes him ("That's why I fix them." mirrors "Give me your hand… Hold still."); her teasing scold voice matches "She says it like a scolding. She says it like she's laughing."; she walks on the river side, and the dream has a river; her grandpa looks after "the Ōzakura," which I assume is a big cherry tree, and the dream is under a cherry tree.
- For the transfer student: "She's been waiting a very long time" and "I kept them waiting far too long" feel more like someone arriving from far away than someone who comes in your back door every morning. The rumour about her ("super tall") lands right at the end of the part like a setup.

**What does the dream have to do with the present?** More confident it's a past-life thing now. Kōhei does kendo ("home from training" + the katana in the picture), the walk to school runs along a river under cherry trees exactly like the dream, and the Ōzakura tree on Grandpa's land feels like the dream's actual location. I expect Saturday's sweeping at the Ōzakura to trigger something.

**What's going on / what kind of story?** A warm, slightly sad slice-of-life romance about an overstretched boy. Dad seems to have died about a year ago ("He wrote it last spring. I've never written over it."), Mom has withdrawn into her room, his sister Akane has some dizziness condition, and Kōhei is running the house, a part-time job and kendo. Nanoha's family has been quietly feeding them for a year. The romance is going to be about whether he lets anyone carry part of the load. That's a good spine. The reincarnation layer sits on top.

**Lines and moments that drove my guesses:**
- "The back door slides open. No knock. There's never a knock." This is the house next door. Lovely way to establish her.
- "She goes to the cupboard left of the sink, the one with the mugs and not the one with the glasses, and takes down two without looking." Best paragraph so far. Shows years of intimacy without saying it.
- "Her hands stay a second longer than the collar strictly needs. / Probably a stubborn crease." Funny, though the oblivious-narrator gag is very familiar.
- "She went looking for mine before her own." Good, quiet ending beat.
- "She walks a little faster after that, and for a while I actually have to work to keep up." She's pleased he's coming Saturday. Subtle and nice.
- Rika: "And Fujisawa-kun's in Two-B too. Hm. Hmmm." Classmates already ship them.
- Akane: "I could make my own lunch, you know." / "I'm already making two." / "…That's sort of my point." Sharp; the sister sees what he's doing to himself.
- Nanoha: "So you'd hear yourself say it." Same point from a different angle. Two characters hammering "you're overloaded" in one part is about the right amount; a third would be too much.

**Small things I noticed:**
- "A bike bell rings somewhere behind us and doesn't pass." That reads like it means something (someone following them?), but nothing comes of it. If it's just atmosphere, it's a red herring by accident.
- Narration calls him "Tokiwa," but his name label says "Ryōsuke." For a beat I thought "Tokiwa" was a different person arriving. Everyone else so far is labelled and narrated by the same given name, so he's the odd one out. The surname/given-name split is realistic, but a casual reader will stumble once.
- "Hoshino Kitchen": is that Nanoha's family's restaurant? I couldn't tell. Her mom makes too much food, which would fit, but Nanoha's surname is never said.
- I don't know yet what the Ōzakura is, exactly. I'm assuming a famous old cherry tree (maybe at a shrine, since Grandpa sweeps "up there"). If the reader is meant to know that, one line saying so would help.

## Checkpoint 3 (after part 3: Natsuki arrives)

**Who does Kōhei end up with, and how sure?** Natsuki, 50%. Nanoha, 45%. Someone else, 5%. I flipped. The story has just handed Natsuki every "destined" signal it has: she does kendo like him, she sits directly behind him, she reads his hand on sight, and both of them have a physical reaction the moment they see each other. Nanoha still has the emotional weight, and I could easily see the ending going her way, but the plot machinery is now pointed at Natsuki.

**Who do I want him to end up with?** Still Nanoha, 6/10 (down from 7, because Natsuki is a lot of fun). Natsuki, 4/10. Natsuki's "Oh, he's cocky. Good. I hate practicing with polite people." made me like her. But Nanoha walking over "the way she holds things when she's about to do something brave" and offering a tour to the girl who might take him away is the most sympathetic thing in the chapter. I'm now actively worried for her.

**Who is the woman in the dream?** Natsuki, 50%. Nanoha, 35%. Someone else / no present-day counterpart, 15%.
- For Natsuki: "Her grin stutters. Just for a second." That's recognition, and it pairs with Kōhei's "I realize I've been holding my pen hard enough to hurt." Both bodies remember something. Then "She repeats it like she's writing it on the inside of her wrist." The wrist is the dream's central image, and the narration hangs it on her. She also looks at his hand ("Left palm"), and the dream woman's line is "Give me your hand." That's three echoes in one scene.
- Against Natsuki: the dream woman is the one who patches up the swordsman. Natsuki is herself a swordswoman; she reads as a sparring partner or rival more than the one waiting at home with a strip of cloth. Nanoha is the one who fixes his collar and feeds his family. So the dream's *role* fits Nanoha and the dream's *recognition* fits Natsuki. I suspect that tension is the point, which makes me more interested, not less.

**What does the dream have to do with the present?** Now pretty sure (65%) at least one of the two girls is tied to the dream's past life and that kendo is the bridge (training, the sword in the picture, the callus "right where the tsuka sits"). The open question the story seems to be setting up: is the fated one the one he's meeting, or the one who's always been there?

**What's going on / what kind of story?** A love triangle with a past-life tiebreaker. Childhood friend vs. fated rival-type transfer student. What lifts it above the template is that Nanoha and Natsuki immediately like each other. If they become real friends, the triangle will hurt more. The "I don't have time for a girlfriend" detail ties the romance back to his home situation, which is good.

**Lines and moments that drove my guesses:**
- "Her grin stutters. Just for a second. Just long enough that I'm sure I'm the only one who sees it." Biggest single clue so far.
- "I realize I've been holding my pen hard enough to hurt." Very effective; he doesn't explain it and doesn't need to.
- "Left palm. You've got the callus right where the tsuka (hilt) sits. It takes years to get it that thick." / "Takes one to know one." Great introduction of a shared world.
- "She repeats it like she's writing it on the inside of her wrist." Clearly placed; a wrist image right after the wrist dream. On a second read this will look like a giant arrow, but on a first read it just felt like nice prose. Right calibration, I think.
- "That's either really honest or really sad. I can't decide." / "I will. I'm very decisive. Usually." Good banter. She has a voice.
- Nanoha: "I'm working up to it. I need time to adjust." Polite reserve, and maybe a little wariness. Nicely ambiguous.
- "…makes me feel weirdly like furniture. / I eat my rice. It's very good rice. I made it myself." Funniest line in the chapter so far, and it's also sad.

**Image check (image_02.jpg):**
- Copper hair: yes, though it reads more orange-ginger than copper. Fine.
- "Cut neat at her shoulders and tucked behind one ear": the hair in the picture is past the shoulders, layered and loose on both sides, and I can't see it tucked behind either ear. Mild mismatch.
- The class rumour was that she's "super tall. Taller than the vice-principal!" Nothing in the picture tells me she's tall; she looks average height, and with only seated students in the foreground there's no scale. If her height matters later, the one illustration of her doesn't sell it.
- She's waving in the picture; the text has her counting on her fingers. Not a contradiction (different beat), just noting it.
- The wall clock's numbers look scrambled (I can see a 9 and an 8 on the upper left, 4 and 5 on the right), so it doesn't read as a real time. Tiny, but noticeable once seen.
- Three of the four classmates in the foreground have the same long straight black hair from behind. Generic, but it's crowd filler.
- Cherry tree and mountain out the window: nice continuity with the walk to school.

**Small things I noticed:**
- Narration calls her "Mizuno," the name box says "Natsuki." Same as Tokiwa/Ryōsuke. It works here because I saw both names in her self-introduction, but it means Kōhei's narration and the UI are on different name systems.
- "Club starts Wednesday" twice in the chapter (once to Nanoha, once to Natsuki). I think that's deliberate (parallel), and it works.

## Checkpoint 4 (after part 4: Nanoha interlude, the two houses at night; end of chapter)

**Who does Kōhei end up with, and how sure?** Nanoha, 50%. Natsuki, 45%. Someone else, 5%. Back to a coin flip with Nanoha slightly ahead. The interlude gives Nanoha her own interior voice, which tells me she's a real contender and not just the friend who loses gracefully. And the closing image (her window lighting up across a gap "so narrow you could pass a book through it", and "I stand there for a while longer than the garbage requires") is the chapter choosing to end on her. Natsuki, though, is the one the supernatural plot is pointed at.

**Who do I want him to end up with?** Nanoha, 7/10. Natsuki, 4/10. I like Natsuki more after the interlude (the "different smile… It looked like it cost her something" shows the six-time new girl has a cost too), but the interlude also made me feel for Nanoha a lot more. "She'd had practice." got me.

**Who is the woman in the dream?** Natsuki, 50%. Nanoha, 40%. Someone else / no present-day counterpart, 10%.
- Natsuki's "Does he ever seem like… someone you've met before?" is the most direct pointer in the chapter. She feels it too, which says she has some past-life link with him.
- But I've now noticed the dream is set at *home*: he's held the cut closed "the whole way home from training," and she's there, waiting, saying "You're late." The girl who is always in his home, who's been waiting "sixteen years," is Nanoha. "She says it like she's been waiting a very long time" fits Nanoha's whole situation uncomfortably well. So my current theory is that both girls are tied to the past life, possibly in different roles (Natsuki as someone from his sword life, Nanoha as the one at home), and the story will make us wonder which of them was the woman with the cloth.

**What does the dream have to do with the present?** Confident now (75%) that it's a past life Kōhei shares with at least one of these girls, centred on the Ōzakura tree that Nanoha's grandpa looks after. Saturday's sweeping is clearly the next trigger.

**What's going on / what kind of story?** A gentle reincarnation love triangle where both girls are good people and like each other, with a grief-and-caretaking family story underneath. The emotional question is "fate vs. the person who was always there," and the practical question is whether Kōhei ever lets anyone help him. That's a good combination; the domestic stuff is the strongest material.

**Lines and moments that drove my guesses:**
- "Natsuki dropped into Kōhei's chair, knowing perfectly well whose it was" Natsuki is a little bit staking territory, or testing Nanoha.
- "Nanoha said it as lightly as she knew how. She'd had practice." Best line of the interlude.
- "Does he ever seem like… someone you've met before?" / "I have met him before. Every day. For sixteen years." Great exchange, and Nanoha's answer is both a joke and a wound.
- "It was fine. / It was fine." Effective, though the device is familiar.
- "She'd decided it firmly, the way you shut a window against the wind. / She did not let herself think about why it had needed deciding at all." The first sentence is lovely. The second spells out what the first already showed.
- "She says it a little too clearly, the way you say something you'd like written down." Good.
- "That one's number four. 'Somebody wants to fight me.'" / "What's number one?" / "Secret." Number one is presumably the face he makes at her. Cute, and it leaves a hook.
- The garbage payoff: Dad's "Mon/Thu" line from the morning comes back. Pickup moved to Tuesday/Friday and he still follows the handwriting. This is the best callback in the chapter; it lands without explanation.

**Small things I noticed:**
- Natsuki's "Someone who knows which drawer sticks in your kitchen" is exactly the detail from Nanoha's morning (the drawer she bumps with her hip). It works as irony, but it's a very neat coincidence; Natsuki has no reason to pick that specific image. It made me notice the writer's hand.
- Nanoha calls him "Kōhei" in front of Natsuki and in the serious moment on the street, then "Kō-chan" at the door. If that's deliberate, it's nice. If not, it's an inconsistency; I read it as deliberate.
- "You're taking out the trash on the wrong day." For a non-Japanese reader, putting the bag out Monday night for a Tuesday pickup sounds like the *right* time (that's what people do in the US). The joke still works because of his reply, but the "wrong day" may confuse anyone who doesn't know Japan's morning-of rule.
- The "super tall" rumour about Natsuki is never confirmed or denied in the text, and the illustration doesn't show it either. The height contrast with tiny Nanoha (who "has to stretch to reach" his collar) is set up and then dropped.

# Final answers

**When I became fairly sure (70%+) of anything the checkpoints asked:**
- That Nanoha is in love with him: "She went looking for mine before her own." (end of part 2). The interlude's "She'd had practice." took it to about 95%.
- That the dream is a past life tied to someone in the present: "Does he ever seem like… someone you've met before?" (part 4). The earlier "Her grin stutters. Just for a second." got me to about 65%; Natsuki's question pushed it over 70%.
- Who he ends up with, and who the dream woman is: I never reached 70% on either. I finished at Nanoha 50/Natsuki 45 for the ending and Natsuki 50/Nanoha 40 for the dream woman. For a first chapter, that's probably where the author wants me.

**Hints that felt heavy-handed, too obvious or too coy:**
- Heavy: the interlude tells us Nanoha's feelings three times in a row ("She'd had practice." / "It was fine. It was fine." / "She did not let herself think about why it had needed deciding at all."). Any one of these works; all three together is overkill. I'd cut the last one, because the shut-window sentence already says it.
- Slightly heavy: Natsuki gets four past-life tells in one chapter (grin stutter, Kōhei's pen grip, "writing it on the inside of her wrist", "someone you've met before?"). The last one comes close to stating the premise in chapter 1. The "very intense jet lag" deflection rescues it, but I'd consider holding that question back a chapter.
- Too neat: Natsuki's "which drawer sticks in your kitchen."
- Familiar: "Her hands stay a second longer than the collar strictly needs. / Probably a stubborn crease." The dense-narrator gag works, but it's the oldest one in the genre.
- Too coy: the Ōzakura. It's the title-sounding word and the apparent location of the dream, and the chapter never tells me what it is (a tree? a shrine? where?). One descriptive line on the walk would let it carry weight. Also, "Hoshino Kitchen" (his job? Nanoha's family business?) is never placed.
- Accidental clue: "A bike bell rings somewhere behind us and doesn't pass." It reads as foreshadowing and goes nowhere.

**Dialogue that felt lively:**
- Nanoha's class-assignment conspiracy ("There have been meetings." / "The pool turns green." / "The pool is green." / "That's what they want you to think.")
- Ryōsuke's hot-dog argument, especially "That's not a counterargument. That's a timestamp."
- Akane: "And I slept yesterday too. Two nights in a row. Medical science is baffled." and the "I'm already making two." / "…That's sort of my point." exchange.
- "You thought it in a short-person direction."
- Natsuki: "Oh, he's cocky. Good. I hate practicing with polite people." and "Very intense jet lag."
- "That one's number four. 'Somebody wants to fight me.'"

**Up to 8 flat lines, and why:**
1. Natsuki: "Please take care of me." Literal translationese for *yoroshiku*. Her intro is vivid and then ends on a stiff phrase that sounds odd in English. "Looking forward to it" or similar would keep her voice.
2. Natsuki: "I'm Mizuno Natsuki. It's nice to meet you all." Realistic, but it's the most generic line she has. It comes right after a big narrated entrance, so it deflates it a little.
3. Natsuki: "That's really nice." Filler; the next line does all the work.
4. Nanoha: "A transfer student?" Exists only to cue Rika's next line.
5. Nanoha: "Good morning, Rika-chan." Flat response to Rika's huge entrance. It may be meant as deadpan contrast, but it reads as nothing.
6. Kōhei: "Good morning, Tokiwa." Filler, and it's where I first tripped on the Tokiwa/Ryōsuke naming.
7. Kōhei: "Club doesn't start until Wednesday. First week." Pure schedule exposition. The schedule appears three times in the chapter (calendar, walk, Natsuki). The second time is purposeful ("So you'd hear yourself say it."); this setup line is the weak link.
8. Ōno-sensei: "All right. Settle down." Stock teacher line (his next sentence is good, though).

Also, "So." opens a line three times between two characters (Nanoha: "So. Class assignments."; Natsuki: "So. You're Fujisawa." and "So. You and Fujisawa."). It's a shared tic that blurs their voices a bit.

**With the names covered, could I tell the characters apart?**
- Rika: instantly (caps, exclamation chains, "Oh! Oh oh oh").
- Ryōsuke: instantly (pedantic, structural arguments).
- Natsuki: mostly. She's pushy, warm and self-deprecating, and she asks for permission while saying she'll do it anyway ("Is that all right? I'm going to do it anyway."). The "So." openers are the only overlap.
- Nanoha vs. Akane: this is the weak spot. Both are deadpan teasers, and both get the same running joke about Kōhei's face (Akane: "Stop doing the face." / "The doctor face." and "I saw his face."; Nanoha: "You have several. I've catalogued them."). With names covered, I could easily give Akane's "Medical science is baffled" to Nanoha, or Nanoha's "It's a sickness" to Akane. I'd give one of them the face bit and move the other's humour somewhere else (e.g. Akane more blunt and sulky, Nanoha more elaborate and absurdist, which she already leans toward).
- Kōhei: his dry one-liners ("The pool is green.", "Looks can be deceiving.") are in the same register as Nanoha's and Ōno-sensei's. The whole Shirasawa cast shares one deadpan comic voice; Natsuki, Rika and Ryōsuke are the ones who break it.

**Mismatches between the text and the illustrations:**
- image_02: the text says her hair is "cut neat at her shoulders and tucked behind one ear"; the art shows loose, layered hair past the shoulders, not tucked on either side.
- image_02: the "super tall" rumour isn't supported. She reads as average height, and there's no figure for scale.
- image_02: the wall clock's numerals look scrambled.
- image_01: the forearm and hand look like a mature man's (thick, veined, weathered), not a teenager's. That works if it's deliberately a past-life body, but it's a mismatch if the dream is meant to be ambiguous about who he is. The grip is also briefly confusing: his fist and her lower hand crowd together, and it took me a second to rule out a third hand.
- image_01: the river from the narration isn't in the picture. That's fine, since it's described as a sound.

**Other small problems:**
- Name systems: Kōhei's narration uses surnames ("Tokiwa," "Mizuno") while the name boxes use given names ("Ryōsuke," "Natsuki"). With Ryōsuke there's no on-screen moment linking the two names, so a casual reader will stumble once.
- Inline glosses in dialogue, "tsuka (hilt)" and "shodan (first dan)", are clunky inside spoken lines. A text box reads them as part of the speech.
- "Wrong day" for the trash (see checkpoint 4) may confuse non-Japanese readers.

**Would I keep reading?** Yes. I'd open chapter 2.
- What hooked me: the domestic material. The two lunch boxes, Dad's handwriting on the calendar, the closed fusuma and "That's an answer. Sort of.", the garbage callback. Then Nanoha as a fully realised person who gets her own interior voice. Then the fact that the two girls like each other, which makes the triangle hurt. The dream's "You're late" and the Saturday visit to the Ōzakura give me a concrete reason to turn the page.
- What would make me stop: if the triangle settles into a standard "dense protagonist + two girls" loop for several chapters; if the past-life plot simply hands Kōhei to whoever turns out to be "fated" and Nanoha's sixteen years are written off; or if Mom and Akane fade into backdrop. Their scenes are the best writing here, and I'd feel cheated if they turned out to be just a sad-backstory garnish.

# Reader 3 notes: cold read, Chapter 1 ("The House Next Door")

## Checkpoint 1 (after part 1: the dream prologue + title card)

**Who does Kōhei end up with, and how sure?** I haven't met him, or anyone else, yet. The only name I have is from the instructions. If I have to bet on genre priors: whoever the "house next door" belongs to. A title like that in a school romance usually means a childhood friend or a new neighbor girl. Low confidence: 20%, and only on "the neighbor," with no name.

**Who do I want him to end up with?** No preference yet. I'm mildly curious about the dream woman, because she's the only person on screen. Strength: 10/100.

**Who is the woman in the dream, and how sure?** My working guess is a past-life lover. The period markers point that way: katana, striped kimono, cloth bandage, "home from training." Her present-day reincarnation will turn up at school or next door. A weaker alternative: a real childhood memory of an older girl, a mother or a nanny, dressed up by the dream. Past-life lover: 55%. Someone from his real childhood: 25%. Something else: 20%.

**What does the dream have to do with the present?** It's almost certainly the frame for a reincarnation or "fated" romance. "She's been waiting a very long time" plus "I kept them waiting far too long" reads like a promise from a past life that the present-day plot will pay off, Your Name / Kimi no Na wa-style. The cut wrist and the bandage feel like a recognition token. I expect someone in the present to bandage his hand or to have a matching scar, and that will be the "it's her" moment.

**What's going on, and what kind of story is this becoming?** A school romance with a supernatural or past-life spine. It may be a love triangle where the "obvious" girl and the "fated" girl aren't the same person. The genre usually has a decoy: the heroine you meet first versus the heroine the dream points to.

**Lines, images and moments that drove my guesses:**
- "She says it like she's been waiting a very long time." It is the strongest line in the prologue and does the most work.
- "That I kept them waiting far too long." The gender-neutral "them" is interesting: a deliberate hedge. Is the dream person who I think it is?
- "Hold still." / "Give me your hand…" A caretaker-scold energy: tsundere-adjacent, or an older-sister type.
- Illustration (image_01): the katana hilt, the striped kimono and obi, and the cherry branch put it in a historical setting, not modern. The arm in the foreground looks like a grown man's (veins, weathered skin), not a kid's, even though he's had the dream "since I was small." That supports "past life" over "childhood memory."
- "Petals." Sakura on its own line: the sakura motif will matter.

**Early craft notes:**
- The prologue is tight and atmospheric. Three escalating "She says it like…" lines is a nice rhythm.
- The illustration: I had to look twice at the hands. There's a large, rough hand on the right gripping over the bandage from above. It reads like a third person's hand, or the narrator's own hand bent back at an unnatural angle. With the woman's two hands also on the cloth, the count looks like three hands on one wrist. Minor, but it pulled me out for a second.
- Text says "the sound of a river"; no river is visible. That's fine, since it's sound.
- Text says the cut is "across the back of" the wrist; the blood spot on the bandage sits on top of the wrist, which matches.

## Checkpoint 2 (after part 2: morning at home, the river walk, class lists)

**Who does Kōhei end up with, and how sure?** Nanoha, at 50%. The unseen tall transfer student, at 35%. Someone else, 15%. # Final answers

## When I became fairly sure (70%+) of anything the checkpoints asked
- **That the dream is a past-life or "met before" frame the present story will pay off:** at Natsuki's "Does he ever seem like… someone you've met before?" (part 4). Before that, the grin stutter ("Her grin stutters. Just for a second.") got me to about 60%.

## Hints that felt heavy-handed, too obvious or too coy
**Heavy or too obvious:**
- Natsuki: "Does he ever seem like… someone you've met before?" It's the chapter's biggest mystery hook, stated out loud on day one. I'd cut it, or hold it for a later chapter. The grin stutter alone was a good, readable hook.
- "Her hands stay a second longer than the collar strictly needs. / Probably a stubborn crease." This is the dense-narrator wink at the reader. It's genre-standard, but self-aware enough to tip into cute. With the later "longer than the garbage requires," it starts to feel like a formula.
- "I realize I've been holding my pen hard enough to hurt." It signals "this girl matters" a little loudly.
- "She repeats it like she's writing it on the inside of her wrist." Wrist equals dream, and I noticed the authorial hand. It's borderline: it works because it's a natural simile (students do write on their hands), but it's one of several hand/wrist pings in a row.
- Rika's "Hm. Hmmm. … A completely neutral hmmm." This is fine as comedy, but it's the friend character announcing the romance subplot.

**Well judged:**
- Dad's handwriting on the calendar, Mom behind the fusuma, Akane's "room starts tilting."
- The Ōzakura mentioned once in passing.
- The dream's "them."

**Too coy:** Nothing badly. The one place I wanted a touch more is the Ōzakura itself. It's the story's title word, and it gets one line of dialogue and no image. One sentence of Kōhei's narration about what or where it is would anchor it.

## Flat lines (up to 8), and why
1. Ryōsuke: "—which is why, structurally, a hot dog is a sandwich." The hot-dog/sandwich debate is a worn internet meme. It makes his introduction feel borrowed. His delivery is good; the topic isn't his.
2. Rika: "Two-B! You're in Two-B! I'm in Two-B! It's destiny! It's in the stars! I'm making us matching keychains!" This is the stock genki-friend entrance, with nothing in it only Rika would say. The crowned frog clip does more characterization than this line.
3. Natsuki: "I also really love horror movies, so if anyone wants to watch one with me, I'll be grateful forever." It reads like a character-sheet trait being entered. "Kendo" gets a payoff three lines later; "horror movies" just sits there. A more specific example would help.
4. Natsuki: "This school's nice. It feels… lived in. Does that make sense?" It's generic, and the narration has already shown her noticing things better (the trophy-case rectangle, the "Oh" at the mountain window).
5. Natsuki: "That's really nice." A filler beat. The next line ("Someone who knows which drawer sticks in your kitchen") does all the work.
6. Kōhei: "It's fine. I like being busy." It's the overworked-protagonist stock line. It's meant to be self-deceiving, but in this phrasing every reader has heard it.
7. Nanoha: "Good. She noticed the squeaky stairs. Both of them." A recap of the interlude we just read, so there's no new information.
8. Natsuki: "I'm Mizuno Natsuki. It's nice to meet you all." It's a stock self-introduction. That's acceptable as convention, but it's the first thing the second heroine says, and "six times now? So I'm basically a professional" should lead instead.

## With names covered, could I tell the characters apart, and who sounds like whom?
- **Natsuki:** easily distinct. She's forward, bright and quick to jump ("Okay, you're fun," "I'm going to do it anyway"), and she drops honorifics ("Fujisawa").
- **Rika:** easily distinct. Caps, stretched vowels ("Nano-chaaaan!"), exclamation chains.
- **Ryōsuke:** easily distinct. Pedantic, with "structurally" and "theoretically." His speaker tag says "Ryōsuke" while the narration calls him "Tokiwa." On a cold read I had to infer they're the same person from "Tokiwa has arrived…" right after his first line. One appositive ("Tokiwa Ryōsuke has arrived…") would fix that.
- **Ōno-sensei:** distinct (weary adult).
- **Nanoha and Akane sound alike.** This is the real problem. Both use dry, deadpan, mock-scientific escalation ("Medical science is baffled" / "It's a sickness" / "There have been meetings"). Both tease Kōhei about his face. Both push back on being looked after with a one-line zinger. With tags covered, I could only tell them apart by honorifics ("Onii-chan" vs "Kō-chan") and topic. Give Akane a different comic register (blunter, more younger-sibling brat, or more clinical) or give Nanoha's teasing a different target.
- **Kōhei sounds a little like Nanoha too.** He's terse and deadpan ("The pool is green," "That's what the attendance sheet says," "Is that in the welcome packet?"). He's shorter and flatter than her, so he's distinguishable, but the chapter has three characters running the same dry-wit engine at different speeds.

## Would I keep reading? What hooked me, and what would make me stop
**Yes, I'd keep reading.** It's the best kind of chapter 1 for this genre: low-key, specific and emotionally layered.

**What hooked me:**
- Natsuki's grin stutter and the unanswered "met before" question.
- Nanoha's restrained, sixteen-year love, especially the interlude.
- The grief in Kōhei's house, told through objects: the calendar handwriting, the fusuma, the floorboard creak.
- The Saturday Ōzakura sweep and the Wednesday kendo practice, set up as obvious next set pieces. I want to see both.

**What would make me stop:**
- Nanoha turned into a pure sacrificial "loser childhood friend" with no agency.

- The family grief used as wallpaper instead of plot.
- Natsuki's recognition resolved with a cheap "we actually met as kids at a tournament" reveal.
- Kōhei's density turning into misunderstanding farce. The "longer than X requires" wink can't carry many more chapters.

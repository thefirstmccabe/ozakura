# Reader 2 — cold read, Chapter 1 "The House Next Door"

## Checkpoint 1 (after part 1)

**Who I think Kōhei ends up with:** Nanoha, 40%. She's the only girl in play so far besides his sister, so she's the default. But this is a childhood friend who walks in without knocking, knows which cupboard has the mugs, and fixes his collar on the first page. Genre habit says that girl is usually set up to lose to someone new. I'm assuming a newcomer is coming (maybe tied to the dream), so I'm holding back on Nanoha.

**Who I want him to end up with:** Nanoha, about 65/100. She's the best thing in the part. The "meetings in a small room with no windows" bit and "You thought it in a short-person direction" made me like her right away. The want is real but not strong yet, because I have no one to compare her to.

**Who I think the woman in the dream is:** I can't tell, ~5% on any guess. **No woman appears in the opening sequence.** I get a foggy rural crossroads, a close-up of a hand gripping a katana hilt (dark zip-up jacket sleeve), fog rolling down a road like a wave, then the crossroads again. No face, no figure, no voice, no text. The hand looks more like a man's than a woman's to me: broad knuckles, plain sleeve. If the question assumes I've "met" a dream woman, part 1 doesn't deliver her. My best guesses: (a) the sword hand is Kōhei's own (he does kendo), (b) it's his dad, (c) it's someone connected to the shrine. None of these feel solid.

**What the dream has to do with the present:** Only setting echoes so far. The fog and mountains match Shirasawa ("wind comes down off the mountains," "the Tanzawa"). The power poles and block wall look like the same kind of rural road he walks. The sword echoes kendo. I half-suspect it's about Dad: he wrote the garbage line "last spring," and nobody has written over it. Mom stays behind a closed fusuma, and Nanoha's family started bringing food "about a year now." Dad is probably dead or gone, and a foggy road could be where it happened. Low confidence.

**What I think is going on / what kind of story:** A quiet slice-of-life school romance with a grief undercurrent and probably a supernatural thread through the shrine and the ancient cherry tree (the Ōzakura, "older than anyone's records" — that's the title, so it matters). Kōhei is the boy holding the house together: he makes the lunches, runs the whiteboard, watches his sister for dizzy spells ("the room starts tilting on her"; red circle for "Akane's clinic day"), and talks to a mother who barely answers. Nanoha is the neighbor who has quietly been propping the family up. I expect the romance to be about whether he lets anyone carry anything for him.

## Hints that felt heavy-handed, too obvious, or too coy

**Heavy-handed / too obvious**
1. **"I feel like I've met him somewhere before. I just can't place it."** (Natsuki, part 3). The grin stutter in part 2 already did this job, and did it better. Saying it outright on day one is the most stock line in the genre, and it shrinks the mystery. I'd rather sit with the stutter for a chapter or two.
2. **"Someone who knows which drawer sticks in your kitchen."** A stranger happens to name the exact detail from Kōhei's kitchen in part 1. I could see the author's hand. Let Natsuki say something general and let the narration supply the drawer.
3. **The Fukuda reveal is explained twice:** "She went looking for mine before her own." + "I don't know why that's the thing that sticks with me all through the opening ceremony." The first sentence is enough. The second is the dense-protagonist trope said out loud.
4. **"She did not let herself think about why it had needed deciding at all."** Borderline. It's a good line, but it's the narration telling me Nanoha is jealous right after "It was fine. / It was fine." had already shown me.
5. **The triple "waiting"** at the end ("waiting a very long time" … "someone was waiting for me" … "kept them waiting far too long") lands the theme hard. Acceptable as a chapter closer, but it's close to the limit. Also, the dream says "She" three times, then the waking lines switch to "someone… them." I get that it's meant to be the memory blurring, but it reads like coyness about a gender the text already gave away. Either keep "her" or make the blur explicit.

**Too coy / opaque**
6. **The prologue (image_01–03)**: no text, no woman, no figure except a sword hand. It's a modern-day setting that doesn't match the closing dream's period setting. It isn't a coy clue so much as one I can't read: I don't know whose it is, whether it's a dream, or how it relates to the kimono dream. If it's meant to be "the dream," the two need a shared element (river, petals, the same sleeve). If it's a flash-forward, a caption or a sound cue would help.

**Well-calibrated (for contrast):** Natsuki's 水 on the inside of her wrist rhyming with the dream's cut "on the inside of" his wrist. I didn't notice it until afterward, which is exactly right. The same goes for "I raise my left hand" setting up the callus line, and Dad's garbage line paying off in part 4.

## Flat lines (8)

1. **Akane: "Geez, Onii-chan! Every single morning!"** Stock little-sister register ("Geez," exclamation marks). She gets one genuinely good line ("…You always are.") and the rest is template.
2. **Akane: "And I slept! Super well! I didn't wake up once!"** Without the speaker tag this could be Rika. Same bubbly exclamation cadence.
3. **Natsuki: "I'm Mizuno Natsuki. It's nice to meet you all."** Boilerplate opener for a girl introduced as "stepping onto a floor she's already won on." Her first line should carry some of that edge.
4. **Natsuki: "Looking forward to this year."** Her recovery line right after the stutter. It's pure filler at the most charged moment of her intro. A slightly-off line would let us see her cover.
5. **Natsuki: "Takes one to know one."** A cliché capping off a sharp, specific observation (the callus). The callus line is the punch; this deflates it.
6. **Kōhei: "We'll find out on the floor."** Stock rival-banter line. It's fine in function but sounds like every sports manga.
7. **Natsuki: "So. You're Fujisawa."** Generic opener, and she reuses the "So." tic with Nanoha ("So. What about you and Fujisawa?"). It starts to feel like a writer's habit rather than hers.
8. **Nanoha: "It's old. The pipes sound like a ghost."** Pure function. Natsuki just tossed her a horror-movie setup, and Nanoha, who invents conspiracies about class assignments, plays it completely straight. A missed chance for her to be funny in front of the rival.

## Would I keep reading?

**Yes.** What hooked me:
- **The household.** Dad's handwriting on the whiteboard, paid off with the garbage day in part 4, Mom behind the fusuma answering with a creak, Akane's "…You always are." That's the best writing in the chapter, and it's why I care about Kōhei.
- **Nanoha's voice.** She's funny in a specific, recognizable way, and her feelings are shown more than stated (mostly).
- **Two girls who genuinely like each other.** Natsuki's smaller smile "that looked like it cost her something" and Nanoha deciding to like her "the way you shut a window against the wind." A triangle where nobody's the villain is more interesting than a catfight.
- **The wrist clue.** Once I caught the 水 / inner-wrist rhyme, I wanted to know what else was planted.

**What would make me stop:**
- If fate decides the romance: Natsuki wins because the dream says so, not because of who she is, or Nanoha is written as a designated loser whose pain is just atmosphere.
- If Kōhei stays at "I don't know why that's the thing that sticks with me" density for several chapters. One more of those and he becomes the oblivious-protagonist trope.
- If the prologue's modern fog-and-sword thread turns into an action plot that pushes out the household story, or never gets explained.
- If Akane stays a stock imouto. She's set up with a real condition and a real line; she should get a real voice.

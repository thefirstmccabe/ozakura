# Reader 3: cold-read notes, Chapter 1 "The House Next Door"

## Checkpoint 1 (after part1.txt)

**Who I think Kōhei ends up with:** Nanoha, 45%. She's the only romantic option on screen so far, so that number is mostly a default. I'm holding back for two reasons. Genre habit says the childhood friend who does the collar and keeps score loses the first-chapter lead to whoever turns up next. And the chapter is called "The House Next Door". Nanoha already comes in through the back door without knocking, so a title about the house next door makes me expect a *new* neighbor, or a reveal about the house she lives in. 55% goes to "someone not yet introduced".

**Who I want him to end up with:** Nanoha, about 65%. She's the best thing in this part. The class-assignment conspiracy bit is funny, and "So you'd hear yourself say it" lands a real point without a speech. I like her, but I'm not attached to her yet.

**The woman in the dream:** I can't answer this from the page. The cold open is four wordless illustrations: a foggy rural crossroads with a convex traffic mirror, a close-up of a hand gripping a sheathed katana, a wall of fog rolling down a road like a wave, then the crossroads again. No woman is visible. Nothing says it's a dream: no caption, no narration, no "I". If a woman is meant to be there, she isn't readable. My best guess is that the hand belongs to her. It's fairly neutral, but the dark zip-up jacket reads as male to me. Guess: Kōhei's mother, or a stranger tied to the shrine. 15% at most, and that confidence is the low part.

**What the dream has to do with the present:** Guesses only. (a) Kendo: Kōhei does kendo, so the sword ties the images to him. Maybe it's his hand, or a future version of him. (b) The crossroads with a traffic mirror looks like an accident site, and Dad is gone as of "last spring". "He wrote it last spring. I've never written over it." So he's dead or he left. A road and a traffic mirror make me think Dad died in an accident there. (c) The fog rolling down like a living thing looks supernatural, and the town has a giant ancient tree at the shrine, which is apparently the title. I'm expecting a tree-spirit or time-loop layer under the romcom.

**What I think is going on / what kind of story:** A slice-of-life grief story under a school romcom. Kōhei is a teenage caretaker. Dad has been gone a year, Mom shuts herself in her room, and his sister Akane has a dizziness condition with clinic visits ("the way she does when the room starts tilting on her"). He runs the house off a whiteboard. Nanoha, from next door, has quietly been feeding them since Dad went. Her grandfather looks after the shrine with the Ōzakura. The schedule is set up so that Saturday afternoon is his only free time, and Saturday is the shrine sweep under the big tree. I expect the plot to happen there. Tone is Clannad-ish family weight with Horimiya-style banter.

**Lines and images that drove my guesses:**
- "All of it except the bottom line, which still says 'Garbage: burnables, Mon/Thu' in Dad's. He wrote it last spring. I've never written over it." This is the best line in the part and drives my whole read on the family.
- "The fusuma to Mom's room is closed. It usually is." / "That's an answer. Sort of."
- "Akane: '…You always are.' She says it to the rice cooker, not to me." Akane sees more than she lets on.
- "She's been bringing food over for about a year now." The timing matches Dad. Nanoha's family stepped in.
- "Her hands stay a second longer than the collar strictly needs." / "She walks a little faster after that." Classic signals that she's into him and he's oblivious, or pretending to be.
- "Nanoha walks on the river side." This is stated flatly with no explanation, so I noticed it and filed it as possibly meaningful (is she protecting him from the water?).
- Ōzakura: "one cherry tree older than anyone's records". The work seems to be named after it, so it matters.
- Image_04 (collar CG) is lovely and sells the pair.

**Problems noticed (part 1):**
1. **The cold open doesn't read as a dream, and there's no woman in it.** Four illustrations, no text, no voice. A first-time reader gets a moody road and a sword, nothing more. If the chapter means "Kōhei dreams of a woman", that isn't on screen yet. Even one line of narration or a silhouette would anchor it.
2. **Image_04 vs. text, position:** the text says "Nanoha walks on the river side", but in the CG the river is behind Kōhei on the left and Nanoha stands on the far side, away from the water. She had stepped in front of him, so it can be explained, but the text made a point of where she walks, so the swap is noticeable.
3. **Image_04 vs. text, "up on her toes":** her feet are out of frame and her posture looks flat-footed and relaxed. The height gap (her eyes at his chin) also looks smaller than "needed the extra inches since middle school" plus the short-person joke suggests. It's minor, but the joke depends on her being small and she doesn't look especially small here.

**Problems noticed (part 3):**
8. **"I feel like I've met him somewhere before" is on the nose.** Coming after the grin stutter and the pen grip, this third signal states the mystery outright. It's a stock line, and it moves the hint from intriguing to telegraphed. The Sendai-tournament follow-up rescues some of it. A margin note or translator's footnote style would sit better than a mid-line gloss.

# Final answers

## When I became fairly sure (70%+) of anything
- **That the dream is a past life, and the story is a reincarnation romance under the cherry tree:** at the dream in part 4, with "She says it like she's been waiting a very long time." together with image_07 (kimono, period sleeve, the same katana as the cold open). Before that I was at about 40% on "supernatural layer of some kind" and had no specific shape for it.
- **Ending, who I want, and the dream woman:** I never reached 70% on any of them. My highest was Nanoha 55% for the ending and about 50% for the dream woman. On the ending, the chapter keeps both girls credible, and I think that's deliberate and working. On the dream woman, it may be too balanced for a chapter-one hook. I finished with no firm guess, only two competing lists of clues. If the author wants the reader to suspect one girl in chapter 1, neither is ahead.
- (Outside the checkpoint questions: I was 90%+ that Nanoha loves Kōhei by "Her hands stay a second longer than the collar strictly needs." That's intended and fine.)

## Hints that felt heavy-handed, too obvious or too coy
- **Too obvious:** Natsuki's "I feel like I've met him somewhere before. I just can't place it." This is the most stock line in the chapter, and it comes after the grin stutter and the pen grip had already said it better without words.
- **Too obvious:** "She went looking for mine before her own." The narration does the reader's arithmetic for them.
- **Slightly stock:** the closing "That someone was waiting for me. / That I kept them waiting far too long." It's the standard chapter-one VN ending cadence. The three "She says it like…" lines just before it are fresher and would make a stronger last beat.
- **Too coy:** the cold open. With no text, no voice, no woman and no "dream" label, and nothing referring back to it by the end of the chapter, it isn't coy so much as unreadable. The checkpoint question assumes I saw a woman in a dream, and in the cold open I didn't.
- **Pleasantly coy and rewarded:** "Nanoha walks on the river side" (paid off by "The sound of a river, close by"), 水 on the wrist / Mizuno / the wrist cut, and "What's number one?" "Secret." These are the right size.
- **Well judged:** Akane's dizziness ("the way she does when the room starts tilting on her"), Mom behind the fusuma, and Dad via handwriting. Grief shown through objects, never explained, and it works.

## Flat lines (up to 8)
1. **Kōhei: "Good morning, Tokiwa."** He's stiff and formal with a friend, and it doesn't match how loose he is with Nanoha.
2. **Akane: "Geez, Onii-chan! Every single morning!"** This is stock little-sister voice. Akane's lines are generic imouto throughout ("Yaaay", "Onii-chan" in almost every line). Her one real line is "…You always are."
3. **Akane: "Nanoha-chan brought kinpira!"** It repeats what we just watched happen and reads as filler between jokes.
4. **Nanoha: "It's old. The pipes sound like a ghost."** It exists only to set up "Perfect." Nanoha is sparkless in the whole interlude dialogue, though the narration around her is excellent.
5. **Kōhei: "I don't have a face."** The wording is awkward (see problem 13) rather than flat, but it deflates the exchange.
6. **Nanoha: "She's nice, Kōhei. I like her."** It restates what the interlude just told us ("Nanoha had decided that she liked Mizuno Natsuki"). As a spoken echo it's in character, but on the page it's redundant. "She noticed the eleventh step" already does that job.
7. **Natsuki: "Looking forward to this year."** It's a generic recovery line. It's plausibly deliberate (she's covering the stutter), but it's the one moment her voice goes generic.

## With names covered, could I tell them apart?
Mostly, yes.
- **Rika** is unmistakable: exclamation marks, doubled words ("Nothing! Nothing!", "Oh! Oh oh oh"). She's possibly too one-note. She needs one quieter beat soon or she'll become noise.
- **Natsuki** is distinct: blunt and casual, physical, opens with "So." ("So. You're Fujisawa." / "So. What about you and Fujisawa?"), and uses sporting challenge-talk.
- **Ōno-sensei** is distinct from context and his dry bureaucratic tone.
- **Nanoha vs. Tokiwa: these blur.** Both use deadpan escalation built as a short declarative plus an absurd extension. "She can't measure burdock. It's a sickness." and "That isn't an opinion. It's thermodynamics." could swap speakers. Nanoha's mock-bureaucratic bits ("That's what it says in the minutes") are also very close to Tokiwa's "I'll need a diagram." Without names I would have misattributed at least one.
- **Akane vs. Rika:** both are bouncy and exclamatory ("Yaaay" / "Nothing! Nothing!"). Akane is identifiable only because she says "Onii-chan".
- **Kōhei vs. Natsuki** in the sparring exchange: "We'll find out on the floor." could be either. That fits two kendo people, so it's acceptable.

## Would I keep reading?
**Yes.** I'd read chapter 2 tonight.

**What hooked me:**
- Dad's handwriting on the whiteboard and the garbage-day payoff. That's the emotional spine, and it's done entirely with objects.
- Nanoha's third-person interlude, especially "She'd decided it firmly, the way you shut a window against the wind. / She did not let herself think about why it had needed deciding at all."
- A triangle in which both girls are decent and likable, so I care which way it goes rather than rooting against one of them.
- Image_07 turning the dream into a period piece. That moved the story from "romcom with a weird prologue" to "reincarnation story", and I want the shape of the old promise.

**What would make me stop:**
- If the cold open stays unexplained for several chapters. Right now it's dead weight at the very start, which is where a first-time reader decides whether to stay.
- If the past-life layer arrives through a shrine-grandpa exposition dump.
- If Nanoha turns into the graceful loser who only smiles and steps aside. The interlude hints she might, and she's the best character in the book.
- If Akane's illness turns into a cheap hospital-melodrama lever.
- If Rika stays at full volume for every line.

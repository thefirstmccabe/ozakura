# Reader 1: cold read notes (Chapter 1, "The House Next Door")

## Checkpoint 1 (after part 1)

**Who I think Kōhei ends up with:** Nanoha, 55%. She's the only girl on the board besides his little sister, the chapter is literally titled "The House Next Door," and the collar CG is pure romance-cover material. I'm holding it at 55% and not higher because I've read enough of these to know the childhood friend who's already in the kitchen on page one is very often the one who *doesn't* win. A new girl (transfer student, someone at the shrine, someone from the opening fog) almost certainly shows up soon.

**Who I want him to end up with:** Nanoha, fairly strongly (7/10). The banter is the best thing in the part. "You thought it in a short-person direction" and the minutes from the windowless room made me laugh. She's also clearly been holding his household together quietly for a year, and that gives her weight beyond being cute.

**Who the woman in the dream is:** Honestly, I didn't see a woman. The opening is four wordless images: a foggy rural junction with a road mirror, a close-up of a hand gripping a real katana (sleeve of a dark zip-up jacket), a road with fog rolling in like a wave, then the junction again. No woman anywhere. If the question assumes I saw one, the opening didn't deliver her. If forced to guess who the dream is "about": maybe Nanoha (shrine family, ancient cherry tree, feels like the supernatural thread would run through her), 15%. More likely someone not introduced yet.

**What the dream has to do with the present:** No idea yet, and it's not labeled as a dream. It reads as a cold open / foreshadowing: fog off the mountains, a sword, a junction where something is about to happen. The katana is a real sword, not a kendo shinai, so I'm guessing either past life / old legend tied to the Ōzakura tree, or something violent coming to this town. The hand could be Kōhei's (he does kendo), but the sleeve is modern and it doesn't look like a school uniform. The repeated first image (bookended junction) suggests a place that matters, maybe where Dad died? That's a stretch but it's where my head went.

**What I think is going on / what kind of story:** A slice-of-life school romance on top of a family-grief story, with a mystical layer waiting in the wings. Key things I picked up:
- Dad is gone, probably dead, "last spring" ("still says 'Garbage: burnables, Mon/Thu' in Dad's. He wrote it last spring. I've never written over it." Very effective line.)
- Mom has shut down, stays in her room behind the fusuma. Depression after Dad's death, I assume.
- Akane has some medical condition with dizziness / "the room starts tilting," clinic day circled on the calendar.
- Kōhei is the de facto parent: two lunches, schedule on the whiteboard, kendo plus restaurant shifts. He's overloaded and Nanoha knows it ("So you'd hear yourself say it.").
- Nanoha started bringing food "about a year now," which lines up with Dad's death.
- The Ōzakura, the giant ancient cherry tree at the shrine, is obviously going to be the supernatural center. Nanoha's grandpa runs (or at least looks after) the shrine.

**Lines/images that drove my guesses:**
- "She's been doing all of that since she was tall enough to reach." Establishes deep familiarity.
- "Her hands stay a second longer than the collar strictly needs." Not subtle, but it does the job.
- "She walks a little faster after that." She wanted him to come Saturday, and she's pleased/flustered he offered.
- Akane: "…You always are." Said to the rice cooker. Nice small moment of the sister noticing he's carrying too much.
- The collar CG (image_04): soft, warm, a petal on her braid as described. Kōhei has a sweat drop / slightly flustered look, which pushes the romance read.

**Small reactions as a reader:**
- Opening with four silent images and no caption is atmospheric but I genuinely didn't know what I was looking at. A second "the junction again" image felt like the game might have glitched.
- 5:40 alarm then "ten seconds anyway, as a matter of principle" is a nice voice beat.
- The text says Nanoha walks on the river side; in the CG she's on the side away from the river (river is behind Kōhei on the left). Tiny, but I noticed. She's also supposed to be "up on her toes"; her feet are out of frame, so it doesn't read.
- The text says she's folding his collar down over his tie; in the CG her hands are basically at the tie knot. Close enough.

## Hints that felt heavy-handed, too obvious or too coy

**Too coy / unreadable:**
1. **The prologue.** Four wordless images with no caption, no voice, no "dream" framing and no woman. I didn't know it was a dream, and I didn't see a woman. Re-showing image_01 at the end of it looked like a glitch on first pass. The sword hand also wears a **modern zip-up jacket sleeve**, while the real dream in part 4 is clearly period. So I couldn't tell whether the prologue was the dream, a different dream, or a flash-forward. One line of text or sound ("The fog again.") would fix most of this.

**Heavy-handed / too obvious:**
2. **Natsuki's "Someone who knows which drawer sticks in your kitchen."** An exact callback to Nanoha's hip-bump in part 1, delivered by a girl who has no way of knowing about it. It lands emotionally, but it's the author's hand, not Natsuki's.
3. **The Natsuki entrance stacks its signals:** her grin stutters, "I'm sure I'm the only one who sees it," he grips his pen "hard enough to hurt," then "I keep my eyes on the board for the entire first period." Any two of these would do. All four, plus his pen reaction having no cause the text acknowledges, tips it from mysterious into insistent.
4. **The dream's last two lines** ("That someone was waiting for me. / That I kept them waiting far too long.") restate "She says it like she's been waiting a very long time" from six lines up. I'd cut one.
5. **The Nanoha interlude's last line** ("She did not let herself think about why it had needed deciding at all.") is explicit, but I'd keep it. It's earned and beautifully phrased. I flag it only because it removes all doubt about her feelings in chapter 1, if the author wanted any left.

**Well calibrated (keep):** the wrist echo (Natsuki writing 水 on "the inside of her wrist" → the cut "on the inside of" his wrist). The left-palm callus thread (he raises his *left* hand, she reads his *left* palm, he rubs it in bed before the dream). "Longer than the collar strictly needs" / "longer than the garbage requires." The face catalogue's "number one" secret. Dad's handwriting on the whiteboard.

## Flat lines (and why)

1. **Akane: "Geez, Onii-chan! Every single morning!" / "And I slept! Super well! I didn't wake up once!"** Stock little-sister chirp, with the exclamation points doing all the characterization. Her real voice shows up two lines later ("…You always are.", said to the rice cooker), and that's much better.
2. **Kōhei: "Monday, Wednesday, Friday, Saturday morning. Shifts Tuesday, Thursday, Sunday."** It repeats the whiteboard narration almost word for word from 80 lines earlier. Nanoha's "So you'd hear yourself say it" justifies it in-story, but as a reader I read the schedule twice.
3. **Nanoha: "Oh. Grandpa says the shrine's cherries are the best they've been in years. I'm helping him sweep up there Saturday."** Plot setup in a costume. The "Oh." doesn't disguise it. (The "convenient back" follow-up rescues the exchange.)
4. **Natsuki: "Looking forward to this year."** Her recovery line after the stutter is a non-line. The CG carries the beat, and the text gives her nothing to recover *with*. A tiny tell (a slightly too-quick line, a joke that misfires) would sell that she's rattled.
5. **Natsuki: "Ha— okay. Then it's his face. He's got kind of a normal one, right? Don't tell him I said that."** The logic wobbles (why would a normal face explain déjà vu?), and "Don't tell him I said that" is boilerplate. Weakest Natsuki line in a chapter where she's otherwise sharp.
6. **Natsuki: "Hey. Does Fujisawa ever compete up north? Around Sendai?"** Reads like a question asked so the reader knows she's searching for a mundane explanation. Fine as plot, flat as speech.
7. **Kōhei: "It's fine. Saturday afternoons are free."** Doesn't sound like anything; the reply to "So you'd hear yourself say it" deserved a better deflection.
8. **Nanoha: "It's old. The pipes sound like a ghost."** Purely functional. She's funnier than this with Kōhei, and her shyness with Natsuki could still be wittier.

## With names covered, could I tell them apart?

Mostly yes. Kōhei (terse straight man), Natsuki (brisk, combative, kendo jargon, "So." / "Huh." / "Eh?"), Ōno-sensei (weary adult) and Nanoha-with-Kōhei (mock-formal deadpan: "Now then." "However." "I've prepared a theory.") are distinct.

The overlaps:
- **Rika and Akane** both run on exclamation points and repetition ("Nothing! Nothing!" / "Super well!"). Without "Onii-chan" in Akane's lines, I'd mix them up.
- **Ryōsuke and Nanoha** share the same comic engine: deadpan pseudo-logic delivered as fact (thermodynamics of taiyaki vs. the feng-shui conspiracy). "'Somewhere far' covers most of the planet" could be a Nanoha line with no edits.
- **Nanoha-with-Natsuki** ("Um—", "That's—yes. That's fine.") sounds like a different, generic shy girl. I read it as intentional (bold with him, shy with strangers), but blind I wouldn't have matched it to the part-1 Nanoha.
- **Natsuki's "Ha— okay... Don't tell him I said that"** could be Rika.

## Mismatches between text and illustrations

1. **Prologue (image_01–03):** no woman, and the sword hand (image_02) wears a modern zip-up jacket. The part-4 dream is clearly period. If these belong to the same dream, the art contradicts it. If not, nothing tells me so.
2. **image_04 (collar):** text says Nanoha "walks on the river side"; in the CG she's on the side away from the river. "Up on her toes" isn't visible (feet out of frame). Her hands are at the tie knot rather than folding the collar. All minor.
3. **image_06 (grin stutters):** Natsuki's startled glance goes toward the right of the frame and toward the front rows. Kōhei sits by the window (left of frame), second-to-last row. She seems to be reacting to someone else.
4. **image_05/06:** Natsuki reads noticeably older and more model-proportioned than a second-year, and stylistically harder-edged than Nanoha's soft CG. Not wrong exactly, but she looks like she's from a different VN.
5. **image_07 (dream):** text puts the cut "on the inside of" the wrist. What works: image_07's hilt matches image_02's, which was a nice connective-tissue catch. Natsuki's hair matches the description. The petal on Nanoha's braid is in the CG.

## Other problems I noticed

- **Garbage-day logic (part 4):** "Dad's handwriting on the whiteboard still says Monday, and tonight I went with the handwriting." But Nanoha's "wrong day" / "moving it closer to the right day" exchange, plus putting burnables out Monday *night* for a Tuesday pickup, muddles whether he's actually honoring Dad's day. Also, if he really followed Dad's Monday, why not that morning? The grief beat is lovely, and it deserves to be unambiguous.
- **Kōhei's pen grip** in part 2 has no motivation the text ever acknowledges. If it's dream recognition, a half-line in part 4 tying it back would pay it off.

## Would I keep reading?

**Yes.** What hooked me:
- **Nanoha.** The interlude, "I have met him before. Every day. For sixteen years.," and the face catalogue. She's the reason I'd open chapter 2.
- **The narrator's voice:** dry, competent, and quietly grieving. Dad's handwriting on the whiteboard and the fusuma "answer. Sort of." did more emotional work than the dream.
- **A triangle where both girls like each other**, which promises it'll hurt.
- **The dream**, especially because the evidence genuinely points both ways.

What would make me stop:
- If the past-life mystery turns into endless "who is she?" teasing with faceless CGs and no new information for several chapters.
- If Nanoha becomes a pure sacrificial childhood friend who steps aside without getting a real say.
- If the grounded material (Mom behind the door, Akane's dizziness, the money and time pressure) gets dropped for supernatural plot.
- If Natsuki flattens into a generic rival-tsundere. Right now her loneliness ("It looked like it cost her something") is what keeps her from being that.

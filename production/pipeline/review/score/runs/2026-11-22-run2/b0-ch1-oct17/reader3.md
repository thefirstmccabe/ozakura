## Checkpoint 3 (after part3: Natsuki's introduction, lunch)

**Who does Kōhei end up with?** It's now a real coin flip, and I think the chapter wants it that way. **Nanoha 45%, Natsuki 45%**, other 10%. Natsuki is a classic co-lead entrance: a full-screen CG, a shared hobby that matches his (kendo), immediate sparring banter ("Oh, he's cocky. Good."), and a private moment only he sees. She's set up as the girl who will crack the "I don't have time for a girlfriend" wall. Nanoha has the history and the title card. The genre usually splits about here: either the childhood friend wins a slow burn, or the fated stranger wins and the childhood friend gets the heartbreak arc. Nanoha's "about to do something brave" approach to Natsuki makes me fear the second.

**Who do I want him with?** Still **Nanoha, about 60/100** (down from 70). Natsuki is charming, and the kendo rivalry promises fun scenes, but Nanoha has more texture. I also already feel protective of her: "the way she holds things when she's about to do something brave."

**Who is the woman in the dream?** **Natsuki 45%, Nanoha 45%**, other 10%. The case for Natsuki is new and dramatic. Her grin "stutters" the moment she sees him, which reads as recognition. Kōhei grips his pen "hard enough to hurt" (pain again, like the dream wrist). She reads his left-palm callus where "the tsuka (hilt)" sits, and the dream had a katana hilt in frame. And "She repeats it like she's writing it on the inside of her wrist": the WRIST again, now attached to her. The case for Nanoha is still the quieter, thematic one: the river side, the Ōzakura, the caretaking, and "waiting a very long time" fits a childhood friend who's been waiting for him to notice her far better than a girl who has moved six times. One thing nags at me: the dream woman ties bandages and waits at home, while Natsuki is a fighter. **What the dream has to do with the present:** The wrist and hilt motifs are now explicitly threaded into the present. A reincarnation or past-life frame is about 70% likely. It's also possible the dream is a premonition of something that hasn't happened yet: a present-day kendo injury with a girl bandaging his wrist. **What kind of story:** A love triangle with a past-life mystery, built on a family drama. Natsuki is the catalyst. The Nanoha–Natsuki friendship is being set up deliberately ("Then I'll just call you Nanoha"), so I expect the triangle to strain a real friendship rather than a cat fight. Good.

# Final answers

**When I became fairly sure (70%+) of anything:**

- **That Natsuki recognizes Kōhei from something beyond ordinary experience: 70%+ at "Does he ever seem like… someone you've met before?"** At "Her grin stutters" I was only at 50%, because a kendo-tournament explanation was still live.

**Hints that felt heavy-handed, too obvious, or too coy:**
- **Heavy:** Nanoha's crush is signaled about eight separate ways in one chapter: lingering hands plus "stubborn crease," walking faster after he agrees to come Saturday, Rika's "Hmmm," finding his name first, "She'd had practice," "the way you say something you'd like written down," "She did not let herself think about why it had needed deciding at all," and the face catalogue. Each is well written; together they're a lot. I'd cut one or two from part 2 (the "stubborn crease" joke is the most worn).
- **Heavy:** Natsuki's "Does he ever seem like… someone you've met before?" is very direct for chapter 1. The grin stutter, the pen gripped "hard enough to hurt," and "writing it on the inside of her wrist" had already done the job subtly. Saying it out loud to Nanoha hands the reader the premise. The jet-lag joke softens it, but it's still the most on-the-nose line in the chapter.
- **Just right:** "She repeats it like she's writing it on the inside of her wrist." Subtle and resonant with the dream. Also the morning-walk staging (river, petals, a girl fixing part of him) as an unspoken echo of the dream.
- **Too coy / unclear:** "A bike bell rings somewhere behind us and doesn't pass." It's specific enough to read as a clue and goes nowhere. Also, what happened to Dad is never stated. That's fine for chapter 1, but I had to infer "dead" from the handwriting beat and the Hoshino-family food deliveries.

**Dialogue that felt lively:**

- Akane: "And I slept yesterday too. Two nights in a row. Medical science is baffled." / "The doctor face. You don't have a license, Onii-chan." / Kōhei: "I've got an egg with your name on it. That's basically a license."

- "You moved from Sendai. That's barely three hours by train." / "Very intense jet lag."

2. Kōhei: "It's fine. I like being busy." The most generic overworked-protagonist deflection there is. The moment matters (it's his wall), so it deserves a line only he would say.
3. Natsuki: "Takes one to know one." A cliché capping an otherwise sharp, specific observation about the tsuka callus.

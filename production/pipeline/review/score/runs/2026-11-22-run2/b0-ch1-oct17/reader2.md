# Reader 2 — cold read notes, Chapter 1 ("The House Next Door")

## Checkpoint 1 (after part 1: the dream prologue)

**Who does Kōhei end up with, and how sure?** No idea yet. Nobody has been named; I don't even know that the narrator is Kōhei except from the question sheet. If I'm forced: whoever turns out to be "the woman in the dream" in present-day form. 5%, essentially a placeholder. The chapter title "The House Next Door" makes me expect a childhood-friend / girl-next-door heroine, so if someone appears from next door I'll lean that way.

**Who do I want him to end up with?** No one to want yet. Mild pull toward the dream woman because the scene is tender, but she's a pair of hands.

**Who is the woman in the dream, and how sure?** Someone from a past life, not a childhood memory. Reasons: the illustration shows an adult man's forearm (veiny, muscular), a katana hilt, and a woman in a striped kimono with an obi under a cherry tree. That's Edo-or-earlier Japan, not a kid's memory. So my guess is a lover/wife from a previous life whose reincarnation will show up in the present. Who exactly in the cast: unknown. Confidence that it's a past-life lover: ~60%. Confidence about which present-day character: 0%, no cast yet.

**What does the dream have to do with the present-day story?** Almost certainly a reincarnation/fated-reunion frame: "That someone was waiting for me. That I kept them waiting far too long." reads as the thesis of the whole VN. I expect one of the girls to carry some echo of it (a phrase like "You're late," a habit of binding wounds, a scar on his wrist in the present, cherry trees, a river).

**What's going on / what kind of story is this becoming?** School romance with a supernatural or reincarnation spine. Tone is gentle, wistful, a little melancholy. "Ōzakura" (big cherry tree?) plus the petals suggests the tree itself will matter.

**Lines/images that drove my guesses:**
- "There's a dream I've had since I was small." — recurring dream = destiny device.
- "I've held it closed the whole way home from training." + katana in the image — training = sword training, so he's a swordsman in the dream.
- "She says it like she's been waiting a very long time." / "That I kept them waiting far too long." — the hook.
- "Hands that aren't mine wind a strip of cloth around a wrist that is." — nice line, the best of the prologue.
- Image: kimono, katana, cherry blossoms. Strongly reads as historical.

**Small notes so far:**
- "You're late." then "Hurt again." — the "again" implies she does this often; a relationship with routine. Good economy.
- The dream says "since I was small" but the arm in the picture is an adult's. That's fine if it's a past life; flagging only in case the author intends the dreamer to be himself as a child.
- The hands in the illustration are a little confusing at a glance: the patient's hand at upper right looks bent oddly and reads almost like a third hand gripping hers. Took me a second look to parse who owns which hand. Not a big deal, but the line "Hands that aren't mine … a wrist that is" depends on the reader parsing it quickly.
- Image matches the text otherwise (branch overhead, petals, strip of cloth, blood on the back of the wrist). The river isn't visible, but it's a sound, so fine.

## Checkpoint 2 (after part 2: morning, walk, class lists)

**Who does Kōhei end up with, and how sure?** Nanoha, 45%. The whole part is built around her: she knows the kitchen better than he does, fixes his collar, walks on the river side, finds his name first. But the genre rule "the childhood friend loses to the transfer student" is loudly in play: Rika announces a tall girl "from somewhere far" joining Two-B, and that's a classic rival-heroine drumroll. Transfer student 35%, someone else 20%.

**Who do I want him to end up with?** Nanoha, fairly strongly (7/10). She's funny, she's dry, and the quiet care ("Her hands stay a second longer than the collar strictly needs") lands. I'm already slightly worried for her, which I assume is the point.

**Who is the woman in the dream, and how sure?** Split. Nanoha 40%, the transfer student 35%, someone/something else (the spirit of the Ōzakura tree, his mother, a past-life figure who isn't anyone in the cast) 25%.
- For Nanoha: the dream woman is a caretaker with her hands on him ("Give me your hand… Hold still") and Nanoha does the same thing with the collar ("Kō-chan. Your collar." / "Before I can do anything about it"). The dream has a river close by and cherry trees overhead; Nanoha "always walks on the river side" under cherry trees, and her grandpa looks after the Ōzakura. Dream: "home from training" with a cut; present: Kōhei does kendo four days a week. The dream setting basically IS this walk.
- For the transfer student: "she's been waiting a very long time" / "You're late" suits someone who arrives from far away to meet him again. Being built up before she appears is the classic fated-heroine entrance.
- The dream image's kimono and katana still say "past life," so I think the dream is his past self, and whoever she is in the present will be her reincarnation.

**What does the dream have to do with the present?** Clearer now: the river, the cherry trees, the "training" (kendo), the wrist. Kōhei seems to be the swordsman reborn. The Ōzakura (Nanoha's grandpa sweeps "up there," so probably a shrine or hill with a big old tree) is probably the hinge between past and present. I expect a scene under the Ōzakura on Saturday.

**What's going on / what kind of story?** Slice-of-life school romance with real weight under it. Dad is gone (dead, I think: "He wrote it last spring. I've never written over it."), Mom is shut in her room and barely responds (grief/depression), Akane has some chronic illness with dizziness and a clinic day, and Kōhei is the 16-year-old who runs the household and has overbooked himself (kendo four days, a job at Hoshino Kitchen three days). Nanoha's family has been quietly feeding them "for about a year now." So: a caretaker boy who needs someone to take care of him. That rhymes with the dream (he's the one being bandaged). Theme seems to be letting yourself be looked after.

**Lines/images that drove my guesses:**
- "All of it except the bottom line, which still says 'Garbage: burnables, Mon/Thu' in Dad's. He wrote it last spring. I've never written over it." — best line in the part; the Dad reveal done by implication.
- "Nanoha walks on the river side. She always walks on the river side. I don't remember when we decided that." — reads as a deliberate dream echo.
- "Her hands stay a second longer than the collar strictly needs. / Probably a stubborn crease." — the romance hook (with a standard dense-hero joke).
- "Grandpa says the Ōzakura's the best it's been in years." — the tree from the title, finally named.
- "Two-B is getting a transfer student! A girl! From somewhere! Far! I heard she's super tall."
- "She went looking for mine before her own."
- "A bike bell rings somewhere behind us and doesn't pass." — noticed it; can't tell if it's atmosphere or someone tailing them. If it's a setup, I'll be pleased; if it's nothing, it's a slightly misleading detail.

**Problems noticed in this part:**
1. **The class-list payoff is muddled.** Rika has already shouted "You're in Two-B!" at Nanoha and then "Fujisawa-kun's in Two-B too," and Ryōsuke adds "we're all in Two-B." So by the time Nanoha points at the board, both of them already know both classes. The closing beat ("She went looking for mine before her own") is meant to be the emotional button of the part, but logically she didn't need to look for either. It still half-works (she went to see his name anyway, and the Fukuda excuse was a fib), but the narrator's conclusion overstates it. Either have Rika not announce Kōhei's class, or let the narrator notice that she checked his even though she'd been told.
2. **The "1-C for five days during the exchange program" joke** contradicts "We've been in the same class four years running" (if he was moved to 1-C for five days, then strictly they weren't). It's Nanoha being silly, so maybe intended, but I had to reread it to understand who was moved where and why it made the pool green. Slightly too convoluted for a quick gag.
3. **Class naming is inconsistent:** "Two-B" is spelled out everywhere, but "1-C" is in numerals. Pick one.
4. **Name/label friction:** the tag says "Ryōsuke" but the narration immediately calls him "Tokiwa" and Kōhei says "Good morning, Tokiwa." I got it, but it's one more name to map at a busy moment (Rika, Ryōsuke, Tokiwa, Fukuda, Hoshino all in one scene). I also don't know Nanoha's or Rika's surnames yet, and I don't know if "Hoshino Kitchen" is connected to anybody (Nanoha's family? Rika's?). If it's meaningful, I didn't catch it.
5. Minor: Akane's scarf in April with her school clothes. Fine if it's part of a uniform or a health thing, but I wondered why.
6. Minor: "five feet tall" from a Japanese high-schooler read a little localized to me. Not a problem, just noticed.

**What worked:** Nanoha's dialogue is the best thing so far ("Then I've arrived." / "You thought it in a short-person direction." / "We've always been keeping score. You're losing." / "So you'd hear yourself say it." / "That's why I fix them."). Akane's "Medical science is baffled" and "You don't have a license, Onii-chan" are good; "…That's sort of my point" quietly tells me she resents being looked after, which is sharp. The Mom beat ("a small shift of weight on the other side of the paper… That's an answer. Sort of.") is excellent and restrained.

## Checkpoint 3 (after part 3: Natsuki arrives)

**Who does Kōhei end up with, and how sure?** Nanoha 45%, Natsuki 40%, someone else 15%. Natsuki jumped from "rumor" to real contender in one scene: shared kendo, the stuttered grin when she sees him, the seat right behind him, and the easy banter. But the prologue's "waiting a very long time" / "kept them waiting far too long" fits Nanoha's situation in the present almost too well: she's plainly been waiting for years and he "doesn't have time." I can see the story going either way, and I suspect it wants me to feel exactly that.

**Who do I want him to end up with?** Still Nanoha (6/10), but less lopsided now; Natsuki is likable and a bit lonely ("like she isn't used to being the one who gets asked"), which got me. The fact that the two girls immediately become friendly makes me want neither to get hurt, which is a good place for a romance to put me.

**Who is the woman in the dream, and how sure?** Natsuki 45%, Nanoha 35%, other 20%.
- For Natsuki: "Her grin stutters. Just for a second." on first seeing him reads as recognition. Kōhei reacts physically at the same moment ("I've been holding my pen hard enough to hurt"), a hand pain that rhymes with the dream's cut wrist. She immediately reads his hand ("Left palm. You've got the callus right where the tsuka sits"), echoing "Give me your hand." She's a sword person. And then "She repeats it like she's writing it on the inside of her wrist." The wrist motif lands on her.
- Against Natsuki: the dream woman is a caretaker in a kimono who bandages the swordsman, not a fellow fighter. Natsuki is the fighter. Nanoha is the one who fixes him (collar, kitchen) and is tied to the river and the Ōzakura. So I half suspect Natsuki is the obvious decoy and the real answer is Nanoha, or that Natsuki is connected to the past in a different role (a rival or sparring partner, maybe even whoever keeps cutting his wrist).

**What does the dream have to do with the present?** Now I'm fairly sure it's a past-life thread: two people react to each other on sight for no stated reason. The dream's "training" = kendo, and the new girl is a kendoka. Wednesday (club) and Saturday (the Ōzakura) look like the next two set pieces.

**What's going on / what kind of story?** A two-heroine triangle (childhood friend vs. transfer student) with a reincarnation undertow and a grief/caretaker family story underneath. Tone is warm and funny on top. The heroines liking each other suggests this will be bittersweet rather than catty.

**Lines/images that drove my guesses:**
- "Her grin stutters. Just for a second. Just long enough that I'm sure I'm the only one who sees it."
- "I realize I've been holding my pen hard enough to hurt."
- "Left palm. You've got the callus right where the tsuka (hilt) sits." / "Takes one to know one."
- "She repeats it like she's writing it on the inside of her wrist."
- "The guy who turned down three confessions last year." / "It's true that I don't have time."
- Nanoha: "the way she holds things when she's about to do something brave." — she's being generous to a rival and it costs her something. Lovely beat.
- "makes me feel weirdly like furniture." / "I eat my rice. It's very good rice. I made it myself." — good closing joke, and it also shows he's shut out of a moment.

**Problems noticed in this part:**
1. **Illustration mismatch, hair:** the text says "Copper hair, cut neat at her shoulders and tucked behind one ear." In image_02 her hair falls well past her shoulders (to mid-chest), loose and layered, not "neat." It doesn't clearly read as tucked behind an ear either. Readers will notice because the line comes right as the picture is on screen.
2. **"Super tall" never pays off.** Rika's whole setup is "she's super tall, taller than the vice-principal." Neither the illustration nor the narration confirms it: Kōhei describes her hair and her uniform but never her height, and in the picture she looks average (the lectern hits her waist; nothing for scale). Either the narration should register it ("Rika was right about the height") or the art should sell it. Right now the setup just evaporates.
3. **"Walking onto a court she's already won on":** a court is tennis/basketball; she's a kendoka. Maybe deliberate (she's sporty in general), but a kendo image ("a floor," "a match") would tie it to her better.
4. **Inline glosses** "tsuka (hilt)" and "shodan (first dan)" feel a bit clunky in dialogue. I understand why they're there. In a VN, a tooltip or just "hilt" would read smoother; manga scanlation readers are used to it, though, so it's minor.
5. **"Please take care of me."** is a literal yoroshiku. Fine for the translated-manga feel, but right after her very natural, idiomatic introduction it sounds stiff. Possibly intentional (her mask going back on after the stutter); if so, it works.
6. Minor: in the illustration she's waving, which is fine, but the text has her counting on her fingers; whatever. Not a real problem.

**What worked:** Natsuki's voice is distinct and fun ("So I'm basically a professional. Please don't go easy on me." / "Oh, he's cocky. Good. I hate practicing with polite people." / "That's either really honest or really sad. I can't decide." / "I'm very decisive. Usually."). Ōno-sensei's "through channels I'd like to know more about" is a good one-liner, and "a man who was born in a cardigan" made me smile. The Nanoha/Natsuki "san" exchange ("I'm working up to it. I need time to adjust." / "I'm going to do it anyway.") characterizes both of them in three lines. Background details in image_02 (cherry trees and mountains through the window) match the setting nicely.

5. Minor: Nanoha uses "Kōhei" with Natsuki and to him ("She's nice, Kōhei") but "Kō-chan" at the door. I read it as deliberate (she's being careful), and I liked it, but only if it's intentional.

# Final answers

## When I became fairly sure (70%+) of anything
- **That Nanoha is in love with Kōhei:** 70% at "Her hands stay a second longer than the collar strictly needs." (part 2); 90%+ at "Nanoha said it as lightly as she knew how. She'd had practice." (part 4).
- **That there's a past-life / déjà-vu link between Kōhei and Natsuki:** 70%+ at "Her grin stutters. Just for a second." combined with "I realize I've been holding my pen hard enough to hurt." (part 3); ~85% at "Does he ever seem like… someone you've met before?" (part 4).
- **That the dream is a past life rather than a childhood memory:** about 60% from image_01 alone (katana, kimono, an adult's forearm), 70%+ by Natsuki's question.
- **Who he ends up with:** never reached 70%. Final: Nanoha 50 / Natsuki 40 / other 10.
- **Who the dream woman is:** never reached 70%. Final: Natsuki 55 / Nanoha 35 / other 10.

## Hints that felt heavy-handed, too obvious or too coy
- **Heavy:** Natsuki's "Does he ever seem like… someone you've met before?" Directly states the mystery a scene after the art and narration already planted it. I'd cut it back to something sideways, or move it to a later chapter.
- **Heavy-ish:** the wrist motif lands three times in quick succession around Natsuki ("pen hard enough to hurt," the callus, "writing it on the inside of her wrist"). The last simile is odd enough on its own that it reads as a plant. One or two of these would be plenty.
- **Too neat:** Natsuki's "which drawer sticks in your kitchen."
- **Too coy:** "Probably a stubborn crease." The dense-hero joke is the most generic beat in an otherwise specific chapter.
- **Possibly too coy / dangling:** the bike bell that "doesn't pass."
- **Well-judged:** Dad (never stated, just the handwriting), Mom (the floorboard creak), Akane's illness (the tilting room, the clinic day). Rika's "completely neutral hmmm" is obvious, but it's meant to be.

## Dialogue that felt lively
- Nanoha basically every time she speaks: "Then I've arrived." / "You thought it in a short-person direction." / "We've always been keeping score. You're losing." / "So you'd hear yourself say it." / "His back is a very convenient back." / "I have met him before. Every day. For sixteen years." / "You have several. I've catalogued them."
- Akane: "And I slept yesterday too. Two nights in a row. Medical science is baffled." / "The doctor face. You don't have a license, Onii-chan."
- Natsuki: "So I'm basically a professional. Please don't go easy on me." / "Oh, he's cocky. Good. I hate practicing with polite people." / "That's either really honest or really sad. I can't decide." / "Very intense jet lag."
- Ōno-sensei: "through channels I'd like to know more about."
- Ryōsuke: "That's not a counterargument. That's a timestamp." (his one great line).

## Up to 8 flat lines, and why
1. **Ryōsuke: "—which is why, structurally, a hot dog is a sandwich." / "A sub roll has a hinge…"** A recycled internet debate, American-coded ("sub roll") in a Japanese school in Kanagawa. It feels pasted in rather than his.
2. **Rika: "It's destiny! It's in the stars! I'm making us matching keychains!"** Generic genki. Rika is all volume and hair clips; the clips are specific, her lines aren't.
3. **Kōhei: "Looks can be deceiving."** A cliché; only Nanoha's comeback saves the exchange.
4. **Kōhei: "Of course you have."** A stock straight-man line. Kōhei's spoken dialogue in general is mostly reactions ("Meetings." "Again." "Please don't." "Great." "Night."). His narration has a real voice; his mouth doesn't yet.
5. **Kōhei: "It's fine. I like being busy."** The right beat (denial), but it's the line anyone would write. Something more specific to him would sting more.
6. **Natsuki: "Please take care of me."** A literal yoroshiku. It's stiff next to her very natural intro. (If it's meant as her mask snapping back on after the stutter, it half works.)
7. **Natsuki: "I'm Mizuno Natsuki. It's nice to meet you all."** Filler, though self-intros are filler by nature.
8. **Natsuki: "That's really nice."** Filler before her much better follow-up line; could be cut.

## With the names covered, could I tell them apart?
- **Nanoha:** yes, mostly. Deadpan, mock-formal ("I've prepared a theory," "There have been meetings," "I do supervise beautifully").
- **Natsuki:** yes. Bright, direct, self-deprecating, lots of "really" and "Okay, okay," asks permission and then does it anyway.
- **Rika:** yes, by punctuation alone (caps, exclamation marks, "Nano-chaaaan").
- **Ryōsuke:** mostly, by pedantry. **But Nanoha's class-assignment conspiracy routine sounds like Ryōsuke's bits.** Both of them do "theory," "structurally," the over-reasoned argument for a silly claim, in the same scene ("I've prepared a theory" / "I chose it theoretically"). Covered, I'd misattribute some of Nanoha's conspiracy lines to him.
- **Akane vs. Nanoha:** this is the weakest pair. Both are dry, both deflate Kōhei, and both do the same joke shape about his face ("Stop doing the face… The doctor face" / "You did your—… I've catalogued them. That one's number four"). Without "Onii-chan" I couldn't reliably tell Akane's lines from Nanoha's.
- **Kōhei:** in dialogue he's a generic straight man; covered, his lines could belong to almost any boy.
- **Ōno-sensei:** distinct enough (tired, dry, adult).

## Text vs. illustrations
- **image_02, hair:** text says "cut neat at her shoulders and tucked behind one ear"; the art shows hair well past her shoulders, loose and layered. Clear mismatch, and it's on screen at the moment the line is read.
- **image_02, height:** Rika says she's "super tall, taller than the vice-principal." Nothing in the art (or the narration) sells that; she reads as average height.
- **image_02:** "walking onto a court" while she's a kendoka; and she's waving rather than doing anything from the text. Minor.
- **image_01:** matches the text well (branch, petals, cloth strip, blood on the back of the wrist). But the hands are hard to parse at first glance (the patient's own hand at upper right looks like a third hand gripping hers), which slightly undercuts "Hands that aren't mine wind a strip of cloth around a wrist that is." Also, the arm is clearly a grown man's while the narration says "a dream I've had since I was small." I read that as a past life (good), but if the author means the dreamer to be Kōhei himself, the art says otherwise.

## Would I keep reading?
**Yes.** What hooked me:
- Nanoha, specifically the interlude and the "every day for sixteen years" line. I care what happens to her.
- The question Natsuki asks and can't explain, plus her stuttered grin.
- The household: Dad's handwriting, Mom behind the fusuma, Akane resenting being looked after. That's a real story under the romance, and it's handled with restraint.
- The window across the gap. I want the next night scene.

What would make me stop:
- If the reincarnation thread turns into exposition (a shrine priest explaining the legend of the Ōzakura in a monologue) rather than staying in small physical echoes.
- If Nanoha is a designated loser: given the interlude, written to lose gracefully so Natsuki can have her fate.
- If Kōhei stays a reactive straight man in dialogue. He needs to want something out loud soon.
- If Rika and Ryōsuke stay sitcom gag machines (hot dogs, keychains) with no inner life.
- If the family threads (Mom, Akane's illness) get dropped once the triangle heats up.

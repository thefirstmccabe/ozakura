# Reader 1 notes: cold read, Chapter 1 "The House Next Door"

## Checkpoint 1 (after part 1: the dream prologue)

**Who does Kōhei end up with, and how sure?** No idea yet. Nobody named Kōhei has appeared; I'm assuming he's the first-person narrator. The only woman on the page is the dream woman, so if forced: "whoever the dream woman turns out to be in the present day." Confidence: 10%.

**Who do I want him to end up with?** No one to root for yet. Mild pull toward the dream woman simply because she's the only character with any warmth so far (she gets three good lines of characterisation in "She says it like a scolding. She says it like she's laughing. She says it like she's been waiting a very long time."). Strength: 15/100.

**Who is the woman in the dream?** Unknown. Guesses, in order: (a) a past-life lover of the narrator (the illustration is clearly period: katana hilt, striped kimono, cloth bandage, so this is not a memory from his own childhood); (b) a real childhood friend or neighbour his memory has dressed up; (c) someone who'll turn up in the present day as a reincarnation. The chapter title "The House Next Door" makes me suspect the girl next door is going to be tied to her. Confidence in any specific identity: 0%, since I have no candidates. Confidence that it's a past-life / reincarnation setup: ~55%.

**What does the dream have to do with the present-day story?** Probably the frame of the whole romance: a promise or a waiting that wasn't fulfilled in a past life ("That someone was waiting for me. That I kept them waiting far too long."), to be resolved now. The wrist wound "from training" plus the katana suggests the past-life him was a swordsman or samurai; I expect a present-day echo (an injury on the same wrist, kendo club, someone bandaging his hand).

**What's going on / what kind of story?** A school romance with a reincarnation or fated-lovers hook layered on top. Tone so far: wistful, quiet, a little literary. The "you're late" line reads like a stock reunion line that will be echoed later by a present-day girl.

**What drove the guesses (quotes/images):**
- "There's a dream I've had since I was small." Recurring since childhood signals something supernatural or fated, not ordinary memory.
- "My wrist stings. There's a cut across the back of it, and I've held it closed the whole way home from training." Training plus the sheathed sword in image_01 means a swordsman.
- "???: 'You're late.'" / "'Hurt again. Give me your hand… Hold still.'" The "again" means this happens often; she's used to patching him up. Intimate, domestic, not a first meeting.
- "Hands that aren't mine wind a strip of cloth around a wrist that is." Nice line.
- image_01: cherry blossoms, katana tsukamaki, woman's striped kimono and obi, bandage with a blood spot. No faces shown, which reads as deliberate withholding.
- "That I kept them waiting far too long." Gender-neutral "them" here is interesting after "She" all the way through; maybe just avoiding a pronoun, but it slightly stood out.

**Small notes on part 1:**
- The illustration shows no river, though the text leads with "The sound of a river, close by." Fine for a sound, not a problem.
- The man's arm/hand in image_01 looks weathered and adult (prominent veins, rough knuckles), so it's a grown man, not a boy. Supports "past life" over "his own childhood memory."
- In the picture the blood spot sits on top of the bandage near the back of the wrist, which matches "across the back of it". Good match.

## Checkpoint 2 (after part 2: morning, the walk, class lists)

**Who does Kōhei end up with, and how sure?** Nanoha, 50%. The unseen tall transfer girl, 35%. Someone else, 15%. The chapter so far belongs to Nanoha. She's the warmest, funniest person in it, and the narration keeps catching her small tells while Kōhei pretends not to. But the genre alarm is going off: a childhood friend who already does everything for him, plus a mysterious tall transfer student announced on day one, is the textbook setup where the childhood friend loses. That's what holds me at 50 and not higher.

**Who do I want him to end up with?** Nanoha, 70/100. "You thought it in a short-person direction" sold me. She's also clearly been holding his household together for a year without making a thing of it, which is quietly moving.

**Who is the woman in the dream, and how sure?** Nanoha 40%, the transfer student 35%, someone else 25% (maybe someone tied to Nanoha's grandpa and the Ōzakura, like an ancestor or a spirit of the tree). Nanoha gets the vote on behaviour. The dream woman scolds and laughs at once and patches him up "again". Nanoha does the same register ("Good morning. I'm coming in.") and stops him to fix his collar with her hands lingering. The transfer student gets votes because the story clearly wants a second candidate and she's built up as mysterious and "from far away."

**What does the dream have to do with the present day?** The present day is now visibly rhyming with it. The dream had a river, a branch, petals, a swordsman coming home from training, and a woman tending his wrist. The present has the riverside path under cherry trees, Kōhei doing kendo, and Nanoha walking "on the river side" and fixing his collar. The Ōzakura (a big cherry tree, apparently on a hill, looked after by Nanoha's grandpa) is presumably the tree from the dream. I think the dream is a past life, and the tree is where it happened.

**What's going on / what kind of story is this becoming?** A grief-tinged slice-of-life romance. Dad seems to be dead ("in Dad's. He wrote it last spring. I've never written over it."), Mom is shut in her room, the little sister Akane has some chronic illness (dizziness, clinic visits), and Kōhei has made himself the parent. Over that sits a reincarnation or fated-love thread and a love triangle (childhood friend vs. new girl). The theme is already visible: Kōhei is too busy carrying everyone to notice that someone is carrying him.

**What drove the guesses (quotes):**
- "Nanoha walks on the river side. She always walks on the river side. I don't remember when we decided that." Echoes "The sound of a river, close by" in the dream.
- "Kō-chan. Your collar." / "Her hands stay a second longer than the collar strictly needs. / Probably a stubborn crease." A hands-tending-him beat right after a dream about hands tending him. Feels deliberately paired.
- "Grandpa says the Ōzakura's the best it's been in years." Named tree, capitalised, plus a scheduled Saturday visit, so it will matter.
- "I heard she's super tall. Taller than the vice-principal!" Sets up a physical contrast with tiny Nanoha. I half expect "tall" to matter for the dream (height difference?).
- "She went looking for mine before her own." Spelled out for us: Nanoha cares.
- "A bike bell rings somewhere behind us and doesn't pass." Odd and slightly ominous. Is someone following them? Possibly the transfer student, possibly nothing.
- "So you'd hear yourself say it." She's worried he's overloaded.

**Problems noticed in part 2:**
1. **Class-list logic wobble.** Rika has already shouted "And Fujisawa-kun's in Two-B too," and Ryōsuke has said "we're all in Two-B." Then Kōhei still squeezes to the board, and Nanoha "finding" his name is played as a reveal. Rika had also already told Nanoha her own class, so "She went looking for mine before her own" doesn't quite hold: she didn't need to look for her own. The payoff beat (the Fukuda excuse being invented) still works, but the setup undercuts it. Either Rika and Ryōsuke shouldn't announce his class, or Nanoha's find should come before them.
2. **Slightly over-explained tell.** "Her hands stay a second longer… Probably a stubborn crease." is fine (oblivious-narrator joke), but combined with "She went looking for mine before her own. I don't know why that's the thing that sticks with me", plus Rika's "Hmmm", the "Nanoha likes him" signal arrives three times in one part. It's leaning toward heavy-handed by the end of the part.
3. **"The vice-principal is five feet tall. In shoes."** Funny line, but a Japanese high schooler giving height in feet reads as visible localisation. Minor bump against the translated-manga feel. Not a big deal.
4. **"Second year of high school starts today."** It's April 7, and Rika says "Two-B is getting a transfer student" as rumour on the first day. Fine. Just noting I briefly wondered if the opening ceremony is before or after classes are posted; it reads OK.

**Lively lines so far:** "And I slept yesterday too. Two nights in a row. Medical science is baffled." / "The doctor face. You don't have a license, Onii-chan." / "I've got an egg with your name on it. That's basically a license." / "You thought it in a short-person direction." / "That's not a counterargument. That's a timestamp." / "His back is a very convenient back. It only hurts on days with a lot of petals." / "The pool is green."

## Checkpoint 3 (after part 3: Natsuki's arrival, lunch)

**Who does Kōhei end up with, and how sure?** Now a coin flip. Natsuki 45%, Nanoha 45%, other 10%. Within this, I've given a small 5% to a curveball where the triangle's real pairing is Nanoha and Natsuki ("the two of them laughing in the window light, over the top of my head, makes me feel weirdly like furniture"). Natsuki gets the full-screen entrance CG, the shared-hobby hook, and a charged first look. Those are lead-heroine signals, so she's caught up fast. Nanoha still has the whole emotional foundation of part 2.

**Who do I want him to end up with?** Still Nanoha, 60/100, down slightly. Natsuki is fun ("Oh, he's cocky. Good. I hate practicing with polite people."), and the kendo rivalry promises good scenes. But Nanoha walking up "about to do something brave" to offer a tour, when she could just as well have been territorial, made me like her more as a person, even as it makes her the more likely to lose.

**Who is the woman in the dream, and how sure?** Natsuki 45%, Nanoha 40%, other 15%. The case for Natsuki: she's a swordswoman tied to the dream's sword, her grin "stutters" when she sees him as if she recognises him, Kōhei grips his pen "hard enough to hurt" (pain in the hand, as in the dream's wrist), and she "repeats it like she's writing it on the inside of her wrist" (the wrist again). The case for Nanoha is thematic: the dream woman has been "waiting a very long time" and the narrator "kept them waiting far too long". That's Nanoha's situation exactly, while Natsuki has been the new girl six times and hasn't waited anywhere. I think the writer is deliberately splitting the clues between them: Natsuki has the body/sword clues, Nanoha has the emotional clues.

**What does the dream have to do with the present day?** I'm now fairly confident it's a past-life memory that at least one of the girls also carries in some form. Natsuki's stutter reads like recognition of someone she's never met. A sword (kendo) runs through both timelines. My new theory: Natsuki has the same dream, or a matching one, and that's why she freezes.

**What's going on / what kind of story is this becoming?** A love triangle with a reincarnation question at the centre: "which girl is the one from the dream", with the reader invited to guess. Underneath that, the more interesting story is Kōhei's overload ("It's true that I don't have time."). The three refused confessions set him up as someone who has shut off romance entirely, so the arc will be him making room.

**What drove the guesses (quotes/images):**
- "Her grin stutters. Just for a second. Just long enough that I'm sure I'm the only one who sees it." The biggest single clue in the chapter so far.
- "I realize I've been holding my pen hard enough to hurt." Kōhei's body reacting before his head.
- "Left palm. You've got the callus right where the tsuka (hilt) sits." / "Takes one to know one." Hands again; the sword again.
- "She repeats it like she's writing it on the inside of her wrist." Wrist motif carried over to her.
- "the way she holds things when she's about to do something brave." Nanoha's courage, quietly.
- "Nanoha: '…Natsuki-san.' / 'I'm working up to it. I need time to adjust.'" Nanoha keeping a little distance; cute, and maybe guarded.
- image_02: Natsuki front and centre, copper hair, waving, cherry trees and mountains outside the window. Confident, open body language. She reads as heroine.

**Problems noticed in part 3:**
5. **The "super tall" setup is dropped.** Part 2 spends a whole exchange on the rumour that she's "Taller than the vice-principal!", and then nothing in her entrance mentions her height: no "she really is tall" and no "she isn't actually tall". image_02 doesn't read as especially tall either; she looks average against the blackboard. Either pay it off in one line of narration or I'm left wondering whether the rumour was wrong.
6. **Hair description vs. image_02.** The text says "Copper hair, cut neat at her shoulders and tucked behind one ear." In the illustration it falls past her shoulders in loose, layered, slightly messy strands framing her face. It's not really "neat", and the tuck is hard to see. Small, but it's the one line of physical description she gets, and the picture contradicts it.
7. **"like she's walking onto a court she's already won on."** "Court" is a basketball/tennis image for a girl who turns out to do kendo, which happens on a floor or in a dojo. It nearly works as a general sports metaphor, but given she's a kendoka, "a floor" or "a match she's already won" would land better.
8. **Natsuki and Nanoha's banter registers overlap.** Both do dry one-liners in the same rhythm. "I will. I'm very decisive. Usually." (Natsuki) could be swapped with "It's not a big tour. The vending machine part is the most important." (Nanoha) and I wouldn't notice. Natsuki is distinct when she's pushy ("I'm going to do it anyway"), but her deadpan lines drift into Nanoha's voice.
9. **image_02 tone.** The skirt is very short and the camera framing is a little pin-up compared with the restrained tone of the prose (and of image_01). It's not a big deal, but it's the one moment where the art feels like a different, more fan-service book than the text. The three students in the foreground also all have identical long straight black hair, which looks a bit clone-stamped.

**Lively lines this part:** "the expression of a man who was born in a cardigan" / "through channels I'd like to know more about" / "So I'm basically a professional. Please don't go easy on me." / "Is that in the welcome packet?" / "That's either really honest or really sad. I can't decide." / "I eat my rice. It's very good rice. I made it myself." (great end beat) / "makes me feel weirdly like furniture."

## Checkpoint 4 (after part 4: Nanoha interlude, the two houses at night; end of chapter)

**Who does Kōhei end up with, and how sure?** Nanoha 55%, Natsuki 40%, other 5%. The chapter is titled after Nanoha's house, it opens and closes on her, and the last image is her window facing his "across a gap so narrow you could pass a book through it". The book is telling me where its heart is. What stops me going higher is that it's also giving Natsuki the destiny thread: she's the one who asks "Does he ever seem like… someone you've met before?" My working theory is that the story is "past-life fate (Natsuki) vs. a life actually lived together (Nanoha)", and these stories usually come down on the side of the lived life. That tips me to Nanoha, but only just.

**Who do I want him to end up with?** Nanoha, 70/100. Back up. The interlude did it: "Nanoha said it as lightly as she knew how. She'd had practice." and "She'd decided it firmly, the way you shut a window against the wind." She's generous to her rival on purpose, at a cost to herself. I'd be annoyed if that generosity is what makes her lose. I'd also happily read more of Natsuki and Nanoha as friends whatever happens.

**Who is the woman in the dream, and how sure?** Natsuki 50%, Nanoha 40%, other 10%. Natsuki's déjà vu question is the most direct clue in the chapter, and it points at her. But the dream woman is the one who *waits*, and who bandages a swordsman rather than being one. Natsuki is the swordswoman who has never stayed anywhere. So either the roles are scrambled across lifetimes, or Natsuki remembers him for a different reason (a sword comrade or rival in that life?) and the dream woman is Nanoha. I can't separate them. That's probably the intent, and it mostly works.

**What does the dream have to do with the present day?** I'm now fairly sure (~75%) it's a past-life memory that Kōhei and Natsuki both carry in some form. Natsuki feels it as "someone you've met before"; Kōhei feels it as gripping his pen until it hurts. The Ōzakura and Nanoha's grandpa will probably be where the past gets explained (Saturday). The dream's "You're late" / "kept them waiting far too long" is the emotional question the series will answer: who has he kept waiting? On the evidence of the last scene, the answer looks like Nanoha, which is a nice irony if the dream turns out to be about Natsuki.

**What's going on / what kind of story is this becoming?** A gentle, grief-tinged school romance in which both heroines are genuinely nice to each other. The love triangle runs on mutual decency rather than cattiness, which I liked. The supernatural layer is so far restrained: one dream and one odd question. The family subplot (Dad's handwriting, Mom behind the fusuma, Akane's illness) is the most affecting thing in the chapter and grounds Kōhei's "I don't have time."

**What drove the guesses (quotes):**
- "Does he ever seem like… someone you've met before?" Most direct past-life pointer, aimed at Natsuki.
- "I have met him before. Every day. For sixteen years." Nanoha's answer. Great line; it's also the thesis of her side of the triangle.
- "Natsuki dropped into Kōhei's chair, knowing perfectly well whose it was". Natsuki is interested.
- "She did not let herself think about why it had needed deciding at all." I read it as jealousy (90%), with a small chance it means something else about her feelings toward Natsuki.
- "She says it a little too clearly, the way you say something you'd like written down." Nanoha giving him permission she doesn't want to give.
- "Nobody notices the eleventh step." / "She did." Kōhei and Natsuki are both noticers, the same kind of person. Points toward a Kōhei/Natsuki match.
- "A moment later her upstairs light comes on, in the window that faces mine" / "I stand there for a while longer than the garbage requires." The final note is Nanoha.

**Problems noticed in part 4:**
10. **Calendar logic.** In part 2 Kōhei reads "the calendar on the wall", which has a "red circle around the eighteenth" (so it's a dated page for this April), yet its bottom line is in Dad's handwriting and "He wrote it last spring." A dated monthly calendar can't carry writing from a year ago. If it's a whiteboard or a reusable schedule board, say so in two words ("the whiteboard calendar") and the beat survives. As written, it snagged me on rereading once part 4 leaned on it again ("Dad's handwriting on the calendar still says Monday").
11. **The garbage-day joke doesn't quite add up.** Pickup is now Tuesday; he takes the bags out Monday night. In ordinary terms that's the night before pickup, i.e. roughly on time, so Nanoha's "You're taking out the trash on the wrong day" and his "I'm moving it closer to the right day" sit oddly. The emotional point (he follows Dad's handwriting) lands. The logic of the exchange doesn't.
12. **Natsuki's drawer line is too neat.** "Someone who knows which drawer sticks in your kitchen." Of all possible examples, she happens to name the exact detail the reader watched Nanoha handle in part 2. It works as an author's wink to the reader, but in-world it's a coincidence big enough that I briefly wondered whether Natsuki is psychic or remembering something. If that's intended as a clue, it's very coy; if not, it's heavy-handed.
13. **Nanoha and Akane share the same "your face" bit.** Akane: "Stop doing the face." / "The doctor face." Nanoha: "You have several. I've catalogued them. That one's number four." It's a good running joke, but giving it to both girls makes their teasing voices converge. One of them should own it, or the second instance should acknowledge the first.

**Lively lines this part:** "It's very old. The heating sounds like a ghost." / "Perfect. I love it already." / "I have met him before. Every day. For sixteen years." / "You moved from Sendai. That's barely three hours by train." / "Very intense jet lag." / "I'm moving it closer to the right day." / "You have several. I've catalogued them. That one's number four. 'Somebody wants to fight me.'" / "What's number one?" "Secret."

# Final answers

**When I became fairly sure (70%+) of anything the checkpoints asked:**
- *That the dream is a past life connected to the present story:* reached ~70% at Natsuki's entrance, "Her grin stutters. Just for a second. Just long enough that I'm sure I'm the only one who sees it." It went to ~75% at "Does he ever seem like… someone you've met before?"
- *Who he ends up with and who the dream woman is:* I never got to 70% on either. Peak was 55% (Nanoha, ending) and 50% (Natsuki as dream woman). Both stayed close to a coin flip for the whole chapter. For a chapter 1 that's a reasonable place to leave a reader, though I'd have liked one small, unambiguous detail pointing either way. Even a false one would have given me something to hold.
- Outside the questions asked: I was 90%+ sure Nanoha is in love with Kōhei by "She went looking for mine before her own" (part 2), and the chapter keeps confirming it.

**Hints that felt heavy-handed, too obvious or too coy:**
- *Heavy:* Nanoha's feelings get signalled roughly six times: Rika's "Hmmm", the collar ("a second longer than the collar strictly needs"), the Fukuda excuse, "She'd had practice", "a little too clearly", and "longer than the garbage requires". Each one is nicely written; together they're a lot for one chapter, and they leave Kōhei looking almost wilfully oblivious.
- *Heavy-ish:* Natsuki's "someone you've met before?" is very direct for a first-day conversation. The jet-lag joke rescues it, but it's the moment where the reincarnation premise is most visibly handed to the reader.
- *Too neat:* Natsuki naming "which drawer sticks in your kitchen" (see 12).
- *Too coy:* "A bike bell rings somewhere behind us and doesn't pass." It's never explained, and it reads like a clue (someone following them?) that the chapter forgets. If it's just atmosphere, it's a misleading choice of detail.
- *Coy, but in a good way:* the Ōzakura (mentioned once, with a Saturday visit booked) and Mom behind the fusuma ("That's an answer. Sort of."). Both are restrained and make me want to know more.
- *Dropped setup:* the "super tall" rumour (see 5).

**Dialogue that felt lively:** Akane's "Medical science is baffled" and "You don't have a license, Onii-chan"; Nanoha's conspiracy-theory riff ("There have been meetings." / "The pool is green."), "You thought it in a short-person direction", "I have met him before. Every day. For sixteen years.", and the face catalogue ("That one's number four"); Ryōsuke's "That's not a counterargument. That's a timestamp." and "I chose it theoretically"; Ōno-sensei's "through channels I'd like to know more about"; Natsuki's "Oh, he's cocky. Good. I hate practicing with polite people." and "Very intense jet lag." The Akane breakfast scene and the walk to school are the best-written stretches.

**Up to 8 flat lines, and why:**
1. Nanoha: "A transfer student?" An echo line that exists only to cue Rika. It's the one moment Nanoha has no personality.
2. Natsuki: "Please take care of me." A literal *yoroshiku* that lands at the chapter's most charged beat (right after the grin stutter). It's stiff in English and deflates the moment. "Nice to meet you all" was already used, so something with a bit of her voice would be better.
3. Natsuki: "Takes one to know one." In English this is a playground comeback to an insult, so as a friendly "fellow kendoka" line it reads slightly wrong.
4. Kōhei: "Club starts Wednesday." By this point the kendo schedule has been given three times (calendar, his recital to Nanoha, "Club doesn't start until Wednesday"). This fourth mention is pure repetition.
5. Natsuki: "I would love that. Really. Thank you." Generic gratitude; any character could say it.
6. Natsuki: "That's really nice." Same problem: filler before her actual, better line about never staying anywhere long enough.
7. Natsuki: "This school's nice. It feels… lived in. Does that make sense?" Slightly greeting-card. The narration around it ("like she'd been handed something") does the work better than the dialogue.
8. Kōhei: "Great." A sarcastic one-word reply that could have been sharper, given how good his dry lines are elsewhere ("Is that in the welcome packet?").

**With names covered, could I tell the characters apart? Who sounds like whom?**
- **Easily distinct:** Rika (capitals, exclamation marks, "Nano-chaaaan", "-kun"), Ryōsuke (pedantic structural arguments), Ōno-sensei (tired adult), Akane ("Onii-chan", sardonic little-sister deflection about her health).
- **Mostly distinct:** Kōhei (dry, minimal, deflecting) and Nanoha (polite-formal surface, "I'm coming in", "Mizuno-san", with absurdist deadpan escalation underneath).
- **Blur risk:** Natsuki's deadpan lines ("I will. I'm very decisive. Usually.") sound like Nanoha. Natsuki is only unmistakable when she's being pushy and casual ("Okay, okay", "Eh, 'san'?", "I'm going to do it anyway", "No way"). Nanoha and Akane also overlap on the teasing-about-his-face bit (see 13). Pairs most likely to be confused: **Natsuki ↔ Nanoha**, then **Nanoha ↔ Akane**.
- Address forms read consistently: Rika's "Fujisawa-kun", Nanoha's "Kō-chan" to his face and "Kōhei" when talking about him, and Kōhei's "Mizuno" in narration. Nanoha switching to "Kōhei" in "She's nice, Kōhei. I like her." and back to "Kō-chan" at the door felt deliberate and effective, not a slip.

**Mismatches between text and illustrations:**
- image_02: the hair is "cut neat at her shoulders and tucked behind one ear" in the text, but falls loose and layered past the shoulders in the art (see 6).
- image_02: the "super tall" rumour gets no visual payoff; she looks average height (see 5).
- image_02: the text has her counting on her fingers ("six times now?") while the CG, which stays up through that line, shows an open-handed wave. Minor, but it's on screen at the same moment.
- image_02 tone: very short skirt and pin-up framing against restrained prose; clone-stamped black-haired students in the foreground (see 9).
- image_01 matches its text well (cherry branch, petals, cloth strip, blood at the back of the wrist, a sword from "training"). The only absence is the river, which is presented as a sound anyway.

**Would I keep reading? What hooked me, what would make me stop?**
Yes, I'd read chapter 2.
- *Hooks:* (1) the household: Dad's handwriting he won't write over, Mom answering with a floorboard creak, Akane straightening her own scarf "before I can reach for it". That's the strongest writing here. (2) Two heroines who choose to like each other, with the cost of that visible in Nanoha's interlude. (3) Natsuki's grin stutter and the déjà vu question. I want to know what she remembers. (4) The Ōzakura visit on Saturday as an obvious next destination.
- *What would make me stop:* the reincarnation thread turning into a lore dump at the tree; Nanoha becoming the designated loser whose niceness is used against her; Kōhei's obliviousness being stretched for several more chapters after the chapter has made Nanoha's feelings this obvious; the art drifting toward fan service while the prose stays literary; or small logic snags like the calendar and the class list piling up.

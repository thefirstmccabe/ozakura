# Dialogue diagnosis: Pilot v2, Prologue + Ch1 (build b0, Oct 17)

**Read:** style guide §3b–§3c (Oct 18 copy), `production/pilot-v2/scene-outline.md` (Prologue, Ch1 1.1–1.7), `production/pilot-v2/script/ch01.ozs` (all of it), lint `b0-ch1-oct17.txt`. Line numbers below are `ch01.ozs` lines.

**Bottom line:** The chapter's skeleton is right. Every outline beat is present, address is mostly correct, and the good material is very good: the kitchen-latch paragraph, the callus read, "That's sort of my point," the drawer irony in the interlude, the wrong-day trash. The dialogue fails in three ways. It runs on buttons (about 15 of 21 exchanges end on a quip). One self-praise and "very" deadpan template is shared by Akane, Natsuki and Nanoha. And the narration keeps telling the reader how lines were said and what they meant. The lunch scene, the triangle's first real exchange, is the weakest stretch: Natsuki hands out verdicts and Kōhei matches her line for line.

**Count:** 51 line-level problems (section 2) and 9 scene-level problems (section 3), 60 in total.

## 3. Scene-level problems

1. **Nearly every exchange ends on a neat button.** About 15 of 21 exchanges close on a quip. The river walk ends five of six exchanges on a Nanoha last word (124, 128, 138, 146/147, 155), and lunch fires five buttons in seven lines (268–274). The exchanges that trail off or miss are the best in the chapter: "…That's sort of my point." (73), "Mm." (149), "Wednesday." (276), "That's—yes. That's fine." (294), "Anyway. Thanks." (333), "She did." (351). Nanoha wins every exchange, so her scorekeeping stops meaning anything.
2. **Humor mechanisms are shared instead of owned.** One self-praise "I'm very X" template runs through Akane (71), Natsuki (270, 328) and Nanoha (155). Spoken "very" appears 8 times against a limit of 3 (lint).
   - **Akane** gets adult deadpan (65) where the outline asks for sweet, and the sheet asks for pouting and cheerful defiance.
   - **Natsuki** uses her own mechanism once (the callus, 242; the stairs, 304) and then switches to verdicts and self-labels (268, 272, 274, 289), which are the sheet's literal Nevers.
   - **Ryōsuke's** seat theory is Nanoha's theory structure.
   - **Rika, Ōno and Kōhei** (outside lunch) hold.
3. **At the flirt, Kōhei goes cleverer, not shorter.** Lines 269 and 273 trade polish with Natsuki. The sheet's rule (sincere flirting makes him shorter) is exactly what 275 does: "Club starts Wednesday." That line should be his whole answer to the volley. The outline's "warmer Kōhei with easy smiles" became banter.
4. **Public Nanoha is over budget.** The sheet allows one dry understatement. With Natsuki she has six: 283 (clipboard), 287 (vending machine), 292 (the second half), 308, 325 and 327. Line 325 is the one to keep, because it's a real dodge after a dangerous question. The rest make her a quipster with a stranger, and the Japanese drops to plain form to match.
5. **Ryōsuke's entrance uses the banned topic.** §3b lists the hot-dog debate and "sub roll" as tics and names taiyaki as his Ch1 argument. The outline (approved before §3b) still says hot dogs; flag the conflict to the Story thread. The seat theory (185–187) then gives him a second, borrowed bit.
6. **The narration repeats or vouches for the dialogue.**
   - "Says it like": 30, 31, 147 and 353 (the limit is one a chapter).
   - "The way you": 336, which the outline requires, and 353.
   - "Something about": 271 and 295.
   - The laugh-to-close device: 295 and 329.
   - Explanations of a beat just shown: 74, 125, 135, 201, 285, 317, 337, and 36–37 in the prologue.
7. **Feelings are stated instead of shown at key turns.**
   - 146: Nanoha explains why she asked.
   - 36–37: the dream's feeling is named right after "I can't put a name to."
   - 337: the reason behind her "decision" is spelled out.
   - 285: Natsuki's surprise is interpreted for us.
   - 352–353: "I like her" is fine as a line she *chooses* to say (the interlude loads it), but 353 then decodes it.
8. **Three girls read Kōhei's face** (the tic is two): Akane at 67–69 and 96, then Nanoha's impression and catalogue at 358–361. Natsuki reading his callus (242) is fine; it's her physical-detail mechanism and isn't about his face. Cut Akane's face reads so Nanoha's catalogue stands alone.
9. **Outline and §3c beats are blunted.**
   - "You smiled." should end the chapter (outline 1.7: "The chapter ends there"). Instead it gets ten more lines of bit (number four, number one, "Secret").
   - The "Chibi" plant (§3c planned shift 1) is missing.
   - Akane is written sharp, not sweet (outline 1.1).

*Outside this check:* the lint's five sprite-expression flags (86, 95, 131, 259, 327: deadpan or smirk left on screen) are presentation, not dialogue. One of them puts `nanoha<deadpan>` on "Kō-chan. Your collar." (131), which is a tender beat under §3c.

## 4. The five highest-value fixes (ranked)

1. **Rebuild the lunch volley (268–274, 289).** Natsuki should ask and notice (posture, hands, what's in his lunch box) instead of issuing verdicts and self-labels. Kōhei should answer short and practical, ending at "Club starts Wednesday," never with a polished counter-line.
2. **Give the deadpan back to private Nanoha alone.** Strip it from Akane (65, 71; make her sweet, pouting and defiant about his *hovering* rather than his face, 67–69 and 96), from Natsuki (270, 328) and from public Nanoha (327, 287). Cut spoken "very" down to the one canonical "very convenient back" (153).
3. **Cut every narration line that says how a line was said or what it meant:** 30–31 (trim to one clause at most), 36–37, 74b, 125, 135, 147, 201b, 271, 285, 295, 317a, 329, 337, 353. Let the dialogue and the voice acting carry the beat.
4. **End the trash walk on "You smiled" and plant "Chibi."** Drop the catalogue tail (362–363) so the outline's last note lands, and answer "Good night, Kō-chan" with "Night, Chibi" as §3c requires.
5. **Swap Ryōsuke's hot-dog and sub-roll debate for the §3b taiyaki head-or-tail argument.** Keep the mid-argument entrance and its structure, and cut the seat theory (185–187, plus the callback in 208) so the argument is his whole introduction.

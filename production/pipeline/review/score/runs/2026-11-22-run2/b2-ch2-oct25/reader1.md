# Final answers

## When I became fairly sure (70%+)
- **That the dream is a past life tied to the cherry tree:** at the end of Chapter 1, image_07 (kimono woman binding a swordsman's wrist under blossoms) plus "She says it like she's been waiting a very long time." and "That I kept them waiting far too long." That got me to 70%; the dojo match ("She was there before I moved." / "This time I don't think.") and the tear in the catch pushed it to ~80%.
- **That Nanoha loves Kōhei** (not a checkpoint question, but it was the first thing I was sure of): "When I finally get to the board, Fukuda's not in Two-B this year… She went looking for mine before her own." Locked by "She did not let herself think about why it had needed deciding at all."
- I **never** got to 70% on who he ends up with (peak: Natsuki 62% after the catch) or on who the dream woman is (peak: Natsuki 60%). I think that's by design and it's working: the triangle feels genuinely open after two chapters.

## Hints that felt heavy-handed, too obvious, or too coy
- **Heavy: Natsuki's "we've met before" is hit about five times in two chapters:** the grin stutter, "I feel like I've met him somewhere before," the debana-kote ("She was there before I moved," plus Takatsuki spelling it out), the unexplained tear, and "i knew what he was going to do before he did it. every time." Each one is fine. Together they're a drumbeat. I'd cut one, probably Takatsuki's "I have never once been there first," since the panel already shows it, or the "met him somewhere before" line to Nanoha, which is the most on-the-nose.
- **Heavy: the grin stutter is underlined three ways at once:** CG swap (image_05 → 06 → 05) + "Just long enough that I'm sure I'm the only one who sees it." + "I realize I've been holding my pen hard enough to hurt." Any two would do.
- **Heavy (but I didn't mind): Nanoha's interlude closes her side of the mystery completely in Chapter 1** ("She did not let herself think about why it had needed deciding at all"). Romance readers will be fine with it; there's no "does she like him?" left, only "will she act."
- **Just right: the wrist motif** (callus on the left palm → 水 written on the inside of her wrist "just under her cuff" → kote → the dream's wrist cut). Subtle and rewarding. Best clue work in the build.
- **Just right: the river/petals/waiting details that quietly point at Nanoha** against the sword/wrist details pointing at Natsuki. That's what keeps the dream-woman question alive.
- **Too coy: the prologue** (fog intersection, curve mirror, a hand in a modern zip-up jacket on a real katana, a wall of fog). It has no woman, it doesn't match the recurring dream we're later shown (river, blossoms, period kimono), and nothing in two chapters refers back to it. I can't tell whether it's the "dream I've had since I was small," a flash-forward, or something else. One line of narration tying it in (or out) would help.
- **Coy, close to the line: Dad.** "He wrote it last spring," "Dad's handwriting," the hakama "off center for a year," but never whether he died or left. Fine for now; by Chapter 3 it'll start to feel withheld. Same with Mom behind the fusuma and Akane's dizziness: set up strongly in part 1, then absent for all of Chapter 2.

## With the names covered, could I tell them apart?
- **Easily:** Rika (exclamation stacks, repetition, "Nacchan"), Akane ("Geez, Onii-chan!" "Yaaay"), Takatsuki (no contractions: "It is the hardest thing in kendo to do on purpose," "Luck is not a technique"), the manager ("Lifesaver!"), Chie (lowercase texting, caps).
- **Mostly:** Natsuki vs. Nanoha. Nanoha is deadpan, absurd-logical ("I've prepared a theory," "It's paperwork") and goes small and formal when it matters ("Um—," "I'm working up to it"). Natsuki is louder and challenge-driven ("Who do I get to fight first?", "So be afraid.", "Race you!") with casual tics ("Eh," "No way," "Okay, okay"). **But both girls have the same narrative function: noticing things about Kōhei's body and face** (Nanoha catalogues his faces and says "You smiled"; Natsuki reads his callus, his apron knot, his posture, his ears). Lines like "You smiled." / "Do you always sit this straight?" / "Are your ears always that red?" could be either girl. That's the one place they blur, and it's the most important pair to keep distinct.
- **Some overlap:** Tokiwa and Takatsuki are both "pedantic corrector" types (Tokiwa on taiyaki thermodynamics, Takatsuki on zanshin). Takatsuki's no-contraction formality separates them, but they fill the same comic slot.
- **Some overlap:** Kōhei and Kirishima are both terse ("On it." / "Shoes off."). Kirishima's lines are commands; Kōhei's are deflections. Fine in context, closer than I'd like with names covered.

## Text vs. illustration mismatches
- **Prologue (image_01–03) vs. the dream in part 4 (image_07):** the prologue hand wears a modern zip-up jacket; the dream swordsman wears a dark period sleeve, with a kimono woman. If the prologue is meant to be the same recurring dream, it's a mismatch; if not, it's unexplained (see above).
- **Natsuki's look:** the CGs give her bright copper-red hair and green eyes, the most striking thing on screen, but the text describes only "long, in soft waves, brushed until it shines." A narrator who notices a frog hair clip wearing a crown would notice red hair.
- **image_08:** "Are your ears always that red?" His cheeks are flushed in the CG, not obviously his ears. Trivial, and the line comes after the CG ends.
- **Everything else matched well:** the collar CG (petal on her braid, height difference, hands on his collar), the stutter swap, the catch (one tear, sweat, faded gi vs. his dark one, bucket and rag, orange window light, hand on back + forearm), the wrist-binding dream (cut on the inside of the wrist, cloth strip, katana, petals).

## Other problems
- **Pronoun bug, part 6:** "Two minutes later she's wiped the practice schedule off the whiteboard…" follows Rika's line, so "she" reads as Rika, but it's Natsuki. Name her.
- **Translator's-note overload:** twelve TL notes in part 6 alone, six in a row between gearing up and the match, mostly repeating glosses already given inline. It kills momentum right before the best scene. Same doubling in part 1 (karaage, kinpira).
- **"writes 水 (Wednesday)":** 水 by itself is "water"; it's shorthand for Wednesday. The gloss slightly misinforms.
- **Nanoha says "Kōhei" and then "Kō-chan" in the same scene (part 4).** I read it as an intentional serious-then-soft shift. If it isn't intentional, it's inconsistent with "Kō-chan" in part 1.
- **"…It's April." as the rebuttal to cedar pollen** (said twice, by Kōhei and by Chie). I believe the pollen season in that region does run into April, so Natsuki may actually be right. Still works as a joke; flagging in case a Japanese reader balks.
- **Balance:** Nanoha is offscreen for nearly all of Chapter 2 (a sticky note, a lit window, one mention in Natsuki's interlude). If the triangle is meant to be open, Chapter 2 tips it hard. That may be fine if Chapter 3 is hers, but on its own it makes the endgame feel decided.
- **Minor:** the narration switches between "Mizuno" (Kōhei's voice) and the "Natsuki:" speaker tag. Consistent and readable, but it caused one double-take at first use.

## Would I keep reading?
Yes. The hook is the open question, fate vs. the present. Is the dream woman the girl who just arrived, or the one who's been next door for sixteen years? And will the story let the *present* win? The prose has real quality in its small moments: Dad's handwriting on the garbage day, "I eat my rice. It's good rice. I made it myself.", the boxes with cities crossed out, eleven photos with ten kept. The dojo chapter is the high point: funny ensemble, a match that's clear even to a non-kendo reader, and a romantic payoff ("That's why I'm so happy").

What would make me stop: Nanoha turning into a food-delivery service who only gets interludes; the "we've met before" beats repeating without new information; the family thread (Dad, Mom, Akane) quietly vanishing after Chapter 1; or the TL notes continuing at the part-6 rate. The fog prologue needs to start paying off within a chapter or two, or it'll feel like a cold open from a different story.

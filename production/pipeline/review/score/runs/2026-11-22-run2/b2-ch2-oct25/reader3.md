- **What the dream is (a past life, tied to the present romance):** 70%+ at part 4, "There's a dream I've had since I was small… There's a cut on the inside of it, where a blade got past me in training," plus image 07 (kimono, real sword, cloth bandage). It went to about 85% at part 6, "left foot forward (the wrong foot)," and part 7's tear.
- **That Natsuki "knows" him from somewhere supernatural, not just from a tournament:** 70%+ at part 7, "like she doesn't know where it came from either."
- **That Saturday at the shrine is a three-way collision:** about 90% at part 8, "Carrying trash bags up at a shrine." / "Okay. That's my project, then." / "I've been wanting to photograph that thing since Monday."

## Hints that felt heavy-handed, too obvious, or too coy

**Heavy-handed / too obvious**
1. **Natsuki's recognition thread is hit in nearly every scene.** The grin stutter (part 2) is perfect on its own. Then come "I feel like I've met him somewhere before" said out loud to Nanoha on day one (part 3), Takatsuki's "I have never once been there first" (part 6), the involuntary tear (part 7), and "i knew what he was going to do before he did it. every time" (part 8). That's five signals in three days. Each is fine in isolation, but stacked, they tell me the answer instead of letting me find it. I'd cut the spoken "met him before" line. The stutter and the tear already carry it.
2. **"Someone who knows which drawer sticks in your kitchen."** Natsuki happens to name the exact detail from Nanoha's kitchen routine. The irony works, but it reads as authorial. A vaguer line ("someone who knows your house") would let me make the link myself.
3. **Wednesday is planted three times** (the 水 on her wrist, "Club starts Wednesday," "See you tomorrow, Fujisawa. Wednesday."). It's minor, but by the third it's underlining.

**Too coy**
4. **The opening montage** (images 01–03: foggy crossroads with a convex mirror, a hand in a modern zip jacket gripping a sheathed sword, a wall of fog rolling down the road). It isn't labelled as a dream, has no woman in it, isn't referenced again in either chapter, and the modern jacket contradicts the kimono-era dream in image 07, even though the sword hilt is the same. If the montage is meant to be "the dream," it doesn't read that way. If it's a flash-forward, it needs one line of anchoring somewhere in two chapters. As it stands, I just forgot about it.
5. **Kōhei's pen-grip** ("I realize I've been holding my pen hard enough to hurt"). Is he reacting to her beauty, to the attention, or to déjà vu of his own? It's never followed up, and his POV never admits to any recognition on his side. If Kōhei feels nothing, the line misleads. If he does, he should notice it once more.
6. **Nanoha's dream clues are so quiet they risk being invisible:** hands tending him, "Again" echoing the dream's "Hurt again," her grandfather's shrine under the Ōzakura, "she's been waiting a very long time." I caught them only because I was hunting. And Nanoha is almost entirely offstage in Chapter 2: a sticky note and a lit window. If she's meant to stay a live contender, for the romance or for the dream, a casual reader will have written her off by the end of Chapter 2. She needs one active beat in this chapter.

## Dialogue that felt lively

- Nanoha's morning banter in part 1: "You thought it in a short-person direction." / "I have Rika-chan. Rika-chan counts as several." / "We've always been keeping score. You're losing."
- "I have met him before. Every day. For sixteen years." Polite, with an edge.
- Nanoha's face catalogue: "That one's number four. 'Somebody wants to fight me.'" / "What's number one?" / "Secret."
- The restaurant: "Do you check ID for the kids' plate?" and the "Lifesaver!" counter paying off on "…Wait. Do you work here?" / "I'm new."
- The kendo lesson: "Somebody hits you in the head, and the referee goes, 'There was a head.'" / "You're doing great, visual aid." / "He's the 'before' picture!"
- After the match: "You didn't let me have that point, did you?" / "No." / "I know. That's why I'm so happy."
- After the catch: "Yeah. You too. I mean— you're welcome." / "Are your ears always that red?" / "…No."
- The Chie texts in full, especially "ok that's actually kind of hot" / "CHIE" and "why does everyone keep saying that."
- Takatsuki: "Yes. It has been off center for a year." and "Luck is not a technique." Kirishima: "That was a joke."

## Flat lines (8), with why

1. **Akane: "Geez, Onii-chan! Every single morning!"** This is the stock little-sister register. With the name covered, she could be any VN imouto.
2. **Akane: "And I slept! Super well! I didn't wake up once!"** Same problem, with three exclamation marks. The sister who is hiding an illness deserves a more specific voice. The one good Akane line ("…You always are.") shows she has one.
3. **Rika: "Two-B! You're in Two-B! I'm in Two-B! It's destiny! It's in the stars! I'm making us matching keychains!"** This is the stock genki friend. With Akane, she's one of two characters I couldn't tell apart without names (see below).
4. **Natsuki: "I do kendo, and I'm looking for a dojo that'll put up with me. I also love horror movies, the cheesier the better, so if anyone wants to watch a bad one with me, come find me."** It reads as a character profile delivered as dialogue, and the horror-movie trait barely recurs (one "is this building haunted?" line). It's a hook planted and not used.
5. **Kōhei: "I've always been comfortable holding one. Since the first time I picked one up."** It sounds like a clue rather than a sixteen-year-old answering a question. The narration line after it ("with one in my hands, everything else goes quiet") does the job better.
6. **Natsuki: "Dō. The side of the body."** (and the rest of the four-targets list) is a glossary read aloud, and then the translator's notes gloss it again. Rika's interruptions rescue the scene, but these lines are pure exposition.
7. **Natsuki: "Allergies. It's allergies. Cedar pollen. Kanagawa's full of cedar, right?"** "Kanagawa's full of cedar, right?" is the author placing the setting; nobody covering a tear says the prefecture name.
8. **Natsuki: "Takes one to know one."** The idiom is normally a retort to an insult, so it lands slightly off. She means "I'm a kendoka, so I can spot one," which a fresher line would say better.

## With the names covered, could I tell the characters apart?

- **Clearly distinct:** **Takatsuki** (no contractions, formal: "It is the hardest thing in kendo to do on purpose."), **Kirishima** (one-sentence imperatives, dry: "Shoes off." / "From memory."), **Kōhei** (laconic, dry deflection: "I'm standing in my own kitchen." / "It stays tied."), and the **manager** (one note, which is the joke).
- **Mostly distinct, with some overlap:** **Nanoha** and **Natsuki** are both witty, deadpan girls who run bits on Kōhei (Nanoha's staff-room "folder" theory, Natsuki's kids'-plate ID gag). Lines like "You're doing great, visual aid" or "I do supervise beautifully" could swap owners. What separates them is mostly *address* ("Kō-chan"/"Kōhei" vs. "Fujisawa") and register: Nanoha is drier and shorter with lots of "Mm." and declaratives, while Natsuki asks more questions and is louder ("Race you!"). Without the address terms, I'd misattribute maybe one line in five.
- **Hard to tell apart:** **Akane** and **Rika** are both exclamation-mark genki. Remove "Onii-chan" and "Nano-chan" and they're interchangeable. **Tokiwa** (Ryōsuke) has a distinct pseudo-scientific lecture voice ("It's thermodynamics."), but it's a cousin of Takatsuki's precision. One scene isn't enough to fix him.
- **Naming confusion:** the speaker label says "Ryōsuke" while the narration says "Tokiwa," and the build never connects the two names explicitly. I had to infer they're the same person. (Mizuno/Natsuki is fine because her intro gives both.)

## Mismatches between text and illustrations

1. **Image 04 (collar):** the text says she folds his *shirt collar* back down over his tie. The art reads as her straightening his tie knot. It's minor.
2. **Images 01–03 vs. 07:** the opening montage's sword hand wears a modern zip jacket, but the dream (07), which shows the same wrapped hilt, is kimono-era. If both are "the dream," they contradict each other. If the montage is something else, the text never says what.
3. **Two art styles:** 04, 05, 06 and 08 are sketchy line-and-watercolor manga CGs. 01–03 and 07 are softer painterly anime backgrounds. They're consistent within each group, but the switch is noticeable.
4. **Natsuki's look:** bright auburn hair and green eyes (05, 06, 08) on a Japanese transfer student. The text never remarks on it, even in a scene where the class is whispering about how she looks. It isn't a contradiction, but it's a gap a reader notices.
5. Small: in part 1, "Nanoha walks on the river side," but in 04 the river is behind Kōhei. That's explainable because she's stepped in front of him.
6. Everything else matches well: the petal on the braid (04), the stutter (06 vs. 05), the cloth, branch and hilt (07), and the tear, wet floor, faded indigo gi and his hand on her forearm (08).
(The manga insert in part 6 came to me only as bracketed panel descriptions with no image files, so I couldn't check its art against the text.)

## Would I keep reading?

Yes, definitely through Saturday at the shrine.

**What hooked me:** the collision that's now set up (Natsuki heading for Nanoha's shrine with her camera), and the past-life clues hidden in kendo technique (wrong foot, wrong distance, the knot). That's the cleverest writing here, and it rewards readers who know the sport. The grief details are also understated and very good: Dad's handwriting on the whiteboard and taking the trash out on the wrong day. And Natsuki's interior ("It's the second semesters I never get to") made her a person and not just a fated-girl device. The two heroines genuinely liking each other is a good choice; it makes the triangle hurt instead of turning catty.

**What would make me stop:**
- If Nanoha keeps getting sidelined into a pure *makeine* who only appears through notes and windows. She was the heart of Chapter 1 and is almost a ghost in Chapter 2.
- If the fate thread keeps being *announced* (more tears, more "met him before," more uncanny reads) without escalating into something concrete. It's at saturation now.
- More translator's-note pileups like the one before the match in part 6. Six notes back to back, most duplicating inline glosses, at the moment the scene should be speeding up.
- If the opening fog montage never pays off, or Akane and Mom stay set dressing. Akane's dizziness and Mom's closed fusuma are the most quietly worrying things in the build, and Chapter 2 drops them almost completely.

**Who do I want him to end up with?** Nanoha, maybe 65%. **Who is the woman in the dream?** I don't know. Sure-ness: 0%, nothing to go on. If anyone, I'd half-guess the dream is about someone in the fog the sword-hand is going toward. The question assumes a woman I haven't been shown. (If she's supposed to register in the prologue, she doesn't.)

**What does the dream have to do with the present day?** Guesses only:

**What's going on / what kind of story:** A quiet slice-of-life school romance with a grief undercurrent. Brother parenting his sick little sister (dizziness, a clinic day on the 18th) and a mother who's checked out. Probably with a supernatural/folklore layer around the old cherry tree and the fog. Small-town rural setting.

**Lines and images that drove this:**

**Small notes so far:**

# Final answers

## When did I become fairly sure (70%+) of anything?

- **Who he ends up with:** never. I peaked at 60% Natsuki (Checkpoints 7–8, after "And she's crying." and "i knew what he was going to do before he did it. every time") and swung back each time Nanoha got a scene. For a triangle three chapters in, that's a success, not a failure.
- **Who I want him with:** I hit 70% Nanoha at the end of Chapter 1, on "What's number one?" / "Secret." Natsuki's Chapter 2 pulled me back to roughly even, and I ended at 55% Nanoha.
- **Who the dream woman is:** never above 55%. It swung between Natsuki and Nanoha four times. The prologue never shows a woman at all, so I had nothing to anchor on until the end-of-Chapter-1 dream.
- **What the dream has to do with the present:** I became fairly sure (about 75%) that the dream is a past-life/ancestral memory set at the Ōzakura at "Under it, for a second, the air smells like snow. / In April. Cold and clean. And the branch is lower than it should be, low enough that I almost duck." Before that I was at about 65% that it was "some kind of reincarnation story," mainly from "I feel like I've met him somewhere before" plus the tear.
- **What kind of story it is:** about 70% supernatural reincarnation romance by the tear in the dojo ("Her eyes are wide and confused, like she doesn't know where it came from either.").

## Hints that felt heavy-handed, too obvious or too coy

**Heavy-handed:**
1. "She didn't know why she'd cried. She didn't know how she'd known where he would move, either." (Natsuki's interlude.) The texts right above it already showed both. This is the story underlining its own mystery.
2. The anticipation motif is used four times in three chapters: the debana-kote, the Saturday sparring ("her sword tip is already where I'm going"), the texts ("i knew what he was going to do before he did it"), and Street Brawlers ("Kagero is already standing where she lands"). Each one works; together, by the fourth, it's a drum.
3. "Someone who knows which drawer sticks in your kitchen." (Natsuki to Nanoha.) Lands, but it's the exact drawer from Part 1, so it's a little too neat.
4. "She did not let herself think about why it had needed deciding at all." The previous line ("the way you shut a window against the wind") already did the work.
5. "I take pictures of every town I live in." / "So I have proof I was there." A slightly on-the-nose theme statement, though I liked it.

**Too coy:**
1. **The prologue.** A foggy modern road, a curve mirror, a hand in a dark zip-up sleeve holding a sheathed sword, a wall of fog. None of it is referenced again in three chapters. No fog, no zip-up sword-bearer, no curve mirror. As a casual reader I'd have forgotten it by Chapter 2. And there's no woman in it, so the "woman in the dream" question has nothing to start from until the end of Chapter 1.
2. **The dream never recurs** in Chapters 2 and 3. It appears once (end of Ch 1), and after that only the snow-smell under the branch touches it. For a story asking "who is the dream woman" at every checkpoint, I'd expect one more fragment by the end of Ch 3: a second line from her, a detail of her kimono, anything.
3. **The point of light (image_14)** is so small and faint that a reader on a phone or iPad could miss it entirely. Then "…You came back." reads as addressed to Kōhei, which changes its meaning.
4. "Just stopping for a rest" / "Looks like rain tomorrow." I liked this as a mystery, but readers without Japanese folklore won't know there's a superstition about stumbling on shrine steps. And "I've heard them say it on these steps my whole life. I've never once asked." strains belief a little for a curious 16-year-old. Coy, but acceptable.

**About right:** "Her grin stutters" (image_05 → 06), "I feel like I've met him somewhere before," the tear in the dojo, "…You came back." + "A petal." These are each deniable and each land.

- **Rika:** caps and exclamation storms ("Oh! Oh oh oh"). Unmistakable.
- **Ishida:** pedantic overstatement ("It's thermodynamics."). Distinct, but barely used after Part 2.
- **Takatsuki:** no contractions, formal and literal. Distinct.
- **Kirishima:** short imperatives and one-sentence verdicts. Distinct.
- **Akane / Mio / Genji / Emi:** distinct (sisterly whine; third-person bossiness; old-man "Good, good"; warm "come in, come in").

## Other problems I noticed (text/production)

1. Dream ending: "the only thing left is a feeling I can't put a name to," followed immediately by two lines that name it ("That someone was waiting for me...").
2. Translator's notes double-gloss (inline parenthetical *and* a full note) for karaage, chibi, shinai, onigiri, torii, tamagoyaki and others. The dojo scene carries seven notes. I started skipping them, so the system is doing less than it could. Keep the inline gloss for common words and save full notes for things that matter (ki-ken-tai-itchi, zanshin, debana).
3. Tense slip: "Takatsuki was on the floor before anyone..." in otherwise present-tense narration (Part 10).
4. Background label "[Background: natsuki apartment, night]" is lowercase and reads like a placeholder or file name, unlike every other location label.
5. "the first-years who bow to her in the hallway" (Part 9). It's the first week of the school year, so this needs a reason (a committee role?) or a different example.
6. Natsuki asks "Head first or tail first?" about taiyaki, an argument she wasn't present for. It's a common debate, so it works, but it reads like an accidental callback.
7. "…It's April." as a rebuttal to cedar pollen. April is still pollen season in Japan; the Japanese-language pass may flag this. (Chie's "it's april" callback means the joke is load-bearing, so it may be better to make the joke not depend on the season being wrong.)
8. Repeated "[Natsuki appears on screen]" lines while she's already on screen (Parts 5, 6, 12). Presumably expression changes; harmless in play, but worth confirming that they're not re-triggering an entrance animation.

## Would I keep reading?

**Yes.** Honestly, more willingly than I expected from the first page.

**What hooked me:**
- A love triangle where the two girls genuinely like each other, and each makes a generous move toward the other (the tour; the broom; the lunch invite). That's rarer and more interesting than rivalry.
- Nanoha. "I have met him before. Every day. For sixteen years." / "You're not the only person who loves her, Kōhei." / "…You came back."
- The grief undercurrent, handled with restraint: Dad's handwriting on the whiteboard, the trash taken out on the wrong day, Mom behind the fusuma, Akane not calling *because* he'd come.
- The quiet supernatural turn at the tree. Snow smell in April, a branch that's briefly lower, three people reacting differently. It's deniable and exactly the right size.
- Akane's unexplained illness. I suspect it's connected to the tree or the fog, and I want to know.

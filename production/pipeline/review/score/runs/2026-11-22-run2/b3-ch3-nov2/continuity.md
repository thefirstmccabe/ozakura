**Draft:** `production/pilot-v2/script/ch03.ozs` (paths are relative to ROOT = `scratchpad/score2/b3-ch3-nov2`).
**Checked against:** `story/canon.md`, `story/chapter-map.md` (Ch1–3 rows, running threads), `story/pilot-notes.md` §3, `story/style-guide.md` §1a–§2 and §3b–§3c, `ch01.ozs`, `ch02.ozs`, `production/pilot-v2/scene-outline.md` (Ch3 scenes and the "### Ch3" targets).

## Problems (most important first)

### 1. Akane's diagnosis is Open in canon, and the draft settles it (Open item treated as settled)
- **Where:** `ch03.ozs:94–96` (#c3_0434–0436), plus `ch03.ozs:24–25` (#c3_0065–0066).
- **Text:** nanoha: "They've checked everything. Twice. They couldn't find anything wrong. It isn't dangerous." / kohei: "And they still don't know what it is." / nanoha: "No. But they know what it isn't." Also kohei: "Did she take her—" / nanoha: "She ate. She took them." (JP 薬も飲んだ).
- **Why:** canon §8 Akane says "**Diagnosis Open;** symptoms must eventually be consistent" (`canon.md:576`), and §11 lists "Akane's diagnosis" as an open question (`canon.md:770`). The draft fixes three things as fact: the tests were done twice, they found nothing, and the condition isn't dangerous. Every later illness beat has to fit those three facts, including FB4's hospital rush (canon §6) and the faint at the Ch12 meet. The regular medicine is a fourth new fact, and it sits awkwardly next to "they couldn't find anything wrong": what is she taking? `pilot-notes.md:266` says these lines came "after the author's decisions on Dad and Akane" (Nov 2), but canon doesn't record any such decision, and canon governs.
- **Fix:** if the author did decide this, the Story thread folds it into canon §8 (Akane, Health) and §11, adds a line to `canon-revisions.md`, and settles what the medicine is (or cuts "Did she take her—"). If he didn't, keep the diagnosis open and let the reassurance be Nanoha's, not the doctors'. For example: nanoha: "They've done the tests. Twice. Nothing showed up." / kohei: "And they still don't know what it is." / nanoha: "No. But it passed. It always passes." Either way, log the medicine as [P].

### 2. Dad's cause of death is a new family fact that canon doesn't have
- **Where:** `ch03.ozs:97` (#c3_0437).
- **Text:** "Dad's was cancer. In his pancreas. By the time anyone found it, we were counting in months."
- **Why:** canon gives only "died after several months of illness around Kōhei's entry into high school" (`canon.md:484`). The draft is consistent with that, but it makes the cause specific in shipped text. The September flashback (hospital, the bills; canon §6, Ch21) would be locked to it. It also wasn't in the Ch3 outline or targets. As with problem 1, pilot-notes credits it to a Nov 2 author decision that canon doesn't record.
- **Fix:** if the author decided it, add it to canon §8 (Kōhei, family) and §6 ("Flashback: Dad's death"), with a line in canon-revisions. If not, mark it [P], and consider dropping the organ ("Dad's was cancer. By the time anyone found it, we were counting in months.") so Ch21 keeps some room.

### 3. Natsuki's practice bag appears out of nowhere (prop continuity)
- **Where:** `ch03.ozs:262` (#c3_0405), `ch03.ozs:272` (#c3_0276).
- **Text:** "At the bottom of the steps, Mizuno shoulders her practice bag." / "Mizuno stands there with her bag half on her shoulder."
- **Why:** the bag isn't mentioned on the walk up (`:153` lists only the track clothes and the camera), at the top, or while they sweep. A reader can't tell whether she carried it up a steep stairway or left it unattended at the bottom for a couple of hours. A kendo bag with armor is bulky, so it should be placed somewhere.
- **Fix:** establish it once on the way up, e.g. add to `:153` "…her practice bag over one shoulder," and at the top have her drop it by the shrine office. At `:262` it becomes "Mizuno hitches her practice bag back onto her shoulder."

### 4. The paper-cup telephone gives the windows a childhood origin and touches FB3, whose age is Open
- **Where:** `ch03.ozs:64` (#c3_0097).
- **Text:** "…a gap so narrow that when we were little we ran a string between our windows with two paper cups, and were disappointed when it didn't work. We've passed a lot of books across it since."
- **Why:** canon's FB3 is "**The windows begin** (childhood or early middle school)… The origin of the window conversations" (`canon.md:361`), and style guide §3c says its age is Open (`style-guide.md:264`). A narrated "when we were little" window game pins FB3 to childhood, or else contradicts it if FB3 ends up in early middle school. It's also backstory told in narration, which canon §1 asks to save for a scene.
- **Fix:** cut the cup telephone and keep "We've passed a lot of books across it." Or make it ageless ("…so narrow that a paper-cup telephone would be pointless. We've passed a lot of books across it."). If it stays, log it as [P] tied to FB3's age.

# Final answers

## When I became fairly sure (70%+) of anything the checkpoints asked
- **That the dream is a past life tied to this town, not a random dream:** about 70% at the end of chapter 1, on image_07 (kimono, katana, cherry branch) plus "She says it like she's been waiting a very long time." Pushed past 80% by Natsuki's interlude: "She didn't know why she'd cried. She didn't know how she'd known where he would move, either."
- **That the Ōzakura is the link between dream and present:** 75%+ at "Under it, for a second, the air smells like snow." and Nanoha's "…You came back."
- **That Nanoha is in love with Kōhei** (not asked, but it drove everything): 90% at "She did not let herself think about why it had needed deciding at all."
- **Who he ends up with:** never reached 70%. Peak was Natsuki 65% after the catch scene ("And she's crying."), drifting back to 55% by the end.
- **Who the dream woman is:** never reached 70%. Peak was Natsuki 60% (parts 7–8). By the end it was a three-way split (Natsuki / a spirit at the tree / Nanoha). I think that ambiguity is intended and mostly works, though see "too coy" below.

## Hints that felt heavy-handed, too obvious or too coy
**Heavy-handed / too obvious:**
- Natsuki to Nanoha: "Someone who knows which drawer sticks in your kitchen." Of every possible example, she lands on the exact drawer from the opening scene. The dramatic irony works, but I saw the author's hand.
- The tear scene already shows Natsuki doesn't know why she cried ("like she doesn't know where it came from either"); the interlude then states it again ("She didn't know why she'd cried. She didn't know how she'd known where he would move, either"). The second telling is a little explanatory.
- "That someone was waiting for me. / That I kept them waiting far too long." comes right after the triple "She says it like…", which already did the job. It's the chapter-ending button, so I mostly forgive it.
- Kōhei pointing out "That's what Akane said." after Nanoha's "I know. That's why." The parallel was clear; he doesn't need to say it.

**Too coy:**
- The opening fog sequence (crossroads, curve mirror, hand on a katana in a dark zip-up sleeve, fog wall) gives me nothing to hold, and three chapters later it hasn't come back at all. It doesn't connect visually or tonally to the kimono dream. I only started wondering whether the sleeve was Natsuki's when she showed up in a navy track top in chapter 3, and that's a guess. Right now it reads as a mood piece.
- Kōhei's mom vanishes after chapter 1. If her absence on Thursday night (Akane texts Nanoha, not her mother) is the point, one line from Kōhei noticing it would make it land; as it stands I couldn't tell if it was deliberate.

**Calibrated well (for balance):** the grin stutter, "I feel like I've met him somewhere before," the snow smell under the branch, "…You came back" / "A petal," and "Just stopping for a rest." Those hit the right level.

## Up to 8 flat lines, and why
1. **Nanoha:** "They've checked everything. Twice. They couldn't find anything wrong. It isn't dangerous." Kōhei obviously already knows this; it's said for the reader. In the middle of the best scene in the build, it's the one moment that sounds like exposition.
2. **Natsuki:** "I asked Nanoha on Thursday, after you'd gone to work, if I could come up and take pictures." Pure logistics delivered as dialogue. The reveal matters ("Thursday. After school. Before the window."), but the line itself is a stage direction.
3. **Kōhei:** "Mizuno came in after karaoke, with some girls from class. She stayed till close." A recap of the scene we just read. Nanoha's reaction carries the beat; this line doesn't.
4. **Nanoha:** "She's nice, Kōhei. I like her." Tells what the interlude already showed better ("Somewhere between the library and the gym, Nanoha had decided that she liked Mizuno Natsuki").
5. **Akane:** "I got a little dizzy after school. On the stairs at the station. I sat down for ten minutes, and it went away." She's the bounciest voice in the house, and this reads like a clinic intake form. Sleepy is fine, but some of her voice should survive.
6. **Natsuki:** "I just mean it was a good match. I haven't had one like that in a long time." Generic; she's sharper than this everywhere else. The beat (her retreating) is right, the words are stock.
7. **Emi:** "Barging? You're saving me. Come in, come in, there's karaage that needs eating. Akane-chan's already here." Stock warm-auntie line that also has to deliver the Akane logistics. She has no voice of her own yet.
8. **Kōhei:** "That's what Akane said." Points at a parallel the reader already caught (see heavy-handed above).

## With names covered, could I tell them apart? Who sounds like whom?
Mostly yes. The distinct ones:
- **Rika** is unmistakable (stretched vowels, triple exclamation, "Oh! Oh oh oh").
- **Takatsuki** is identifiable by never using contractions ("It is," "I have never once").
- **Kirishima** speaks in short imperatives.
- **Mio** refers to herself in the third person ("Mio is the best in this house").
- **Genji** has the grandpa register ("Good, good." "Ha! This one'll do.").

Where they blur:
- **Nanoha and Natsuki share a comic rhythm.** Kōhei makes a literal objection and she reframes: Nanoha's "Then I've arrived." versus Natsuki's "Then I'm a spiritual first-year." / "Then I'm taking the long way." Both are cheerful steamrollers ("It's decided." / "I'm doing it anyway."). Natsuki is more casual ("Eh," "No way," "Okay, okay") and Nanoha more formal-deadpan ("Now then," "We don't discuss it"), so I'd get them right about 80% of the time. But lines like "So be afraid." or "You're doing great, visual aid." could come from either.
- **Ishida and Takatsuki** are both pedant boys who correct people. Takatsuki's lack of contractions saves him, but Ishida ("That isn't an opinion. It's thermodynamics.") is a lighter version of the same joke.
- **Akane and Rika:** without "Onii-chan," Akane's "And I slept! Super well! I didn't wake up once!" could be Rika.
- **Emi** has no distinguishing voice yet.

## Other problems (not dialogue, not image)
1. **Translator's notes overload.** Many terms are glossed twice, inline and again in a full note: "karaage (fried chicken)" plus a note, "Chibi (shorty)" plus a note, "Usagi-san (Mr. Rabbit)" plus a note. The kendo lesson stacks seven notes in a row (shinai, men, ki-ken-tai-itchi, zanshin, kote, dō, debana-kote). I started skipping them by part 6. Pick one: inline gloss for quick words, a note only where there's real content.
2. **Cedar-pollen gotcha.** "Cedar pollen." / "…It's April." (and Chie's "it's april"). As far as I know April is still hay-fever season in Japan, so the rebuttal that two characters use as an obvious gotcha rests on a shaky fact.
3. **Texting emojis** are heavy enough to tip into cute-overload, and 🥋 is a karate gi, which a kendo girl would probably not pick.
4. **"the first-years who bow to her in the hallway":** it's the first week of term and Nanoha isn't shown in any club, so this reads oddly.
5. **Ishida vanishes.** He promises a taiyaki diagram "at lunch," then never appears again in three chapters.
6. **Kōhei's mom drops out** after chapter 1 (see "too coy"): absent from Thursday night and from the Saturday lunch without a line.
7. **Saturday sparring result is fuzzy.** "I still take most of what there is to take" is the only sign Kōhei won; Natsuki "looks as pleased with herself as if she'd won." One clearer line would help.

## Would I keep reading?
**Yes.** I'd read chapter 4 tonight.

**What hooked me:**
- The window scene with Usagi-san. That's the best thing in the build.

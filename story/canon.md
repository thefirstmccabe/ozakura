## 1. Premise, themes, tone
A capable but overburdened Japanese high-school boy, Fujisawa Kōhei, falls into a relationship with his beloved childhood friend and next-door neighbor, Tachibana Nanoha. Meanwhile he develops an inexplicably familiar attraction to a new transfer student, Mizuno Natsuki.

In an earlier life, Kōhei and Natsuki were husband and wife. He died killing a beast that trapped them on a walk. The local cherry-tree deity granted their promise to meet again. That promise is fulfilled, but the reunion does not decide whom they should love now.

A supernatural beast then threatens their town. The tree blooms out of season, its fairy messenger grows more prominent, and the friends find that they have the access and experience to confront it. Kōhei must learn to cooperate, including with a rival who grates on him, rather than repeat his earlier life's solitary sacrifice.

**Themes:**
- Love enduring across time and death.
- Present commitments that are freely chosen.
- Belonging.
- Accepting help, and the limits of protection.
- The difference between being wanted and being known.
- Meaningful survival after heartbreak.
- **A family putting itself back together after a father's death** (Oct 10). This is one of the story's main spines, not background; see §6 "The Fujisawa family".
- Masculinity, strength and honor exercised responsibly.

**Tone:**
- A genuinely enjoyable, mostly uplifting school romcom first: a major slow burn with familiar romcom tropes done well. The author's shorthand is "Blue Box with kendo," with a consequential love triangle, a more dangerous male lead and supernatural elements.
- Ordinary chapters must be worth reading without clues or plot progress.
- Gradual oddness and past-life fragments add mystery. Later danger is real and lethal but stays personal and local.
- **The genre is promised from the start (Decided Oct 18):** the reader knows early that this is also a dark supernatural story, with many more threads of dread and horror throughout, in the Muv-Luv *Unlimited*/*Alternative* manner (Kōhei's fight flashbacks, local myths). Individual beats stay deniable. No new supernatural events are added for this; it comes from how the existing ones are told.
  - **Frame devices the author likes (Oct 18):** a flash-forward cold open, short vignettes between the Parts, and the title screen and music. **The cold open is in the pilot (Decided Oct 18):** it opens the game: two or three images of the present-day misty walk (one male hand in a modern sleeve on a sword grip, a modern landmark passing twice, the mist moving), no faces, no voices, nothing that reads as the past. Then straight to Ch1's morning; **the dream moves to the end of Ch1** (cold open plus dream back to back was too much at once). Vignette placement is Open.
    - **Parked (author, Oct 19):** the cold open needs improvement, but it waits until the very end of the project. Then the author decides what goes there and whether it's animated or music over CGs (Open). The pilot keeps the Oct 18 version until then.
  - **Dread ramp by season (Decided Oct 18):** spring, local customs; summer, kaidan (Ryōsuke tells the okuri-ōkami legend); autumn, night walks home from shifts and institutional dread; all year, fragments of Kōhei's past-life fight (mist, the mask, the grip). The trade itself is withheld until the climax, and beast threads stay separate from past-life clues.
- Readers should want to spend ordinary afternoons with this cast.
- Keep the cosmology mysterious, and keep actions and consequences clear.
- **Influences:** Tolkien, Muv-Luv, Baldr Sky and the earlier Fionavar ideas are influences, not structural obligations.

**Kendo is setting and practice, not a sports manga.** (Oct 6; supersedes the Sept 29 "sports-manga goals and scored matches.")
- **Kept:** the club as a social stage, sparring with Natsuki, and past-life recognition through movement.
- **Competitions:** rare (one or two at most), used only where the drama needs a stage, such as the meet where an opponent mocks Akane.
- **Dropped:** tournament arcs and ranking goals.
- **Why it matters to Kōhei:** he loves the feel of a sword, which comes from his past life, and finds peace in it. It is the disciplined counterweight to his anger.

**Writing guidance:**
- **Show more and tell less.** The pilot leaned on narrated explanation, and the longer format gives room to let scenes carry it.
- **In openings, a fact goes in only if the scene shows it happening.** Save backstory for real scenes. Ryōsuke arrives mid-argument on screen; he isn't introduced by a list of facts about him. (Oct 8; Oct 17, Working [P]: the topic is a Japanese one, taiyaki head or tail first, not the hot-dog debate)
- **No telegraphic name-tag intros or trait lists** ("Name. Role. Club. Glasses."). People arrive through what they do and say. Narration uses complete sentences by default. (Oct 8)
- **No manga self-reference.** Characters never compare their own situation to manga. Owning and reading manga is fine. (Oct 8)
- **Keep the strangeness subtle** across the long runtime, and drip-feed the fairy. (Oct 8)

## 2. Format and presentation
- **Format (Working, the author's leaning):** VN-primary, with frequent interludes, CGs and occasional manga pages for scenes that only work as manga. The author thinks the story works best as manga, but full manga production is too costly and inconsistent at his quality bar.
  - **Inserts are full color** (Decided Oct 10; reconfirmed Oct 12 after a side-by-side B&W test), in the VN's art style, so sprites and backgrounds serve directly as references and the reader never sees the style switch. No B&W screentone.
  - **No storyboard step (Decided Oct 12).** Claude makes inserts from the script directly, runs a robust self-review, and presents only finished pages (style guide §4a).
  - **Presentation: panel by panel (Decided Oct 13).** Inside the VN, an insert opens on the whole page, then each tap zooms to the next panel while the rest of the page stays visible but dimmed. The author: "don't change a thing." Spec in style guide §4a. **Inserts are designed for this view from the start** (every panel reads alone, one beat per tap, landscape-friendly panel shapes, surprises on a new page), not adapted from print layouts.
  - **Era treatments (Decided Oct 17, with the pilot v2 outline):** the past life is cool and desaturated with softer edges (still color, never grayscale); childhood flashbacks are warm, sunlit and slightly faded, like an old photo, with a soft vignette. The two never share a palette, so the eras never blur.
  - **No cutscenes or longer animation (Decided Oct 12).** After two rounds of tests, getting them to commercial quality would cost too much in takes and review time; pivotal moments use CGs and manga inserts instead.
  - **Kept (Decided Oct 12):** subtle 2–4 s living-still loops on CGs (hair, petals, breathing, water), and **a few very short animated moments** of the kind the models handle well, such as the fall and catch (Ch2; tested Oct 10). Motion is punctuation, not the default.
- **The feel:** a Japanese manga translated into English. Aim for manga plausibility rather than exhaustive realism.
- **Language:**
  - Keep honorifics and meaningful forms of address in natural English: -san, -kun, -chan, senpai, and sibling terms like onii-chan.
  - Bare names and changes in address matter.
  - **Address forms and pronouns (Decided Oct 17; Claude's choices, delegated by the author):** style guide §3c. Key forms: Kōhei teases Nanoha as "Chibi" (his nickname for her since middle school; it means *little kid*) and first calls her "Nanoha" to her face when they start dating, keeping "Chibi" for teasing after; she calls him "Kōhei," or "Kō-chan" in private. He and Natsuki stay on surnames ("Mizuno" / "Fujisawa") until January (the farewell at the tree, if used), apart from her one "Kōhei" in the kiss sequence.
  - If a female character ever uses *boku*, preserve it with a cue. None has been assigned, and none may ever be.
- **Hair color is a medium convention, never commented on in-world**, for any character, including Natsuki's copper and Akane's lavender-silver. (Oct 6.)
  - **Working default:** narration may describe a color plainly as visual description. No character ever remarks that a color is unusual, and no one's color is explained by the plot.
- **Romance and intimacy:** honest about attraction, but not an ecchi story. Nothing explicit involving the school-age cast; intimate beats stay non-explicit, and none belong in the early chapters.
  - **Agreed Oct 6:** the school-age cast's appeal comes through personality, styling and presence, never figure-focused design or framing.
  - **Agreed Oct 6:** violence can be drawn at manga intensity (blood, wounds, death). Gore for its own sake is out.
  - **Oct 10 (Claude's boundary):** no sexual activity is depicted or implied for the school-age cast, however obliquely, and no sexual humor centered on them. Kissing, hand-holding and emotional closeness are fine. The past-life couple were married adults, so their intimacy can be implied non-explicitly.
- **Keep the romcom fun.** Don't attach moral disclaimers to every trope. Photography, horror movies and friendship keep their ordinary pleasure rather than all becoming supernatural devices.
- **Expressive close-ups** inspired by *The Fragrant Flower Blooms with Dignity*, especially in nonverbal scenes.
- **POV: Open.** The pilot uses Kōhei in first-person present tense plus third-person interludes from the girls' side.
- **Kiss-scene song (Decided Oct 10):** an **original song** made for the scene, vocals allowed. The author's reference ("Carolina Lies," All the Damn Vampires; unverified) sets the feel only, and the licensing question is dropped. Vocal language and lyrics are Open.

## 3. Setting
### The town
- A fictional town in western Kanagawa, modeled on Hadano's foothill neighborhoods.
- **Landscape:** a river, wooded hills, and a Tanzawa-style mountain backdrop.
- **Layout:** quiet residential outskirts, plus a built-up center with the school, kendo hall, a family restaurant, shops and the station.
- **Access:** a larger city and the Shonan coast are reachable, so city outings and a beach day are possible.
- **Winter:** cold but mild, with little snow. Snowfall is a special event.
- **What's ruled out:** no Tokyo invasion, national catastrophe, military mobilization or AGI subplot.
- **Open:** the town name, the exact map, commutes and travel times.
- **Era (Decided Oct 13):** contemporary, with smartphones and messaging apps everywhere, but before generative AI. The exact year is Open.
- **Proposal:** Natsuki lives nearer the station.

### The two houses (updated Oct 6)
- Kōhei's and Nanoha's houses stand side by side near the wooded edge, **built almost touching, with about three feet between their walls.**
- Their upstairs bedroom windows face each other close enough to reach across and pass a book. **Reference: the houses in Muv-Luv Extra.**
- **No alley, path or stairway runs between them.** The way up to the shrine starts somewhere else along the street.
- Window conversations, lights on while studying, and silly distractions are optional recurring scenes.

### Shrine, tree and rock
- **The shrine:** a small but maintained **community shrine**. It has a modest worship building, a torii, an approach and an offering box; it is neither a tourist complex nor abandoned. It sits slightly uphill, an ordinary walk from the houses. Detailed architecture is Open.
- **Who runs it:**
  - No resident priest. A visiting priest who serves other shrines conducts the important rites, and local families maintain the grounds.
  - **Nanoha's grandfather is a principal caretaker.** He is not a priest and has no secret omniscience.
- **Use:** New Year hatsumode with neighbors is plausible. The shrine is quiet most days.
- **The cherry tree** is the sacred focus and **was already enormous in the past life**. A broad natural **boulder** appears in both eras as a familiar sitting place, not a worship object.
- **Working composition:** the immense tree, a low branch, the boulder, the river and rooftops below, the mountain behind and the shrine to one side.
- **Across eras:** the site was sacred in the past too, perhaps with a small older shrine or offering place. The current buildings are newer.
- **Purity practice:** respect Japanese ritual-purity norms. **No graves or cemetery at the shrine.**

### The earlier era
- Long ago, in the same place: a broadly historical, lightly fantasy Japanese community with **rōnin and landholding families**.
- No named period and no exhaustive reconstruction of norms. Invent customs consistently.
- Its absence from recorded history needs no explanation.

## 4. Structure and calendar
**Decided:** one continuous present-day story with past-life flashbacks. They start as brief fragments and grow into substantial scenes and a sustained account of the earlier climax. There are no mandatory standalone parts.

### Full-year calendar (Decided Oct 6)
**Rules:**
- The order of events is fixed. The dates are working anchors and can shift.
- **There are two hard ends:**
  - **Start:** Natsuki arrives at the start of April in second year.
  - **End:** the Beast is defeated around the close of second year.
- **The out-of-season bloom is tied to the start of the Beast phase, not to a date.** If that phase moves, the bloom moves with it. New Year is the working anchor.
- **Kiss to dating:** about ten weeks (changed Oct 18 from 4–8, so Kōhei's choice gets real time): the kiss in late August, the choice in early November.
- **First deaths to battle:** must stay short, about 4–8 weeks at most. Once the characters understand the danger, they can't spend months ignoring deaths.

| When | Beats |
|---|---|
| Apr–Aug | Natsuki arrives. Slow burn and fragments; Nanoha's jealousy; anger warning signs. Rainy-season umbrella scene and the girls' pact (June–July, working). Summer festival, backyard watermelon, horror movie. |
| **Late Aug** (end of summer break) | Karaoke, street fight, wound care, the kiss. |
| **Within about a week** | Natsuki tells Nanoha. Kōhei owns his half. The fallout runs into September: the mentor arc with Nanoha's father begins, the flashback to Dad's death, and Emi's confrontation that starts Mom's recovery. |
| **Oct** | **The in-between (Oct 18):** Kōhei's real choice, with real feelings for both girls. Chapters about the triangle, not only the family. |
| **Early Nov** | Kōhei and Nanoha start dating. The cultural festival hosts it, around Culture Day (Nov 3) (Oct 18; was October). |
| Nov–Dec | Visible couple time, including Christmas. Fragments intensify. Nanoha senses something about the past life (degree and timing Open). |
| **New Year** | The out-of-season bloom. The memories flood in. |
| **Early Jan** | Kōhei and Natsuki compare memories. He tells Nanoha about the past life and reaffirms his choice. The girls talk. |
| Mid-Jan → late Feb/early Mar | The deaths begin and escalate toward the battle. (Low-level activity — people lost, maybe injured, possibly one disappearance — starts earlier, in autumn background.) |
| Mar–Apr | Aftermath, graduation, the ordinary spring bloom. The main story ends. |
| Much later | Epilogue: Natsuki, old, at the tree. |

**Available set pieces:** school trip, summer festival, backyard watermelon (replaces the beach, Oct 12), cultural festival, Christmas, New Year, Valentine's, graduation. **The school trip (Decided Oct 18): Kyoto, late October**, in the in-between before the choice. Nanoha and Natsuki room together; the trip's center is their lights-out talk, two rivals who each know the other knows (and Natsuki holding her secret). Kōhei gets one quiet moment with each girl, with no shifts and no family to hide behind. Earlier note: Kanagawa schools have documented trips to Okinawa and Kansai.

### Length and pacing
- **Total length (Decided Oct 12):** a 40-chapter spine (`chapter-map.md`); **42 since Oct 18** (the school trip, and the January disclosure split in two). Chapter length floats, about 4,000–5,000 words where a chapter covers a week or more, for roughly 12–15 hours of reading. Slice-of-life chapters and extra development get added as needed. Minor characters stay light; that's fine.
- **Earlier discussion (Oct 6):**
  - **Option under discussion:** a fixed spine of about 40 chapters (~120k words, ~10 hours), plus an expandable layer of standalone daily-life chapters.
  - **Growth path:** the spine could grow toward Muv-Luv Extra scale (55–60+ chapters). The author likes that length but considers it ambitious for a first run.
- **Working, Oct 7:** about 40% of chapters go to the April–August romcom first half. Expandable daily-life chapters go there first.

- **Reader-ahead past-life interludes: Open (parked).** The proposal is to give the reader substantial past-life scenes about once per seasonal arc (5–6 in total) while the characters keep only fragments. That way the final act isn't the first time the past romance becomes substantial. The fatal walk is withheld until the climax either way.

## 5. The past life (rewritten Oct 7)
- **The couple:** Kōhei and Natsuki grew up in the same area and knew each other as children.
  - **He** became a **rōnin**, a highly accomplished swordsman without a master.
  - **She** was an ordinary woman in a traditional gender role, the daughter of a **modestly propertied family** that had no male heir.
- **The marriage:** it was **arranged so that her father would gain a male heir**, with Kōhei marrying into her family. They were nonetheless deeply in love.
  - **Open:** exact ages, how long they were married, and her family's livelihood.
- **Proposal:** a mukoyōshi-style adoption into her household. The rōnin gains a home and a name; her family gains an heir.
- **The romance** is real, warm and meaningful. It is shown through selected scenes (teasing, domestic familiarity, affection, his prowess), not a complete second romcom. Give the happiness enough room for the loss to matter.
- **They never sparred together.** **She watched him train** for years.
- **The death:**
  - **Setting:** a walk together; a misty morning is the author's working image.
  - **Season (Decided Oct 10): winter.** So the out-of-season bloom comes at about the same time of year in both eras: past = after his death in winter; present = New Year.
  - **Before (Decided Oct 10):** there have been a rumor or two, but the tree hasn't bloomed. He goes armed; she teases him a little about it and thinks it's nothing. Then they're trapped.
  - **The trap:** a beast that distorts familiar paths traps them, so she cannot go for help.
  - **The fight:** long. He realizes he is losing a battle of attrition, and that if it goes on they will both die.
  - **The trade:** **he deliberately opens himself to a fatal wound so he can get close enough to kill it**. The beast and Kōhei kill each other.
  - **Look (Working, Oct 13):** the past-life beast fight gets its own visibly different art style, an homage to *Vagabond*: heavy brush-ink, gritty and painterly, like the kiss sequence's *Fragrant Flower* homage. Homage to the style, never copies of actual pages.
  - **Her role:** she is **helpless throughout**. She survives and stays with him.
- **After (Decided Oct 10):** once he has killed the beast and they've made the promise, **the tree blooms out of season.** The distortion falls away.
  - **The CG:** her cradling his broken body, blood everywhere, cherry blossoms falling all around her.
  - **Decided Oct 10:** they were near the tree the whole time, walking in circles around it (the repeating-landmark motif), so she's kneeling beneath it when it blooms.
- **The promise:** they promise to find one another again.
  - **Proposal:** the promise has two halves. She promises "I'll find you"; his last word to her is "Live."
  - **Staging preference:** the promise comes before the deity's assurance, so they make it without certainty. **The out-of-season bloom is that assurance** (Oct 10).
  - The tree deity grants a reunion "in some form": reunion only, with no guarantee of recognition, simultaneous memory or a romantic claim.
  - The exact wording is unwritten. "Beyond the western sea" was a tonal reference only.
- **Optional coda:** her life afterward. Her widowhood leaves the household without an heir again, which is a poignant possibility. This is separate from the modern epilogue.
- **Costume:** she wears **a real kimono, simple and everyday, not elaborate**. Copper hair tied back with a plain cord. Her face is hidden in the manga. **Oct 18: her hair is hidden too until the bloom** (a cord, a hood, shadow, framing on her hands), so nothing settles who she is; at the bloom the copper is the visual confirmation.

### What the reunion is for (Decided Oct 8)
**The reunion's payoff is rescue and closure, not getting him back as a husband.** Natsuki's arc across both lives has three beats:
1. **She finds him:** the April transfer.
2. **She saves him:** in the climax she stops the same trade she once watched helplessly. **That is the reunion's payoff; the romance never was.**
3. **She lets him go and lives:** her full life and the old-age epilogue fulfill his dying wish. In the past life she also outlived him and grew old, so both lives end with her full and grateful at the tree.

**She chooses it.** Her wound is wanting to be someone's first choice, loved for who she is. Being loved because she was his wife in another life is exactly what she doesn't want. When the memories come, **she is the one who says, in effect, "I don't want you to love me because of *her*."** Losing the romance is her decision, made from strength, not a punishment.

**The past love stays complete and honored, not interrupted.** Kōhei never dismisses it.

**The real risk is Nanoha.** Kōhei and Nanoha must be **the love story of this life on their own merits** (the windows, the familiarity, her private oddness, the fear of changing something precious). Otherwise readers will feel the wrong girl won, however kindly Natsuki is treated. The first-half chapters carry this.
- **Restated (Decided Oct 18):** "my girl lost" is fine; it's a real triangle and half the readers may be disappointed. "The wrong girl won" (an unearned win) is not.

**What follows from this:**
- **Her kendo means something.** Natsuki was helpless while he died for her. Now she is a swordswoman, and in the climax she isn't helpless.
- **Recognition in sparring is one-sided.** She anticipates him because she has watched him a thousand times. He doesn't anticipate her. This also explains how she lands strikes while he generally keeps the edge.
- **Armor:** helping him into armor was a wife's job, which she knows how to do.
- **The wrist motif (Decided Oct 8):** she binds a cut on his wrist when he comes home late and hurt from training, scolding him ("You're late"). Patching him up afterward was all she could do in that life, and the climax reverses it. It's echoed when Natsuki treats his wounds in August.

## 6. Present-day plot
### School life and the triangle
- **Kōhei and Nanoha** already love each other in some form; she understands her romantic desire more clearly.
  - She is an assumed part of his home. He notices her beauty but barely considers how to turn their closeness into dating, out of familiarity and fear of changing things rather than total obliviousness.
- **Natsuki** joins through kendo and is welcomed into the five-person friend group.
  - She and Kōhei are genuinely attracted to each other in the present, with a faint recognition beyond their actual familiarity.
  - She is not a full rival at first, though Nanoha feels threatened.
- **The girls' pact (Decided Oct 7):**
  - The two girls come to recognize that they're both into him, and talk about it openly. They agree, at least tacitly, to compete fairly.
  - **This is part of why the kiss is a betrayal:** it breaks their agreement.
  - **Working placement (Oct 10; sharpened Oct 18):** they're smart girls, and each already knows the other is a rival before June. The umbrella scene is where it becomes **mutual knowledge**: each knows the other knows. (Neither learns that the other also cheated; that joke is the reader's.) The pact itself happens when **Nanoha brings food to a sick Natsuki** (scene bank 13a).
- **Proposal:** the romcom rivalry runs through summer. The festival, the backyard watermelon and outings get a competitive edge, and the girls' friendship stays sincere.
- **A real triangle (Decided Oct 18; replaces "the triangle isn't symmetrical"):** Kōhei has real feelings for Natsuki, real but smaller than for Nanoha, and he comes to realize it. Choosing Nanoha is a real choice (the model is Muv-Luv *Extra*'s Sumika route), so the festival chapter (Ch25 since Oct 18) is a decision, not only a realization.
  - **Author's concern (Oct 18):** the triangle is hot from Kōhei's side for a fairly short time; as canon stands he's barely aware until the kiss (Ch19–24 in the Oct 12 map, about six weeks) though the girls compete from June. **Decided Oct 18:** he feels the pull from summer (the festival, sparring, moments he notices and doesn't want to look at) and avoids it rather than missing it. **It's obvious to the reader early that both girls are the love interests and that he likely has feelings for both;** he himself doesn't admit it until late September, when Natsuki nearly accepts someone else's confession and it hurts more than he expected (scene bank 16, now Ch22). Then he has about five weeks with feelings for both before he chooses.
  - **Natsuki's in-world advocate (Oct 18):** Ryōsuke backs her in the lunch scoring; Chie gets more room.

### Anger: warning signs
- **Warning signs** stay sparse: a too-intense practice, and a quiet, threatening confrontation.
- **The kendo meet** where an opponent mocks Akane, who is faint or unwell, and Kōhei fights noticeably punitively.
  - It's a possible competition stage.
  - No deliberate equipment sabotage.
  - Injury and technical details need research if used.
- **Never frightening anger toward Akane.** Ordinary sibling arguments are fine.

### Street fight → wound care → kiss (locked sequence, dates updated)
**Late August.**
1. The group is out at karaoke, possibly in a less safe part of town; Natsuki has left early. A lone, genuinely unpleasant antagonist is the leading staging. The venue and the initial threat are Open.
2. Kōhei takes damage and wins the fight. The other man stays conscious, can leave, and has no permanent injury. He is no justification for continuing.
3. Kōhei holds the beaten man by his clothes, preparing another blow. Nanoha intervenes before it lands.
4. In his fixation he jerks as she grabs him and **accidentally knocks her down**. It is a minor fall, uninjured, not a shove. He stops at once and releases the man, who runs.
5. **He offers his hand, face and shirt and hands bloodied. She backs away and doesn't take it.** Full page, from Nanoha's POV: she sees someone frightening.
6. The Rival helps her and takes her home. Kōhei lets them go. The Rival witnessing it leads to trouble in the kendo club; there is **no legal subplot**.
7. Nanoha is frightened and repelled, and also feels a small, troubling gratification that she matters this much to him. She is not responsible for his violence.
8. Hurt, ashamed and shaken, Kōhei can't face home or the Tachibanas, so he goes to Natsuki's.
9. **Natsuki treats his wounds.** Gentle, necessary contact leads to her initiating a mutual comfort kiss. They begin making out (shown as kissing, non-explicit). **She stops it** even though she wants to continue: she knows Nanoha's feelings, she doesn't want to be an emotional refuge mistaken for a choice, and it breaks the girls' pact.
10. **She asks Kōhei not to tell Nanoha, because she wants to be the one to tell her.** She is painfully aware Nanoha loves him. Kōhei is only partly aware of that ("kind of unaware," not oblivious).

**Rules for this sequence:**
- Not a cool demonstration of kendo mastery.
- It has consequences without consuming the romcom's entire tone.
- No supernatural compulsion; his distress doesn't erase his attraction or his agency.
- It isn't infidelity, since Kōhei and Nanoha aren't together. The breach is the pact and the trust.
- **Kiss chapter staging (Working):** almost wordless even where the characters speak, with brief visual echoes of an analogous past-life moment. Only Natsuki's request is voiced aloud.
- **The wound-care and kiss sequence (Decided Oct 13):** told in the style of *The Fragrant Flower Blooms with Dignity*: close-ups of faces and hands, with many half- and full-page images. Beat by beat:
  1. Natsuki gets a text from Kōhei, something like "Are you home?" or "I'm at your door," and is shocked. (Decided Oct 13.) **Proposal:** she checks the apartment intercom's video screen and sees his bloodied face on it.
  2. She opens the door: his face, his bloody hands, blood dripping on the floor.
  3. At the sink she washes his hands; her hands caressing his; bandaging them.
  4. She tends his face while he sits on a stool, so they're at eye level or she's slightly higher.
  5. He starts sobbing, really for his dad, and buries his face against her.
  6. Close-up of her face, full of conflicting feelings: sad for him, happy to be holding him, déjà vu, satisfaction, worry about Nanoha.
  7. She cleans his face and dries his tears, then lingers longer than she needs to.
  8. Half or full page: she kisses him.
  9. Full page: he stands up and kisses her back, wrapping his arms around her.

### The threat and the climax
- **Before the bloom (Decided Oct 10):** the beast is already active at a low level. There are rumors and reports of people getting lost, maybe injured, and possibly one disappearance. The bloom signals that it's ramping up, not that it has just begun.
  - **Decided Oct 10:** autumn background noise that reads as ordinary bad news: a hiker lost on a familiar trail who turns up dazed, an injury nobody can explain, and one disappearance with posters at the station. The missing person is found dead in January, the first death of the escalation.
- **The deaths:** unexplained deaths occur in town. The cause is a supernatural beast that **distorts familiar paths, trapping and separating people**. When it dies, the distortion ends.
- **The beast:**
  - It need not be the same beast as before.
  - **Size (Decided Oct 10):** a really big dog, not a giant: its back is at a man's belt line (about 3′4″ at the shoulder), and its head reaches his chest (about 4′3″ to the ear tips). **Raised Oct 10** from about 3′ at the shoulder. The fight is sword's reach, not stabbing at paws.
  - **Look (candidate, Oct 10):** an ink-black wolf with brushstroke-edged fur and a pale, smooth carved-mask face with narrow glowing eyes; its legs and tail fray into mist (style guide §1a).
  - **Nature (Decided Oct 10):** a supernatural **wolf-like** creature. It has a physical form and can be wounded, but it is mainly spiritual, and it **leaves no corpse** when killed.
  - **Folklore source (Decided Oct 10): the okuri-ōkami / okuri-inu,** the "escorting wolf" of mountain-path legend, known from Kantō to Kinki.
    - **The legend:** it follows travelers at night. If they stumble and fall, it attacks. If they keep their footing, or pretend they're only resting, they're safe, and some versions say it even escorts them home. Saying goodbye or thanks at the end of the road, or leaving it a gift such as a single sandal, ends the escort. In some regions people distinguish the protective okuri-inu from the attacking mukae-inu.
    - **Why it fits:** walkers on familiar paths, circling (the beast as the thing walking behind you while the path won't end), and a protector turned predator. That mirrors Kōhei, the protector who hits a man who has already fallen.
    - **Folk rules as plot tools:** don't fall, "I'm only resting," thanks at the end of the road. Nanoha's grandfather or elderly locals know the customs (canon's elderly help); the methodical Takatsuki notices the pattern.
    - **No corpse:** it comes apart into mist. The mist on the past-life walk was its presence all along.
    - **Caution:** "okuri-ōkami" is also modern slang for a man who walks a woman home with bad intentions. A Japanese reader may hear it when Takatsuki walks Nanoha home after the fight. Avoid wording that leans on that.
  - **Open:** its exact appearance and rules, its vulnerabilities, and how it relates to the earlier beast.
  - Any larger evil can stay undefined.
- **Authorities** investigate competently but lack the friends' access. Most adults don't believe or understand. Elderly locals might help the friends understand what to do; the source of that knowledge is Open.
- **The tree** shelters the local community, not a country. It helps but can't simply end the threat.
- **Kōhei's recovered prowess matters** without making the others redundant, and memories don't erase a modern teen's physical limits.
- **Cooperation is the decisive difference** from his solitary past victory.
- **Plan and breakdown:**
  - The friends make a genuine, non-suicidal cooperative plan.
  - It falls apart under pressure, because they are inexperienced teenagers.
  - Kōhei truly trusts the others; the failure is not a relapse into fighting alone.
- **The lunge (Decided Oct 7):**
  - As the plan breaks down, **his old instinct pulls him toward the same deliberate opening that killed him**. He does not choose it.
  - **Natsuki recognizes the moment, because she watched it happen once**, and strikes or unbalances the beast by inches, turning a fatal neck strike into a serious shoulder wound. **She saves him.**
  - Never a planned suicidal maneuver, and it never implies his trust was false.
- **The indispensable task:** someone Kōhei must trust has to make an indispensable contribution. Takatsuki (the Rival) currently fills it, but his functions can move if he doesn't work.
  - **Proposal:** the plan needs both styles, Takatsuki's precision and Kōhei's instinct.
- **Nanoha's role (Working):**
  - She protects people near the tree and senses the beast or its location.
  - She can exhaust herself holding it within one path so it can't escape, at the risk of losing herself.
  - While the others confront it there, they're beyond outside help.
  - Details are unfinalized; guiding or anchoring safe paths through the tree is a proposal.
- **Don't let the mentor's job solve the threat.** Neither the father's profession nor the authorities settle the beast.
- **Heirlooms:** Nanoha's household may hold Kōhei's old weapons, and possibly armor (provisional), as poorly understood heirlooms. Their condition and practicality are Open. Establish the objects before their significance is recognized.
  - **Proposals:** past Natsuki put them in safekeeping; a real old blade lands the past-life feeling the bamboo shinai can't.
- **Proposal: Kōhei's own practice.** A solo routine, such as dawn suburi under the tree.

## 9. Supernatural principles
- **The tree:** the cherry tree is a local good deity, or its embodied presence. It protects homes and community and counterbalances nearby evil. Its exact nature is Open.
- **Its limits:** no continental root network and no countrywide system.
- **The bloom:** the out-of-season bloom signals the awakening and that the tree's help is needed.
  - **It means something different in each era (Oct 10):**
    - **Past:** the bloom comes *after* his death and the promise. It is the tree's answer to the promise.
    - **Present:** the bloom comes as the beast's activity ramps up. It is a warning, and a sign that the reunion has a purpose.
  - **Decided Oct 10:** the modern bloom triggers the memory flood. The last time either of them saw the tree bloom out of season, he was dying beneath it.
- **Nanoha and the fairy:** the fairy grows more prominent and Nanoha's connection develops.
  - Power doesn't track moral purity.
  - Negative emotions don't automatically collapse protection.
- **Memories:** Kōhei and Natsuki recover fragments, then fuller memories. There's no deliberate replay mission and no one-use ritual.
- **Romance stays free:** the reunion grant doesn't compel romantic choices.
- **The modern beast:** dangerous enough to kill townspeople. It isn't automatically the resurrected old beast.
- **Why the friends:** they need a specific supernatural advantage, without making the authorities incompetent. That reason is still to be established.
- **Afterward:** memories persist after the powers fade. The afterlife and the ultimate cosmology stay unknown.
